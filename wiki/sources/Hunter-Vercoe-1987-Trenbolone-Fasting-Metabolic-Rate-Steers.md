# Trenbolone Acetate LOWERS Fasting Metabolic Rate by ~10% in Steers (Hunter & Vercoe 1987)

The only quantitative metabolic-rate data on trenbolone in any species, and it points the opposite way to the folk claim that trenbolone is a thermogenic drug.

Six steers implanted with 300 mg trenbolone acetate versus six controls, two experiments on low-protein roughage diets, metabolic rate measured after a 72-hour fast.

## Numbers

| Experiment | Control FMR | Trenbolone FMR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (roughage alone, 8 wk) | 83.3 kJ/kg/day | **74.5 kJ/kg/day** | **−10.6%** (P < 0.01, intake-adjusted) |
| 2 (+urea/sulphur, 6 wk) | 76.9 kJ/kg/day | **70.7 kJ/kg/day** | **−8.1%** (P < 0.05) |

Also: feed intake fell in experiment 1 (12.8 → 10.9 g DM/kg LW, P < 0.01), plasma insulin fell (24 → 19 µU/mL), and liveweight gain in experiment 2 nearly doubled (0.23 → 0.41 kg/d). Free T3, NEFA and urea-N were unchanged.

## What the design was actually for

This is agricultural science asking how to make cattle grow on poor feed. Trenbolone was being used deliberately as a **maintenance-energy-requirement reducer** — the whole point was to make the animal more metabolically efficient, gaining more weight on less feed. That is the mechanistic inverse of raising TDEE.

> [!contradiction] The niche consensus that trenbolone raises body temperature, causes night sweats and "burns" calories is tier-X creator assertion with no supporting calorimetry in any species. The one species where fasting metabolic rate has been measured shows a ~10% **decrease**. Subjective heat and sweating are plausibly sympathetic/neuroendocrine effects (trenbolone is a potent progesterone-receptor and glucocorticoid-receptor ligand) that need not track whole-body energy expenditure.

> [!gap] Castrated male ruminants on protein-deficient roughage, 300 mg implant, fasted measurement. Ruminant energy metabolism, nutritional state, and androgen-receptor context all differ from a fed human on a high-protein diet. This is tier C and must not be quoted as a human number — but it is the only measurement that exists.

## Relevance to This Domain

Answers the trenbolone part of the question with the only hard data available, and the answer is counterintuitive: **measured metabolic rate went down, not up**. Pair with the absence of any human trenbolone pharmacology at all.
