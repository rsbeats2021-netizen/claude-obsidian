# Growth Hormone (GH)–IGF-1 Axis

The GH-IGF-1 axis is the principal systemic driver of longitudinal bone growth: pituitary growth hormone stimulates IGF-1 synthesis both in the liver (circulating IGF-1) and locally within the growth plate itself, with local IGF-1 generally considered more important than circulating levels for chondrocyte proliferation. This axis interacts with, rather than acts independently of, the sex-steroid signaling covered by most other vocabulary terms in this domain.

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]]** — growth hormone stimulates IGF-1 synthesis in liver and locally in the growth plate; local growth-plate IGF-1 is considered more important than circulating IGF-1 for chondrocyte proliferation and hypertrophy. Low-dose estrogen is proposed to drive the pubertal growth spurt partly *via* activating this axis, before high-dose estrogen later triggers fusion.
- **[[Role-of-Growth-Plate-Longitudinal-Bone-Growth|Pines & Hurwitz]]** — general review treatment of the GH-IGF-I axis as the systemic framework for growth-plate proliferation, alongside local cAMP/cGMP second-messenger signaling and vitamin D metabolites.
- **[[Growth-Hormone-Androgen-Synergy-Hypopituitary-Dwarfs|1974 clinical trial]]** — direct human clinical demonstration that GH and androgen (fluoxymesterone) interact synergistically rather than additively: GH+androgen combined produced greater growth velocity in hypopituitary dwarf patients than either alone, more than the sum of the two individual effects.
- **[[Aromatase-Inhibitors-Near-Final-Height-Males|Bouliari et al. 2026]]** — concurrent GH therapy was independently associated with greater height gain (+5.9 cm on average) in boys also receiving aromatase inhibitors, showing the GH axis and the estrogen/aromatase axis contribute additively (at least) to height outcomes in a clinical population.

- **[[GH-Dose-IGF1-Pubertal-Height-Gain|Lundberg et al. 2015]]** — a rigorous randomized human clinical trial: a higher pubertal GH dose (mimicking the natural pubertal GH/IGF-1 surge) produced significantly greater pubertal IGF-I rise and correspondingly greater pubertal/total height gain in GH-deficient children, with dose-dependent ΔIGF-I explaining up to 28% of height-gain variance in multivariate models — the domain's most quantitatively rigorous demonstration that the *magnitude* of the pubertal IGF-I surge, not just its presence, drives growth outcomes.

- **[[Thyroid-Hormone-Receptor-Skeletal-Phenotypes|O'Shea et al. 2005]]** — shows thyroid hormone acts on the growth plate significantly *through* this axis: mice with a thyroid-hormone-receptor mutation causing local skeletal hypothyroidism (TRα1PV) had markedly reduced growth-plate GH receptor and IGF-I receptor expression/signaling, while mice with the same mutation causing skeletal thyrotoxicosis (TRβPV, via a different tissue-dominant receptor) had increased GH/IGF-I receptor expression/signaling — establishing GH receptor and IGF-I receptor as physiological targets of local thyroid hormone action in bone, not just downstream of pituitary GH secretion.

- **[[GH-Stimulates-Human-Osteoblast-Proliferation-Kassem1993|Kassem et al. 1993]]** and **[[hGH-Colony-Formation-Rat-Epiphyseal-Chondrocytes-Lindahl1986|Lindahl et al. 1986]]** — direct cellular evidence (human osteoblasts; rat epiphyseal chondrocytes) that GH acts directly on bone/cartilage cells to stimulate proliferation and differentiation, not solely via systemic liver-derived IGF-I. **[[hGH-Chondrogenesis-Osteogenesis-Chondroprogenitor-Maor1989|Maor et al. 1989]]** extends this to the chondroprogenitor stage, showing GH promotes both chondrogenic and osteogenic differentiation before lineage commitment.
- **[[GH-Direct-Indirect-Action-Longitudinal-Growth-Loveridge1995|Loveridge et al. 1995]]** — used an anti-GH antiserum to neutralize endogenous GH in vivo, directly testing the direct-vs-indirect action question via GH removal rather than addition.
- **[[Cartilage-Targeted-IGF1-Bone-Growth-Lui-Baron|Lui, Baron et al.]]** — a novel cartilage-targeted IGF-1 fusion protein designed to concentrate IGF-1 action locally at the growth plate, addressing GH therapy's limited efficacy in non-GH-deficient short stature (e.g., chondrodysplasias).
- **[[HighDose-rhGH-Puberty-NearFinalHeight-Mauras2000|Mauras et al. 2000]]** — a randomized multicenter trial found high-dose rhGH timed to puberty (matching the natural pubertal GH-amplitude surge) increased near-final height in GH-deficient patients versus standard dosing.
- **[[Growth-Plate-Physiology-Review-Lui2014|Lui et al. 2014]]** — a modern review synthesizing growth-plate physiology through gene-expression-network and GWAS approaches, situating this domain's individual mechanistic findings within a systems-level framework.

- **[[DHEA-and-the-Skeleton-Review|Gordon et al. 1999]]** — reports a significant human correlation between circulating DHEAS and IGF-I levels (r=0.43), and that DHEA supplementation raises serum IGF-I in postmenopausal women, elderly men, and anorexia nervosa patients — positioning DHEA as an upstream modulator of the skeletal IGF-I system distinct from GH itself.

- **[[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]]** — the domain's first direct evidence of GH action in human (not just rodent) growth-plate tissue: using a novel human explant culture system, GH directly stimulated stem-cell proliferation via JAK/STAT, autocrine TGFβ, and ERK activation while inhibiting AKT — confirming GH's local, direct-to-growth-plate mechanism is not purely mediated by liver-derived IGF-I, and mapping this to the specific resting-zone stem-cell population identified via single-cell RNA sequencing.

- **[[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]** — the domain's most direct head-to-head clinical comparison of this axis against androgen therapy: in boys with puberty delay, rhGH produced no growth or pubertal-progression advantage over oral testosterone undecanoate or their combination (a near-exact tie), while in peri-pubertal boys with familial short stature, rhGH+oxandrolone (androgen) outperformed either agent alone — suggesting the GH axis and direct androgen signaling are substitutable in some contexts and synergistic in others, depending on the population's baseline pubertal/androgen status.

- **[[GH-IGF1-Distinct-Zones-Rat-Growth-Plate-Oberbauer|Oberbauer & Peng 1995]]** — an early (1995) demonstration that GH/IGF-I act zone-specifically within the growth plate, preferentially stimulating proliferative-zone chondrocytes with minimal effect on resting-zone cells, and that combining GH+IGF-I produced no additive proliferative benefit — foundational groundwork later confirmed at single-cell resolution in human tissue by [[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]].
- **[[GH-Therapy-Circulating-Stem-Cells-VSELs-Wedrychowicz|Wędrychowicz et al. 2025]]** — an 8-year human cohort study showing long-term GH therapy increases circulating (not growth-plate-resident) very small embryonic-like stem cells (VSELs) alongside other circulating progenitor populations, with no adverse organ effects — a body-wide stem-cell angle on the GH axis distinct from the epSSC/resting-zone-chondrocyte stem-cell thread covered in [[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]].
- **[[SOCS2-Accelerated-Growth-Plate-Fusion-Osteoarthritis|Samvelyan et al. 2022]]** — introduces SOCS2 as a negative regulator of GH-receptor signaling; Socs2-knockout mice show accelerated growth and accelerated growth-plate fusion, but — notably — no increased osteoarthritis vulnerability despite the accelerated fusion, a useful null result decoupling "faster fusion" from "worse joint outcomes."
- **[[GHF1-Somatotropic-Progenitor-Immortalization-Dwarfism|Lew et al. 1993]]** — traces GH deficiency to a point further upstream than anything else in this domain: arrested pituitary somatotrope progenitor-cell development (via GHF-1-targeted genetic manipulation) causes dwarfism before GH is ever produced, distinct from every growth-plate-level or receptor-level mechanism covered elsewhere.

- **[[IGF1-GH-Differential-Effects-Growth-Plate-Hunziker|Hunziker et al. 1994]]** — a foundational hypophysectomized-rat infusion study showing GH and IGF-I act at different chondrocyte maturation stages: GH recruits resting-zone cells into proliferation, IGF-I expands cells already proliferating — "GH initiates, IGF-I amplifies," some of the earliest direct evidence for local (not just liver-mediated) GH action.
- **[[GH-Single-Cell-Enzyme-Activity-Proliferation-Gevers1996|Gevers et al. 1996]]** and **[[GH-Rapid-STAT5-Phosphorylation-Resting-Chondrocytes-Gevers2009|Gevers et al. 2009]]** — a decade-plus of single-cell-resolution GH/growth-plate work from the same lab, culminating in direct demonstration of GH-induced STAT5 phosphorylation specifically in resting-zone chondrocytes, modulated by feeding status.
- **[[GH-IGF1-Exercise-NonWeightBearing-Muscle-Hypophysectomized|Grossman et al.]]** — extends this axis to skeletal muscle (non-weight-bearing fast-twitch fibers) in hypophysectomized rats, a NASA-funded disuse-atrophy research context distinct from this domain's usual growth-plate focus.

- **[[GH-Replacement-Hematopoiesis-GHD-Children-Kawa2014|Kawa et al. 2014]]** — GH replacement directly stimulates hematopoiesis (erythroid progenitor clonogenicity, RBC counts) in GH-deficient children, a third distinct GH/IGF-1-responsive stem/progenitor population alongside growth-plate epSSCs and circulating VSELs.
- **[[GH-Receptor-Osteogenesis-BMPC-Cool2005|Cool et al. 2005]]** — GH receptor signaling drives osteoblast-lineage commitment from bone marrow progenitor cells, complementing Kawa et al. 2014's hematopoietic-lineage finding from the same broad marrow-progenitor pool.
- **[[GH-Mesenchymal-Stem-Cell-Commitment-Review-Bolamperti2019|Bolamperti et al. 2019]]** — reviews GH's role in directing MSC fate (osteoblast/chondrocyte/adipocyte), connecting to [[Equine-MSC-Gene-Expression-Adipocyte-Chondrocyte-Differentiation|Ropka-Molik et al. 2026]]'s transcriptomic data on the same fate decision.
- **[[GH-Breast-Epithelium-Progesterone-Stem-Cells-Lombardi2014|Lombardi et al. 2014]]** — normal breast epithelium secretes its own GH locally in response to progesterone, stimulating tissue-resident stem/progenitor proliferation — a rare example of extrapituitary, tissue-autonomous GH production and action.
- **[[Somatotropic-Axis-GH-IGF-Related-Independent-Roles-Butler2001|Butler & Le Roith 2001]]** — the theoretical foundation this domain's whole "local, not just systemic, GH/IGF-I action" thread rests on: a major review directly overturning the traditional somatomedin hypothesis, establishing GH and IGF-I have substantial actions independent of each other rather than GH acting purely through liver-derived IGF-I.
- **[[Autocrine-Paracrine-IGF1-Skeletal-Development-Wang2013|Wang et al. 2013]]** — synthesizes tissue-specific IGF-I knockout data confirming local (autocrine/paracrine) IGF-I action across skeletal cell types.
- **[[IGF2-MSC-Glutathione-Osteoarthritis-Senescence-Cho2025|Cho et al. 2025]]** — MSCs with high glutathione secrete more IGF-2, which paracrinely rejuvenates senescent osteoarthritic chondrocytes — a third independent mechanism (alongside ERα and SIRT1) for reversing chondrocyte senescence in this domain.
- **[[Pulsatile-GH-IGF1-mRNA-Isgaard1988|Isgaard et al. 1988]]**, **[[GH-Pulsatility-Hepatic-Receptors-IGFBP-Bick1992|Bick et al. 1992]]**, and **[[GH-Secretory-Pattern-Peak-Trough-Gevers1996|Gevers et al. 1996]]** — a cluster establishing that GH's temporal secretion PATTERN (pulsatile vs. continuous, and specifically peak vs. trough) independently determines its skeletal/hepatic effects, not just total dose.
- **[[GH-Pulses-Chromatin-Liver-Sex-Dimorphism-Rampersaud2023|Rampersaud et al. 2023]]** — a modern chromatin-level mechanism for this pulsatility cluster: the sex difference in GH secretion pattern (pulsatile in males, continuous in females) drives male-biased chromatin opening in liver, mechanistically bridging this domain's classic GH-pulsatility physiology and its sexual-dimorphism thread.
- **[[GH-Multiple-Effects-Prohormone-Review-Devesa2016|Devesa et al. 2016]]** — proposes GH may function as a prohormone rather than a hormone, proteolytically cleaved into tissue-specific shorter forms with uncharacterized activity — a conceptual reframing that could help explain this axis's strikingly varied, tissue-specific effects across this domain's sources.

> [!gap] No source in this domain traces the GH-IGF-1 axis down to the same Sox9/Runx2/Dlx5 transcription-factor level covered in [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]] — how systemic GH signaling actually engages that intrinsic machinery is not addressed by any single source currently ingested.
