# Video script — "What steroids actually do to your jaw"

60s · 175 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**
⚠️ Framing must stay negative-to-neutral. This is not an enhancement narrative.

### [0:00–0:07] HOOK
SPOKEN: Anabolic steroids do change your jaw. Just not the way the internet
        thinks they do. The animal data shows alteration — not enhancement.
  [VISUAL: mandible outline, arrows in different directions]

### [0:07–0:16] PROBLEM
SPOKEN: Jaw development is a huge topic online, and most of what circulates
        about hormones and facial structure is inference dressed up as
        mechanism. There is actual data. It's in rats.
  [ON-SCREEN: "THERE IS DATA. IT'S IN RATS."]

### [0:16–0:36] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: Nandrolone altered mandibular growth in juvenile and adult female rats,
        measured by radiographic cephalometry — real morphometry, not visual
        impression. The word the literature uses is altered. Not increased. Not
        improved. And the response varied by site and by whether the animal was
        still growing.
  [ON-SCREEN: "ALTERED ≠ IMPROVED"]

### [0:36–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: That site-dependence is the actual finding. Craniofacial growth isn't
        one process — the mandible, the condyle and the cranial base have
        different mechanisms and different hormone sensitivities. Facial
        harmony depends on proportion between sites. Something that changes
        several sites by different amounts is more likely to distort than to
        improve.
  [ON-SCREEN: "UNEVEN CHANGE = DISTORTION"]

### [0:50–0:60] CAVEAT + CTA
SPOKEN: And the limits are serious. Rat cephalometry, supraphysiologic
        exposure, rodent skulls that grow differently than ours. Real
        measurement in the wrong species is still the wrong species. Don't
        forget, it's never over.
