3 PAPERS · 1 CONCLUSION

03PAPER 1

FIRST, THE CLASSIC. 2001.

Genes & Development

Vol. 15 · Research Article · 2001

Hypoxia in cartilage: HIF-1α is essential for chondrocyte growth arrest and survival

Schipani E, Ryan HE, Didrickson S, Kobayashi T, Knight M, Johnson RS

The growth plate is an avascular tissue. Cells in its centre therefore sit furthest from any oxygen supply. Deleting HIF-1α from chondrocytes caused massive cell death in the interior of the growth plate, establishing that the hypoxia response is not incidental to cartilage but required for the tissue to survive its own architecture.

Put simply: the cells in the middle of your plate sit further from a blood vessel than almost anything else in your body. They need their own way to stay alive down there.

SCHIPANI 2001

04WHAT HAPPENED

Remove the switch, kill the centre

Notice the dying is central, not spread out. That's the signature of an oxygen problem, and it's the clue that sent people looking for how the tissue normally copes.

AVASCULAR BY DESIGN

05PAPER 2

NOW THE 2023 ONE. THIS IS IN NATURE.

Nature

Article · 2023 · s41586-023-06611-6

An extra-erythrocyte role of haemoglobin body in chondrocyte hypoxia adaption

Zhang F, et al.

Chondrocytes were found to express haemoglobin at high levels and to condense it into membraneless cytoplasmic bodies, termed Hedy, by phase separation. Hedy functions as a localised intracellular oxygen reservoir, enabling chondrocyte adaptation to hypoxia and the survival of developing cartilage.

Cartilage cells make their own haemoglobin. Not borrowed from your blood. Made right there, and kept as a droplet inside the cell.

ZHANG 2023 · NATURE

06MECHANISM

It builds its own oxygen tank

It's phase separation. The haemoglobin clumps itself into a droplet with no membrane around it, held together by a floppy tail on the beta subunit.

LIQUID-LIQUID PHASE SEPARATION

07THE NUMBER

It holds oxygen tighter than your blood

P50 of 27.85 mmHg against 58.2 for red cells. The curve sits to the left, which means it grabs oxygen and hangs onto it. That's a storage tank, not a delivery van.

08PROOF

SO WHAT IF YOU TAKE IT AWAY?

Zhang et al. 2023 · results

Chondrocyte-specific deletion of haemoglobin abolished Hedy. Massive cell death occurred in the inner zones of the developing growth plate, accompanied by a delay in cartilage hypertrophy. Homozygous animals became hydropic and died between embryonic days 16.5 and 18.5.

This isn't a subtle effect. Take the oxygen store away and the tissue that makes you taller dies from the inside out.

EMBRYONIC LETHAL

09WHERE I GET STUCK

The jump I can't make

This is the point where I expected to find a protocol, and I couldn't get there. Here's what kept stopping me.

  • The plate is low-oxygen because nothing plumbs into it. That's about distance from a vessel, not about how much oxygen is in your blood.
  • Hedy looks like a buffer, and a buffer's whole job is keeping things steady. Hard to see it responding to how you breathe.
  • All of this is embryo knockout work. Nobody has tested any of it in a teenager who is actually growing.

So I'd love to be shown otherwise. If someone has a study that closes that gap, put it in the comments and I'll read it.

STILL LOOKING

10IF YOU RUN ANYTHING

Nothing you take gets there easily

Whatever is in your blood has to cross the vessels at the edge and then diffuse the rest of the way in. The middle of the plate is the last place anything arrives and the first place it runs out.

DIFFUSION, NOT PERFUSION

11THE USEFUL PART

What I actually take from this

If you track bloods or think about compounds at all, these three follow directly from the papers.

  • Your serum number is not the plate's number. An IGF-1 reading tells you what your liver did, not what arrived at the cartilage.
  • A delivery bottleneck doesn't care how many inputs you add. More items in a stack raises the side-effect load, not the concentration at the target.
  • The oxygen system in there is locally set. KLF1 responds to the cell's own environment, and there's no version of that you take.

Which is the boring answer, but it's the one the anatomy gives you.

THE LIMIT IS THE TISSUE

12WHAT IT'S ACTUALLY FOR

Why I still think it's worth knowing

Everything anyone tries to do about height has to work through this tissue. Once you know it's running on its own little oxygen store with no blood supply, a lot of the category makes more sense.

  • It's why growth is slow. You can't rush something that runs on diffusion.
  • It's why most things you swallow reach it badly. No vessels, no direct route in.
  • It's why timing matters more than effort. Check my coming posts, that one needs its own slideshow.
