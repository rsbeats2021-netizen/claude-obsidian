# GH and IGF-I Act on Different Growth-Plate Chondrocyte Stages (Hunziker et al. 1994)

A foundational (1994) in vivo dissection of the GH-IGF-1 axis at the growth plate, using hypophysectomized rats (surgically removing the pituitary to eliminate endogenous GH/IGF-I confounds) and infusing each hormone separately. The result: GH and IGF-I act at different points along the chondrocyte maturation sequence — GH primarily recruits resting-zone cells into active proliferation, while IGF-I primarily drives the expansion of cells already committed to the proliferative and early hypertrophic stages. This "GH initiates, IGF-I amplifies" division of labor was some of the earliest direct evidence for local, stage-specific GH/IGF-I action at the growth plate itself, rather than GH acting purely through systemic liver-derived IGF-I.

> [!gap] A 1994 in vivo infusion study; the specific molecular mechanism behind each hormone's stage-selectivity (e.g., receptor expression differences by zone) wasn't resolved until much later work, some of it already in this domain (e.g., [[GH-IGF1-Distinct-Zones-Rat-Growth-Plate-Oberbauer|Oberbauer & Peng 1995]], [[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]]).

## Relevance to This Domain

A foundational data point for [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]], directly complementing [[GH-IGF1-Distinct-Zones-Rat-Growth-Plate-Oberbauer|Oberbauer & Peng 1995]]'s zone-specific findings with an even earlier in vivo whole-animal demonstration that GH and IGF-I have genuinely distinct, non-redundant roles at different growth-plate maturation stages.
