# Deca-Durabolin (Nandrolone) Reproductive Organ Damage in Male Rats (Zabiba 2017)

A histopathology study documenting severe reproductive-organ damage from prolonged, high-dose Deca-Durabolin (nandrolone decanoate) administration in male rats (15 mg/kg twice weekly for 10 weeks — a dose regimen modeling athletic/bodybuilding abuse patterns rather than therapeutic replacement doses). Extensive H&E histopathology revealed basement-membrane destruction, tubular necrosis, severe spermatid loss, cystic tubular distension, epithelial hyperplasia with papillary growth, and interstitial fibrosis throughout the epididymis and testis.

The proposed mechanism follows the classical exogenous-androgen suppression pathway: nandrolone's negative feedback on the hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular axis reduces endogenous GnRH, LH, and FSH secretion, which secondarily reduces Sertoli cell number and testicular testosterone production — compounding whatever direct cytotoxic tissue damage the drug itself causes. The paper situates this within a broader cited literature of similar findings across other anabolic-androgenic steroids (testosterone derivatives, methandienone) in rodent models, consistently showing testicular atrophy, apoptosis, and spermatogenic damage at supraphysiological doses.

> [!gap] Rodent model with a deliberately abuse-modeling dose regimen (far above therapeutic replacement doses); the paper does not test lower, clinically-relevant doses to establish a dose-response threshold for reproductive toxicity, limiting direct translation to therapeutic nandrolone use.
