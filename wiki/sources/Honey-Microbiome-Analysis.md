# Source: Microbiome Analysis of Raw Honey (Xiong et al. 2023)

**Type**: Original research, amplicon metagenomics on 36 honey samples (Frontiers in Microbiology, 2023)

## Summary

Uses 16S rRNA (bacteria) and ITS (fungi) gene sequencing — not traditional culture-based methods — to characterize the microbial communities of four honey types (monofloral, wildflower, manuka, and feral) and identify which physicochemical properties (pH, water activity, Brix, titratable acidity, color) correlate with microbial diversity.

## Key Findings

- **Bacterial community**: relatively uniform across honey types, dominated by *Lactococcus lactis* (a lactic-acid bacterium present in all samples) plus *Citrobacter*, *Pseudomonas*, *Serratia*, and *Cedecea*. 66 bacterial ASVs (amplicon sequence variants) were shared across all four honey types — suggesting a "core" honey bacterial microbiome.
- **Fungal community**: much more diverse and type-specific than bacteria — 0 fungal ASVs were shared across all four honey types. Dominant genera included *Bettsia*, *Yarrowia*, *Zygosaccharomyces*, *Skoua*, and *Metschnikowia*; feral honey specifically was dominated by *Zygosaccharomyces mellis*.
- **Physicochemical correlations**: titratable acidity (TA) was the strongest predictor of bacterial community richness/diversity; for fungi, Brix, TA, and color predicted richness while water activity and color predicted diversity.
- **Pathogen detection caveat**: potential honeybee pathogens (*Aspergillus flavus* — stonebrood disease; *Ascosphaera* sp. — chalkbrood disease) were detected as DNA in some samples, but the authors note detecting pathogen DNA does not mean the hive was symptomatically diseased — these fungi often persist as dormant spores without causing outbreaks.
- **Novel contribution**: manuka and feral honey microbiomes had not been characterized via NGS/metabarcoding before this study — feral honey (from wild-swarmed colonies, no human management) showed a distinct fungal profile potentially linked to these colonies' independent survival strategies.
