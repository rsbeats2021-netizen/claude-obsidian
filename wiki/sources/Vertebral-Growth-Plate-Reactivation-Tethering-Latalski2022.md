# Vertebral Growth Plate Reactivation via Body Tethering (Latalski et al. 2022)

A scoliosis-treatment-context finding with implications for [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]]: vertebral body tethering, a mechanical growth-modulation technique, can reactivate vertebral growth-plate function in an animal model — suggesting that at least in the spine, growth-plate activity may be more mechanically inducible/reversible than the typically one-way "closure" narrative for long-bone growth plates.

> [!gap] Animal model, and specifically a scoliosis-correction surgical context; whether this reactivation principle applies to normally-fused long-bone growth plates (the focus of most of this domain's other fusion-mechanism sources) is untested.

## Relevance to This Domain

Introduces a mechanically-induced growth-plate reactivation phenomenon to [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]], specific to vertebral tissue and a surgical growth-modulation context.
