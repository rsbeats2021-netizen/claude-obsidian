# MENT's Tissue-Selective Potency in Humans (Suvisaari et al. 1997)

The first human-subject quantification of MENT (trestolone)'s tissue-selective potency profile: 10-12-fold more potent than testosterone on muscle and pituitary, but only 4-5-fold more potent on prostate and seminal vesicles — directly because MENT resists 5α-reduction, avoiding the DHT-mediated amplification testosterone undergoes specifically in reproductive tissue. This quantifies, in humans, the exact rationale behind MENT's development already covered via [[MENT-Trestolone-Pharmacokinetics-Monkeys-Kumar2013|Kumar et al. 2013]]'s monkey data.
