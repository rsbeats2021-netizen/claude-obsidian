# Haemoglobin body (Hedy) in chondrocyte hypoxia adaptation

**Design.** Mouse genetics, chondrocyte-specific haemoglobin deletion, plus biochemistry of the condensate. Tier C: no human data.

## Key claims
- Chondrocytes express haemoglobin at high levels and condense it by **phase separation** into membraneless cytoplasmic bodies termed **Hedy**.
- Hedy acts as a **localised intracellular oxygen reservoir**, enabling chondrocyte survival in the avascular plate.
- The C-terminal intrinsically disordered region of **HBB** is required for condensate formation.
- Expression is driven by **KLF1**, independently of HIF1/2α, via hypoxia-mediated inactivation of **KDM5A** raising H3K4me3 at the KLF1 locus.
- **P50 = 27.85 mmHg** for haemoglobin-containing chondrocytes versus **58.2 mmHg** for red blood cells — a left-shifted curve consistent with storage rather than delivery.
- Chondrocyte-specific deletion caused **massive cell death in the inner zones** of developing growth plates, delayed cartilage hypertrophy; homozygotes became hydropic and died E16.5–E18.5.

## What it does not show
Nothing about intervention. No breathing, altitude or oxygen protocol is tested. See [[plate-hypoxia-implies-intervention]].
