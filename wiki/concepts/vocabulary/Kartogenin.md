# Kartogenin (KGN)

Kartogenin is a small molecule that promotes chondrogenic differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells and protects cartilage from degradation — a drug candidate for osteoarthritis regeneration therapy, sold commercially as a research compound (this domain also encountered it as a Cayman Chemical product safety data sheet, left un-ingested as non-scientific commercial content, before finding the actual research papers behind the compound).

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Kartogenin-OA-Cartilage-Protection-Mechanism|2021 mouse study]]** — KGN protects against cartilage degradation in a post-traumatic osteoarthritis model, with mechanism explored via interleukin-exposed chondrocyte culture.
- **[[Kartogenin-Mode-of-Action-4ABP-PA|Pharmacological mode-of-action study]]** — KGN is cleaved in vivo into 4-aminobiphenyl (4-ABP) and phthalic acid (PA) via amide-bond enzymolysis; tests whether these metabolites (rather than KGN itself) account for its activity. Notable because 4-ABP has known carcinogenicity concerns in other contexts, an important safety consideration for a candidate regenerative-medicine drug.

> [!gap] No source in this domain tests Kartogenin specifically in growth-plate (rather than articular) cartilage, or in a pediatric/developmental context — its evidence base here is entirely adult osteoarthritis-oriented.
