# Research brief — Letrozole's adrenal signal

## Core claim
The study most often cited for aromatase-inhibitor efficacy in boys also
reported mild adrenal suppression in roughly a quarter of patients. The
efficacy half gets quoted; the safety half doesn't.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — 24 adolescent males with limited growth potential from advancing
  skeletal maturation; mean 12.4 months of letrozole (Jacobson 2005).
- **Tier 1** — Bone age / chronological age ratio fell 1.51 → 0.68. Predicted
  adult height SDS improved from −1.41 → −0.64.
- **Tier 1** — ~1 in 4 patients showed subnormal response to low-dose ACTH
  stimulation testing. Authors conclude letrozole causes **mild adrenal
  suppression**.
- **Tier 1** — The efficacy endpoint is *predicted* adult height, not achieved.
  The one randomised trial measuring real final height found no difference
  (see `aromatase-inhibitor-evidence`).
- **Tier 2** — Mechanism: aromatase inhibition is not organ-selective; the
  adrenal participates in steroid synthesis.

## Why this deck is strong
It's a single paper containing both an encouraging result and an inconvenient
one, which is exactly the shape that makes a source trustworthy. Slide 8 makes
that the takeaway rather than the numbers.
