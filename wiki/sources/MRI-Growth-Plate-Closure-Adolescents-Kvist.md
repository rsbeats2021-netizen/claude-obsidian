# MRI Study of Growth Plate Closure Factors in Adolescents (Kvist et al. 2021)

Ola Nilsson — co-author of both [[Growth-Plate-Senescence-Epiphyseal-Fusion-Review|Nilsson & Baron 2004]] and [[Depletion-Resting-Zone-Chondrocytes-Growth-Plate-Senescence|Schrier et al. 2006]], already central to this domain's finite-proliferative-capacity thread — appears again here as a co-author on the largest human population study of growth-plate fusion timing in this domain's corpus: MRI of wrist, knee, and ankle growth plates in 958 healthy 14-21.5-year-olds.

The core finding echoes and quantifies at population scale what this domain's molecular sources have established mechanistically: girls fuse roughly 2 years earlier than boys across essentially all measured skeletal sites (consistent with earlier/lower-dose estrogen exposure driving fusion, per [[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]]), and fusion timing correlates more strongly with pubertal *stage* than with chronological age — direct human confirmation that the hormonal/developmental trigger, not the simple passage of time, drives closure.

> [!gap] Cross-sectional design (not longitudinal within the same individuals), so causal claims about the pubertal-stage/fusion relationship rest on correlation across a population rather than tracking individual trajectories. The mechanistic "why" of fusion (apoptosis vs. autophagy vs. hypoxia vs. transdifferentiation, per [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]]) is entirely outside this paper's imaging-based scope.

## Relevance to This Domain

The largest and most directly quantified human population dataset on growth-plate fusion timing in this domain, providing a real-world numerical anchor (the ~2-year male/female fusion gap, the pubertal-stage correlation coefficients) against which the mechanistic and molecular findings elsewhere in [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]] can be understood.
