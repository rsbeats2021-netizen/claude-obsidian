# Source: Honey Extracts Inhibit PTP1B and Enhance Insulin Signaling in HepG2 Cells

**Type**: Original research (Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy, 2019)
**Authors**: Lori et al., University of Florence, Italy

## Summary

Tests whether honey's phenolic/flavonoid content can inhibit PTP1B (protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B), a major negative regulator of insulin receptor signaling and drug target for type 2 diabetes. Using 20 Tuscany honey samples across 4 floral types, found honeydew and chestnut extracts strongly inhibit PTP1B (mixed-type non-competitive inhibition), while acacia extracts were largely inactive. In HepG2 liver cells, chronic low-dose exposure to active extracts (co-stimulated with insulin) increased insulin receptor expression/phosphorylation and glucose uptake.

## Key Findings

- **PTP1B inhibition potency by floral type**: honeydew (mean IC50 12 µL/mL) > chestnut (27.8) > wildflower (31.7) >> acacia (weak/inactive).
- **Mechanism**: mixed-type non-competitive inhibition (Lineweaver-Burk analysis); correlates with flavonoid content (R²=0.29), not total phenolics (R²=0.09).
- **Active compounds identified via LC-MS**: fisetin (IC50 0.6 µM) and eriodictyol (IC50 33.4 µM) among the most potent PTP1B inhibitors found in the active extracts; other candidates include kurarinone, acacetin, chrysin, pinocembrin, naringenin, apigenin, caffeic acid.
- **Cell model result**: honey extracts alone did not activate insulin receptor (no insulin-mimetic effect), but when co-administered with insulin, chronically treated HepG2 cells showed higher IR expression, higher IR phosphorylation, and greater glucose uptake than controls — except with the inactive acacia extract.
- **Interpretation**: suggests a real molecular mechanism (PTP1B inhibition + IR upregulation) behind honey's reported antihyperglycemic effects, distinct from simply "honey has a lower GI."

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] In vitro / cell-culture study only — no animal or human data in this paper.
