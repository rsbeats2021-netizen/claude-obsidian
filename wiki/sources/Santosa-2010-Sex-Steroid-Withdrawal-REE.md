# Acute Testosterone Withdrawal Does Not Change REE (Santosa et al. 2010)

The cleanest mechanistic test of whether androgens act *directly* on metabolic rate. Fifty-four men underwent a 3-week eugonadal run-in with endogenous steroid production suppressed (letrozole + leuprolide) while testosterone and estradiol were maintained transdermally, then were randomised to four 3-week arms: (-T,-E), (-T,+E), (+T,-E), (+T,+E). REE and RER by indirect calorimetry before and after.

**Result: nothing.** No change in REE or RER within or between any group.

## Why this matters more than its null result suggests

Three weeks is long enough for an acute thermogenic or substrate-oxidation effect to appear, but short enough that lean mass has barely moved. The design therefore isolates the *direct* hormonal signal from the *indirect* tissue-mass route — and finds the direct signal is absent.

This converts [[Welle-1992-Testosterone-BMR-Lean-Mass]]'s regression finding from a statistical artifact risk into a mechanistically corroborated conclusion: **androgens raise TDEE by building tissue, not by turning up the thermostat.**

> [!gap] Older men (50-80), replacement-level hormone concentrations, 3 weeks. Does not test whether grossly supraphysiological concentrations (2,000+ ng/dL) have a direct effect that replacement-range concentrations do not.

## Relevance to This Domain

The anchor for the "no direct thermogenesis" side of the question. Any claim that a given androgen "revs your metabolism" has to explain this null.
