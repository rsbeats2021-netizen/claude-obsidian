# Source: A Review of Potential Beneficial Effects of Honey on Bone Health (Kamaruzzaman et al. 2019)

**Type**: Review article, most comprehensive synthesis in this domain cluster (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2019)

## Summary

The broadest review in the [[Health and Nutrition]] domain's bone-metabolism cluster — synthesizes cellular, animal, and human evidence for honey's skeletal effects via its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, and explicitly cites/summarizes several other sources already in this vault (Zaid et al., Ooi et al., Tavafzadeh et al., Mosavat et al., Bigham-Sadegh et al.).

## Key Findings

- **Mechanistic model** (Figure 1 of the paper): honey's phytochemicals (kaempferol, quercetin, gallic acid, ascorbic acid) inhibit MAPK pathway activation and NF-κB signaling → reduces RANKL production by osteoblasts/BMSCs → less osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption, while promoting osteoblast/osteocyte viability and differentiation → net bone formation gain.
- **Animal evidence surveyed**: 
  - Normal rats: honey improved calcium absorption acutely (not chronically); improved BMD after 1 year vs. sugar-free diet, comparable to sucrose-fed rats.
  - Ovariectomized rats (postmenopausal model): Tualang honey improved trabecular bone structure, superior to 1% calcium supplementation in one study (see [[Tualang-Honey-Bone-Structure-Ovariectomized-Rats]]).
  - Fracture healing: honey+hydroxyapatite combined outperformed either alone (see [[Honey-Hydroxyapatite-Bone-Healing]]).
  - Exercise-combined studies: all from the Ooi/Tavafzadeh/Mosavat group, consistently showing honey+jumping/dance exercise elicits larger effects than either alone.
- **Human evidence surveyed**: a 79-subject RCT found Tualang honey (20g/day) performed no better or worse than HRT on bone density, blood sugar, or lipids over 4 months — small sample, single dose, short duration limits interpretation. A separate study found Tualang honey reduced oxidative stress markers comparably to estrogen-progestin therapy in postmenopausal women.
- **Author's conclusion**: honey shows promising skeletal benefits in animal models; dose-response relationship is unclear and honey composition varies by type/brand, so no universal effective dose can yet be recommended. More human RCTs, especially long-term, are needed.
