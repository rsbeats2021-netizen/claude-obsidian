# Video script — "What the AI evidence actually contains"

60s · 178 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:08] HOOK
SPOKEN: The entire randomised evidence base for aromatase inhibitors increasing
        height is four trials and two hundred and seven people. And only one of
        them checked whether anyone actually ended up taller.
  [ON-SCREEN: "4 TRIALS · 207 PEOPLE"]

### [0:08–0:18] PROBLEM
SPOKEN: These get talked about as the established way to delay fusion and gain
        height. The confidence in how people discuss them is not matched by
        what's behind them.
  [VISUAL: confident claim vs thin evidence stack]

### [0:18–0:38] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: A systematic review went looking. Four randomised trials met criteria.
        Two hundred and seven participants, eighty-four actually on the drug.
        All four improved predicted adult height — and predicted adult height
        is a model's output, not a measured person. Only one trial followed
        people to actual near-final height, and it did that under
        non-randomised extension conditions.
  [ON-SCREEN: "PREDICTED ≠ MEASURED"]

### [0:38–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: That one trial found no meaningful difference. The single time anyone
        measured the outcome that matters, the effect didn't show up.
  [ON-SCREEN: "NO MEANINGFUL DIFFERENCE"]

### [0:50–0:60] CAVEAT + CTA
SPOKEN: That doesn't make them useless — the mechanism is well grounded and
        they're used clinically. It means plausible and proven are different
        words. Ask whether a study measured the thing or a proxy for it. Don't
        forget, it's never over.
