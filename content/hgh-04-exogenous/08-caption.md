Exogenous HGH does not work the way the protocols assume.

1982, Science: inject GH into ONE leg's growth plate in a rat with no pituitary.
That leg grows more. Same bloodstream, same circulating IGF-1 reaching both legs.

1995: block GH with an antiserum. The growth plate narrows while circulating
IGF-1 stays unchanged, 10.3 vs 10.7 nmol/l.

2010: build an IGF-1 that sticks to cartilage and an identical one that doesn't.
The targeted one grows bone. The non-targeted one does nothing.

The pool that drives growth is LOCAL IGF-1, made inside the plate. It never
appears on a blood test.

Bone age: Nwosu 2021, 71 children up to 14 years. BA-CA -1.05 at start, -0.69 at
end, P = 0.09. Not significant.

Total gains: KIGS, 83,803 children. Median near-adult-height gain 1.79 SDS boys
and 2.07 girls in GH deficiency; 1.37 and 1.62 in idiopathic short stature.
