# Estrogen Therapy Increases Bone Mass in an Aromatase-Deficient Man (Bilezikian et al. 1998)

A landmark case report: a man with genetic aromatase deficiency (unable to convert androgen to estrogen) had unfused epiphyses and low bone mass into adulthood despite normal-to-high testosterone — until estrogen therapy itself substantially increased his bone mineral density. This is very likely the original case underlying the "8 known aromatase-deficient men worldwide" cited by [[Aromatase-Inhibitors-in-Men-Effects-Therapeutic-Options|de Ronde & de Jong 2011]] already in this domain, and remains one of the cleanest human proofs that estrogen — not testosterone directly — drives male bone maturation and mass accrual.

> [!gap] A single case report; while historically foundational, later, larger studies in this domain provide more generalizable evidence for the same conclusion.

## Relevance to This Domain

The likely original clinical proof-of-concept for [[Aromatase]]'s centrality to male bone health, directly complementing and probably underlying [[Aromatase-Inhibitors-in-Men-Effects-Therapeutic-Options|de Ronde & de Jong 2011]]'s broader review of aromatase-deficient men.
