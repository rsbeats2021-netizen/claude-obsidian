# GH as a Prohormone: Effects Beyond Growth (Devesa et al. 2016)

A wide-ranging review proposing a genuinely novel reframing for this domain: GH may be better understood as a prohormone than a hormone. In many tissues, GH is proteolytically cleaved into shorter forms with as-yet-uncharacterized biological activity — meaning "growth hormone" circulating levels may not capture the full picture of its tissue-level action, which could depend on local proteolytic processing this domain's other GH sources don't account for.

> [!gap] A review proposing a conceptual reframing rather than presenting new primary experimental data on the specific cleaved GH forms' activity; the prohormone hypothesis is explicitly noted by the authors as still requiring direct functional characterization.

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a significant conceptual layer to [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]] — if GH functions partly as a prohormone requiring tissue-specific proteolytic processing, this could help explain why this domain's other GH sources find such varied, tissue-specific effects (growth-plate zones, hematopoiesis, circulating stem cells) without a single unifying mechanism.
