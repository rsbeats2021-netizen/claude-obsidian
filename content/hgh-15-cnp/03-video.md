The second pathway. It is not GH, and it has an approved drug.

[cover card]

They studied 39 members of a single family in which one person had the full dysplasia. The carriers had a height z-score of -1.8, against -0.4 in the non-carriers, at P < 0.0005.

[then they looked at carriers]

From carrier frequency the authors estimated that roughly one in 30 people with idiopathic short stature carry an NPR2 mutation. So for a meaningful slice of people who were told there was no explanation for their height, there is one, and it sits in this pathway rather than in the GH axis everybody tested them for.

[the estimate]

Knock CNP out only in cartilage, leaving it intact everywhere else in the body, and the mice come out almost as short as deleting it everywhere. The same holds for its receptor.

[the knockout result]

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 121 children with achondroplasia across 24 sites, 52 weeks of treatment. Adjusted mean difference in annualised growth velocity: 1.57 cm per year over placebo, 95% CI 1.22 to 1.93, p < 0.0001.

[the phase 3 result]

There are at least two independent routes into the growth plate, and both of them work through signals manufactured locally inside the tissue rather than delivered through circulation. Which means a blood test reading one axis is reading even less of the overall picture than it looked like three posts ago, and the second axis has human genetics behind it that are cleaner than most of the first one.

[takeaway card]

Two pathways. Both local. Neither on your bloodwork.
