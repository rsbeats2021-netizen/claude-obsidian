# Source: Homologous Androgen Receptor Up-Regulation in Osteoblastic Cells (Wiren et al. 1999)

**Type**: Original cell-biology research, two human osteoblastic cell lines (Endocrinology, 1999)

## Summary

A direct mechanistic counterpoint to the "androgens work on bone only via aromatization to estrogen" narrative found elsewhere in this domain. This paper shows that DHT — the androgen that *cannot* be aromatized to estrogen — has a specific, receptor-mediated, self-reinforcing effect on osteoblasts: exposure increases the number of androgen receptors available, which in turn amplifies the cell's response to future androgen exposure.

## Key Findings

- **DHT increases AR protein 2-4 fold** in both SaOS-2 and U-2 OS human osteoblastic cell lines, confirmed by both radioligand binding assays and Western blot; effect was time-dependent (apparent by 24h, maximal by 72h) and dose-dependent (detectable at 10⁻⁹ M, maximal at 10⁻⁸ M DHT).
- **Steroid specificity**: testosterone produced essentially the same up-regulation as DHT; progesterone, 17β-estradiol, and triamcinolone acetonide (synthetic glucocorticoid) did **not** increase AR binding — this is a genuinely androgen-specific effect, not a generic steroid response.
- **AR mRNA also increased** (1.5–1.7 fold) after DHT exposure, suggesting at least part of the up-regulation is transcriptional, though the mRNA increase was smaller than the protein-level increase (suggesting post-transcriptional/protein-stability mechanisms also contribute).
- **Functional consequence**: using transient transfection to artificially raise AR levels (bypassing the need for prolonged androgen pre-exposure), the researchers showed that higher AR abundance directly translates to a much larger transcriptional response to DHT — nearly 20-fold induction of an androgen-responsive reporter gene with elevated AR, versus only ~1.5-fold at basal AR levels.
- **Mechanism confirmed receptor-dependent**: the AR antagonist hydroxyflutamide blocked both the AR up-regulation itself and the enhanced transcriptional activation, ruling out a non-receptor-mediated explanation.

## Confidence Note

> [!contradiction] This 1999 in vitro finding — that a non-aromatizable androgen (DHT) has a direct, self-amplifying, receptor-specific effect on osteoblasts — sits in tension with the later clinical consensus captured in [[Dihydrotestosterone-Clinical-Implications|Swerdloff et al. 2017]], that endogenous DHT is not required for maintaining BMD in men and that estradiol is what "maximizes" male bone health. Both can be true simultaneously (a real, measurable cell-culture mechanism that turns out not to be the dominant driver of whole-body bone density clinically) — see [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] for the full comparison.

> [!gap] Cell-culture (osteosarcoma-derived cell lines) study from 1999; whether this homologous AR up-regulation mechanism is physiologically significant in living human bone (versus a cell-culture artifact) is not established here.
