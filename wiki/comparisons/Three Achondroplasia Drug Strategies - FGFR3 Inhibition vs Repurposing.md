# Three Achondroplasia Drug Strategies: Selective Inhibition vs. Repurposing

Achondroplasia — caused by gain-of-function FGFR3 mutations that constitutively suppress chondrocyte proliferation — has become this domain's most actively drug-targeted single-gene disease, with three genuinely different strategic approaches now tracked here, all converging on the same [[FGFR3-Achondroplasia-Chondrodysplasia-Foundational-Review|underlying mechanism]]: block excess FGFR3 signaling, and chondrocyte proliferation resumes.

---

## Strategy 1: TYRA-300 — A Purpose-Built Selective Inhibitor

**[[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|TYRA-300]]** is a novel, orally bioavailable, FGFR3-selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor, developed from scratch specifically to treat both achondroplasia and FGFR3-mutant urothelial cancers — a dual-indication development strategy that spreads R&D cost across two disease markets.

## Strategy 2: Infigratinib — A Second Selective Inhibitor, With a Full Preclinical-to-Clinical Arc

**[[Infigratinib-FGFR-Achondroplasia-Savarirayan2024|Infigratinib (BGJ398)]]** is a second orally bioavailable, FGFR1-3-selective inhibitor, tested in a major 2024 NEJM clinical trial. This domain also holds its **[[Infigratinib-FGFR-Achondroplasia-Mouse-Model-Preclinical|preclinical foundation]]**: a mouse model precisely engineered to mimic human achondroplasia (Fgfr3^Y367C/+), in which daily infigratinib significantly improved the skeletal phenotype — the dosing and mechanistic justification directly preceding the human trial. This is one of the few drugs in this domain's corpus with both ends of the development arc represented.

## Strategy 3: Meclizine — Repurposing an Old, Cheap, Already-Approved Drug

**[[Meclizine-Achondroplasia-Phase2-Japan-Trial|Meclizine]]**, a decades-old over-the-counter antihistamine (typically used for motion sickness/vertigo), was found to promote bone growth by inhibiting the same pathway, and was tested in a Phase 2 Japanese trial in children with achondroplasia, co-administered with GH therapy. Its strategic appeal is entirely different from the two compounds above: an already-approved, cheap, well-characterized long-term-safety-profile drug, rather than a novel purpose-built molecule.

## Independent Real-World Validation of the Shared Mechanism

**[[Skeletal-Overgrowth-Pan-FGFR-Inhibitor-Case-Report|A 2024 case report]]** supplies a natural experiment validating the logic behind all three strategies from the opposite direction: a child treated with a *pan*-FGFR inhibitor (for an unrelated tumor, not selectively targeting FGFR3) developed skeletal *overgrowth* — the mirror-image phenotype to achondroplasia. If broadly blocking FGFR signaling causes overgrowth, then selectively blocking the specific FGFR3 gain-of-function mutation should relieve achondroplasia's growth suppression by the same mechanism in reverse. This is real clinical evidence for the mechanistic premise, arrived at via an adverse-effect observation rather than a therapeutic trial.

## Mechanistic Downstream Effector

**[[Snail1-FGFR3-Chondrogenesis-Achondroplasia-DeFrutos2007|de Frutos et al. 2007]]** identifies Snail1 as the transcription factor executing FGFR3's downstream growth-suppressing signal — a molecular link between the receptor these three drugs target and the transcription-factor network covered in [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]], suggesting a potential future combination or biomarker strategy (measuring Snail1 activity as a pharmacodynamic readout for any of the three drugs above).
