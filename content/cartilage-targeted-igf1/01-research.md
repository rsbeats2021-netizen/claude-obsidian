# Research brief — Why systemic IGF-1 isn't the lever

## Core claim
The growth plate responds to locally produced and locally available IGF-1.
Systemic levels are a poor proxy, which is why cartilage-targeted delivery is
an active research strategy rather than a solved problem.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — IGF-1 acts on growth-plate chondrocytes through autocrine and
  paracrine signalling; the tissue partly supplies its own signal (Wang 2013).
- **Tier 2** — Cartilage-targeted IGF-1 delivery exists as an explicit research
  strategy (Lui & Baron). Its existence implies systemic levels are insufficient.
- **Tier 2** — Raising systemic IGF-1 raises it in every IGF-1-responsive
  tissue; the dose-limiting constraint is not the growth plate.
- **Tier 2** — GH acts directly on human growth-plate stem cells via JAK/STAT
  and autocrine TGF-β, independent of the liver IGF-1 axis (Chu 2025).

## The argument to keep intact
Slide 4's logic — "if systemic levels were sufficient, targeting would be
pointless" — is inference from the existence of a research programme, not a
published null result. It's sound but it is inference; don't harden it.

## Honest limits — slide 8
Targeted delivery is not an available intervention. Systemic manipulation is
not a substitute for it.
