# ERα Mediates Estrogen's OPG/RANKL Bone-Remodeling Effect in Male Mice (Lindberg et al. 2001)

Using the same ERKO/BERKO/DERKO knockout panel as [[ERalpha-ERbeta-Opposing-Effects-Longitudinal-Bone-Growth-Vidal2000|Vidal et al.]], this paper identifies a specific downstream molecular mechanism for estrogen's protective effect on male bone: estradiol treatment of orchidectomized (castrated) male mice increased trabecular BMD by 143% and decreased the OPG/RANKL mRNA ratio (a key regulator of osteoclast activity) in vertebral bone. In a separate cohort of intact (non-castrated) male mice, baseline OPG expression and the OPG/RANKL ratio were elevated in ERKO and DERKO mice but not in BERKO mice — meaning the absence of ERα specifically (not ERβ) disrupts normal suppression of OPG/RANKL and serum IL-6/TRAP 5b. Directly relevant to male bone health despite using a female-sex-hormone-focused knockout panel, since the mice studied here are male.

## Relevance to This Domain

Supplies a specific molecular mechanism (OPG/RANKL) for estrogen's known bone-protective effect in males, directly relevant to [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] and extending [[ERalpha-ERbeta-Opposing-Effects-Longitudinal-Bone-Growth-Vidal2000|Vidal et al.]]'s knockout-panel findings into the bone-remodeling (rather than growth-plate) domain.
