# Source: Honey and Hydroxyapatite on Bone Healing in Rats (Bigham-Sadegh et al. 2018)

**Type**: Original animal study, 15 adult male rats, radial bone defect model (Trauma Monthly, 2018)

## Summary

Tests honey and hydroxyapatite (a bone-mineral-mimicking scaffold), alone and combined, for healing a surgically created bone defect (not a fracture — a segment of bone removed) in rat radii. This is a useful counterpoint to the diabetic-wound-healing literature: honey helps *soft tissue* wounds, but this paper shows it does **not** help hard bone-tissue defects on its own.

## Key Findings

- **Radiographic scoring over 8 weeks**: honey-alone group was significantly *worse* than every other group (autograft, hydroxyapatite, honey+autograft, honey+hydroxyapatite) at all four measured timepoints (weeks 2, 4, 6, 8).
- **Best performer**: honey+autograft was best at week 2; autograft and honey+autograft were statistically tied for best by weeks 6-8.
- **Hydroxyapatite alone**: also underperformed autograft-containing groups, and specifically had the poorest bone-marrow formation histologically at 8 weeks.
- **Histopathology at 8 weeks**: no significant difference between groups in union, cancellous bone, or cortical bone formation — only bone-marrow formation differed (hydroxyapatite-alone worst).
- **Authors' interpretation**: honey's known wound-healing benefits (from the soft-tissue/skin literature) do not transfer to hard bone-defect healing when used alone; it may still help indirectly (e.g. via improved soft-tissue integration) when combined with a scaffold or graft.

## Confidence Note

> [!contradiction] This directly complicates a naive reading of [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] — while honey *supplementation* (oral, low-dose, chronic) shows favorable effects on bone metabolism markers in multiple other sources in this cluster, *topical/local* honey application to a bone defect performed the *worst* of all tested treatments here. These are different routes of administration and different outcomes (systemic bone turnover vs. local defect healing) — don't conflate them.

> [!gap] Small sample (n=5/group), no long-term follow-up beyond 8 weeks, honey composition/type not standardized ("natural honey containing no sugar or antibiotic" — vague sourcing).
