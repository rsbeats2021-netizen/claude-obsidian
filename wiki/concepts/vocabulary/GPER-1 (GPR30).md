# GPER-1 (GPR30)

GPER-1 (originally named GPR30, "G protein-coupled receptor 30") is a transmembrane estrogen receptor structurally and mechanistically unrelated to the classical nuclear receptors ERα and ERβ. Rather than translocating to the nucleus to directly regulate gene transcription upon ligand binding, GPER-1 sits in the cell membrane and mediates rapid, non-genomic estrogen signaling — the third distinct "estrogen receptor" mechanism this domain tracks, alongside classical ERα/ERβ genomic signaling and ligand-independent ERα mechanotransduction.

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[GPER1-Discovery-Transmembrane-Estrogen-Receptor-Revankar2005|Revankar et al. 2005]]** (Science) — the original discovery paper establishing GPR30 as a genuine transmembrane estrogen receptor mediating rapid cell signaling, predating and underlying every other GPER-1 source in this domain.
- **[[GPER1-Chondrocyte-Proliferation-PTHrP-Ihh|Chou et al. 2025]]** — the domain's central growth-plate application: GPER-1 activation *increases* chondrocyte proliferation and *delays* hypertrophy by raising the PTHrP/Ihh ratio (see [[PTHrP-Ihh Feedback Loop]]) — the opposite physiological direction from classical ERα signaling, which drives growth-plate closure. Confirmed via agonist, antagonist, chondrocyte-specific knockout, and isolated 3D culture. GPER-1 expression peaks in early-to-mid puberty and falls as classical estrogen signaling rises later, suggesting a receptor-subtype handoff over pubertal time.
- **[[GPR30-GPER1-Deficiency-Bone-Mass-Male-Mice-Ford|Ford et al.]]** — independent genetic confirmation via the opposite methodology: constitutive GPR30 knockout male mice (lifelong loss-of-function, rather than Chou et al.'s acute pharmacology) show increased bone mass, mineralization, and growth-plate proliferative activity — consistent with GPER-1 as a genuine, non-redundant regulator of growth-plate proliferation.

> [!key-insight] GPER-1 is the clearest example in this domain of estrogen signaling working in the OPPOSITE direction from its classical reputation: where ERα drives growth-plate closure, GPER-1 promotes proliferation and delays hypertrophy — meaning "estrogen's effect on the growth plate" is really at least two competing effects mediated through structurally unrelated receptors, with the net outcome likely depending on their relative expression at a given developmental stage.
