# Testosterone Effects on Liver and Kidney Function in Male Rabbits (Elesawi et al. 2019)

A dose-response toxicology study explicitly motivated by anabolic-steroid misuse in athletes: since most clinical testosterone research uses physiological replacement doses, but athletes commonly use doses roughly 10x higher, this study tested two supraphysiological doses (6 and 12 mg/kg body weight, weekly intramuscular injection for 6 weeks) in male rabbits, measuring liver enzymes (AST, ALT, ALP) and kidney function markers (creatinine, urea).

The high dose (12 mg/kg) produced statistically significant increases in all five measured markers compared to controls; the low dose (6 mg/kg) showed the same directional trend but reached significance only inconsistently, suggesting a dose threshold effect. No rabbits showed overt clinical toxicity signs despite these biochemical changes. The discussion section surveys a genuinely mixed literature: some studies (often shorter-duration or single-dose designs) found no significant testosterone effect on liver/kidney markers, while others — particularly longer-duration, higher-dose designs closer to this study's own — found clear damage, including one case report of a hypogonadal adolescent developing elevated creatinine/urea after testosterone treatment.

> [!gap] Small sample (n=5/group), short-duration study (6 weeks) in a single species; the authors themselves note the extensive literature disagreement likely stems from dose and duration differences across studies rather than a straightforward "testosterone is/isn't toxic" answer.
