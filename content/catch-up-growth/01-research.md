# Research brief — Catch-up growth

## Core claim / hook angle
Suppress a child's growth and they grow *faster* afterward. That shouldn't be
possible if height ran on a clock — and it's the cleanest proof that it doesn't.

## Misconception it corrects
That time lost to illness, malnutrition or medication is permanently lost
height. Also the opposite error — that catch-up is complete and reliable.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Growth inhibition conserves proliferative capacity by slowing
  the division rate, so senescence is delayed rather than time being lost.
- **Tier 1** — Effect is not hormone-specific: hypothyroidism, glucocorticoid
  excess and unrelated inhibitors all delay senescence. Senescence tracks
  cumulative proliferation, not calendar age.
- **Tier 1** — Local infusion into one rabbit tibia produces local suppression
  then local overshoot; contralateral limb unaffected. Rules out systemic/CNS
  explanations.
- **Tier 1** — Nilsson 2014 two-component model: estrogen's *functional* effect
  (suppressed proliferation) is fully reversible; its *structural* effect
  (progenitor depletion) is irreversible, persisting 5 weeks post-treatment.
- **Tier 2** — Catch-up is frequently partial. Full restoration of predicted
  adult height depends on duration, severity and timing.

## Why it matters
"Slowed" and "spent" look identical on a growth chart and end differently.
That distinction is the most useful idea in this whole topic.
