# Research brief — Constitutional delay of growth

## Core claim
Late puberty with lagging bone age usually resolves into normal adult height on
its own. A 1973 study with an actual untreated control group is still the most
useful evidence on whether to intervene.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — 19 boys with constitutional delay treated with
  methyltestosterone, fluoxymesterone or testosterone enanthate; low doses,
  averaging ~5.3 months; compared with 21 untreated controls, followed to final
  height. Retrospective, not randomised.
- **Tier 1** — Androgens accelerate growth velocity and skeletal maturation
  together. The two effects arrive as a package.
- **Tier 2** — Low dose + short duration is the design logic: enough to start
  puberty moving, not enough to burn remaining bone age.
- **Tier 1** — Higher doses over longer periods push maturation faster than
  growth — the mechanism by which height-intended treatment subtracts height.

## Why it matters for this audience
The population most drawn to height content overlaps heavily with people who
are simply developing late. For many of them the evidence-backed action is
waiting, and that deserves saying clearly.
