# Video script — "The man who couldn't make estrogen"

60s · 176 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:07] HOOK
SPOKEN: This man's testosterone was normal. Slightly high, even. His bones were
        falling apart and he was still growing as an adult.
  [VISUAL: height chart continuing past adulthood]

### [0:07–0:16] SETUP
SPOKEN: You might remember the case of the man whose estrogen receptor didn't
        work and who never stopped growing. This is the other half of that
        experiment.
  [ON-SCREEN: "SAME OUTCOME, DIFFERENT BREAK"]

### [0:16–0:36] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: He had aromatase deficiency. Aromatase is the enzyme that converts
        androgens into estrogen, and his didn't work. So he had plenty of
        testosterone and none of it ever became estradiol. Result: unfused
        epiphyses, open growth plates in an adult, and low bone mass — despite
        testosterone levels that should have built strong bone by themselves.
  [VISUAL: T → ✕ aromatase → no E2]

### [0:36–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: Then they gave him estrogen. His bone density went up substantially.
        The deficit normal testosterone had completely failed to prevent was
        corrected by supplying the one hormone he couldn't make. His receptors
        worked fine — only the supply was broken.
  [ON-SCREEN: "SUPPLY BROKEN, NOT THE RECEIVER"]

### [0:50–0:60] CAVEAT + CTA
SPOKEN: Two natural experiments, opposite failures, same conclusion: for male
        bone, testosterone matters largely because of what it turns into. And
        yes — these are single patients. Don't forget, it's never over.
  [ON-SCREEN: "n = 1, BUT CLEAN"]
