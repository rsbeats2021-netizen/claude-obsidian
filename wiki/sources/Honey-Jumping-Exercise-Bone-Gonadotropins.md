# Source: Honey + Jumping Exercise Intensity on Bone Mass and Gonadotropins (Mosavat et al. 2014)

**Type**: Original animal study, 84 female Sprague-Dawley rats, 7 groups (BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2014)

## Summary

Tests whether combining honey supplementation (1 g/kg/day) with low (20 jumps/day) or high (80 jumps/day) intensity jumping exercise for 8 weeks affects bone mass/metabolism markers and reproductive hormones (FSH, LH) differently than either intervention alone.

## Key Findings

- **Bone mass**: high-intensity jumping + honey (HJ80) group had significantly greater tibial/femoral wet and fat-free dry weights than all other groups, including honey-alone, jumping-alone, and low-intensity combined groups.
- **Bone metabolism markers**: serum calcium and alkaline phosphatase (formation marker) highest in HJ80; bone resorption marker (1CTP) lowest in honey-containing groups.
- **Honey alone**: still significantly increased bone mass and ALP versus sedentary controls — the honey effect is not purely a byproduct of exercise.
- **Gonadotropins**: both jumping intensities alone lowered FSH and LH versus sedentary controls (consistent with known strenuous-exercise suppression of reproductive hormones); honey combined with jumping largely normalized FSH/LH back toward control levels, suggesting honey may protect against exercise-induced hormonal disturbance.
- **Mechanism proposed**: honey's minerals (Ca, P, Mg) and flavonoids (e.g. kaempferol) delivered via increased blood flow during exercise may drive the bone-formation and antiresorptive effects.
