# Video script — Two aromatase inhibitors

220 words, ~74s at 178 wpm. Target band 45-90s.

Spoken lines only. Visual cue in brackets.

---

Two aromatase inhibitors. The difference is 12.8 fold.

[cover card]

So letrozole and anastrozole are both non-steroidal aromatase inhibitors. There is one measured difference that makes them completely different drugs above the neck, and it has been sitting in the pharmacology literature since 2013 without reaching anyone who actually takes them.

[same class, same target]

Miyajima 2013 measured the brain-to-plasma ratio of three aromatase inhibitors in mice. Divide the first by the last and letrozole reaches the brain 12.8 times more effectively than anastrozole does from the same circulating level.

[where they actually go]

Anastrozole and vorozole were transported by P-glycoprotein, an efflux pump embedded in the blood-brain barrier whose entire job is recognising foreign molecules and throwing them back into circulation. Knock that pump out genetically and anastrozole's brain level rises 12-fold.

[p-glycoprotein]

They checked it a different way, perfusing the brain directly and measuring uptake clearance. Anastrozole 0.108, letrozole 0.422, vorozole 0.910.

[from a second method]

At clinical doses letrozole reaches brain concentrations above the aromatase IC50, the threshold at which the enzyme is genuinely inhibited rather than nudged. Anastrozole reaches brain concentrations below it.

[above the neck]

Two drugs sold as the same class differ 12.8 fold in brain exposure, because one is a substrate for an efflux pump and the other is not. That is measured, replicated by two methods, and mechanistically explained.

[takeaway card]

Interchangeable in the body. Not in the head.
