Hormones given. Not just the offspring. The generation after that.

[cover card]

What a compound does to the person taking it, over the period they take it. That framing has an obvious boundary.

[stops at one body]

During late pregnancy. Their F1 offspring showed clear developmental effects: females with PCOS-like features, altered glucose tolerance and dyslipidemia.

[were given testosterone]

F1 males showed reproductive alterations but not the metabolic ones seen in females — the same exposure producing sex-specific outcomes.

[differently]

F2 offspring of F1 mothers were smaller in both weight and length, with sex-specific glucose and anogenital-distance differences.

[also differed]

The F2 generation was never exposed. The effect travelled through development rather than through direct action on the animal showing it.

[worth sitting with]

Androgen use in this space is discussed as a personal risk calculation with a defined end date. Multigenerational animal data suggests the accounting may be incomplete.

[in this conversation]

Rat model, prenatal injection, doses and timing not comparable to any human use pattern. This is not a demonstrated human multigenerational effect.
