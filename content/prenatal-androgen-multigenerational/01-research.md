# Research brief — Prenatal androgen excess across generations

## Core claim
Testosterone given to pregnant rats produced developmental effects in offspring
and, through them, in a second generation that was never exposed.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Dams injected with testosterone in late pregnancy; F1 females
  showed PCOS-like features, altered glucose tolerance, dyslipidemia, increased
  anogenital distance.
- **Tier 2** — F1 males showed reproductive but not metabolic alterations —
  same exposure, sex-specific outcomes.
- **Tier 2** — F2 offspring of F1 mothers were smaller in weight and length,
  with sex-specific glucose and anogenital-distance differences. F2 was never
  directly exposed.

## Why it belongs in this library
Androgen use here is framed as a personal risk calculation with an end date.
Multigenerational animal data suggests that accounting may be incomplete. The
deck says "the timeline may be longer than the user" and stops there.

## Honest limits — slide 8, load-bearing
Rat model, prenatal injection, doses and timing not comparable to human use
patterns. This is **not** a demonstrated human multigenerational effect and the
slide states that plainly. Do not strengthen it — the topic invites
overstatement and the evidence doesn't support it.
