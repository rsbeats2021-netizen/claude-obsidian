# Supraphysiological Doses Did Not Prevent RMR Falling in Peak-Week Bodybuilders (Almeida et al. 2023)

The closest thing in the literature to "what happens to the metabolic rate of a bodybuilder on very high doses," and the answer is dominated by energy availability, not pharmacology.

Ten amateur competitors, all coached by the same person, so drug dose and frequency were effectively standardised across subjects. Body composition and RMR measured by the research team through peak week and one day post-contest.

## Result

Resting metabolic rate **fell** significantly. So did lean-mass and fat-mass markers. The authors attribute this to high-volume aerobic exercise combined with dietary energy restriction — classic adaptive thermogenesis — and explicitly note the drug protocols "did not fully attenuate the loss of lean mass."

## What this does and does not show

It does **not** show that high-dose androgens lower metabolic rate. It shows that **energy deficit outranks drug dose** as a determinant of resting metabolic rate. A bodybuilder on a gram of gear per week in a severe deficit has a *lower* RMR than he did at maintenance.

This is the single most practically important finding in this corpus for anyone trying to compute their own TDEE: the pharmacology does not protect you from metabolic adaptation.

> [!gap] n=10, no control group, no non-enhanced comparison arm, observational, and peak week specifically involves deliberate dehydration and carbohydrate manipulation that corrupt both RMR and body-composition measurement. Doses were recorded but this is self-report via a coach.

> [!gap] No study anywhere measures TDEE by doubly labelled water in enhanced lifters at maintenance or in a surplus. That is the measurement the question actually needs and it does not exist.

## Relevance to This Domain

The only data on very high doses. The lesson is that drug dose is not the dominant term in the TDEE equation — energy availability and training volume are.
