# Research brief — The nervous system input to bone growth

## Core claim
Catecholamines act on bone and cartilage cells via the β2-adrenergic receptor.
This is a growth-plate input sitting entirely outside the hormone framework
this niche runs on.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Catecholamines act on bone/cartilage cells via β2-AR.
- **Tier 2** — Mice lacking β2-AR, systemically or osteoblast-specifically,
  show **increased** bone mass.
- **Tier 2** — Catecholamines influence chondrocyte-to-osteoblast
  transdifferentiation — one of the four competing theories for how fusion
  actually executes.
- **Tier 3 — speculative** — Chronic stress, sleep and stimulant medication all
  move catecholamine signalling, making this a *plausible* mechanistic link to
  skeletal outcomes. Slide 6 is explicitly tier-flagged; it must stay that way.

## Honest limits — slide 8
Mouse genetics and cell-level work. No human height outcome data. This is a
mechanism worth knowing, not a lever worth pulling.

## Why it's worth making
It genuinely isn't covered in this space, and the reason is structural: it sits
in a different literature silo, not a hidden one. That observation — that a
field's blind spots track its categories — is the durable idea here.
