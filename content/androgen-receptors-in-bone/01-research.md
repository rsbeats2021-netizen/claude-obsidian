# Research brief — Androgen receptors in bone

## Core claim
Human bone cells carry functional androgen receptors, and non-aromatisable DHT
produces effects estrogen cannot. Whether that direct action meaningfully
drives longitudinal growth is contested at the bench, not settled.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Human osteoblasts carry functional androgen receptors
  (Colvard 1989); later localised in human bone tissue (Abu 1997).
- **Tier 2** — DHT — which cannot aromatise — upregulated AR levels in human
  bone cells and amplified transcriptional response. Estradiol did **not**
  increase AR levels at all (Wiren 1999).
- **Tier 1** — Cartilage metabolises testosterone to DHT locally, DHT dominant
  (Takahashi 1984). The machinery for direct androgen action is present.
- **Tier 2 — counter-result** — Testosterone and non-aromatisable oxandrolone
  had **no effect** on baseline longitudinal growth rate in organ culture
  (Chagin 2009).
- **Tier 2** — Chagin cites the earlier work directly and argues the results
  aren't strictly contradictory: preventing glucocorticoid-induced suppression
  is a different claim from promoting baseline growth.

## Why this deck earns trust
It's the rare case where two primary sources engage each other directly rather
than the tension being a synthesis artefact. Slide 7 says so, which is more
honest than presenting either side as settled.

## Editing note
Do not resolve slide 8. "Receptors existing doesn't tell you what they
accomplish" is the correct end state for this evidence.
