# Research brief — Mechanical loading and the GH/IGF-1 axis

## Core claim
Jumping exercise moved GH/IGF-1 markers in short-stature children. Real
mechanism, real measurement, and no demonstrated height outcome.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Non-randomised controlled intervention: jumping exercises in
  short-stature children, measuring serum GH, IGF-1, IGFBP-3 and the
  IGF-1/IGFBP-3 ratio.
- **Tier 1** — IGFBP-3 binds circulating IGF-1; the ratio approximates free
  IGF-1 rather than total. Total is not availability.
- **Tier 1** — Bone and cartilage are mechanically responsive tissues; impact
  loading is a recognised anabolic stimulus independent of added hormone.

## Honest limits — slides 6 and 7
Non-randomised, unblinded, and the endpoints are blood markers, not adult
height. Nobody followed these children to final stature. The deck states
plainly that a marker moving is a long way from a centimetre gained — that gap
is where most of this niche's confident claims live.

## Why include it anyway
Free, no adverse-effect profile, real mechanism. Almost nothing else in this
topic can claim all three, and slide 8 says exactly that rather than
overselling the result.
