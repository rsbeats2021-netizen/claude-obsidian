# Meclizine: A Repurposed Antihistamine for Achondroplasia (Phase 2, Japan)

A genuinely novel addition to this domain's achondroplasia drug-development thread: meclizine, a decades-old over-the-counter antihistamine (typically used for motion sickness/vertigo), was previously shown to promote bone growth by inhibiting a key FGFR3-related signaling pathway. This Phase 2 trial tested its safety and efficacy over 26 weeks in 9 Japanese children (ages 5-10) with achondroplasia, co-administered with GH therapy. Unlike [[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|TYRA-300]] and infigratinib (novel, expensive, purpose-built FGFR-inhibitor compounds), meclizine's appeal is being an already-approved, cheap, well-characterized-safety-profile drug being repurposed for an entirely new indication.

> [!key-insight] This adds a genuinely distinct strategic angle to this domain's achondroplasia drug-development literature: rather than developing novel FGFR-targeted molecules from scratch, repurposing an existing approved drug with a known long-term safety record could offer a faster, cheaper path to treatment — a meaningfully different risk/development-cost profile from the TYRA-300/infigratinib approach.

## Relevance to This Domain

A third, strategically distinct drug-repurposing approach to achondroplasia treatment, alongside [[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|TYRA-300]] and infigratinib — notable for using an already-approved medication rather than a novel compound.
