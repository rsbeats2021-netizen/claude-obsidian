Secretagogues by day. There is a study on why that fights itself.

[cover card]

The GH was given as a 6-minute square-wave intravenous pulse at 08:30. The GHRH challenge came at 10:30, two hours later.

[the experiment]

Injection of rhGH attenuated subsequent GHRH-stimulated peak serum GH concentrations. The earlier dose measurably blunted the later pulse, two hours downstream.

[to the later response]

The midpubertal boys showed greater fractional autoinhibition but also markedly accentuated postnadir escape, a 4.6-fold steeper slope, at P < 0.001 against both other groups. So in midpuberty the axis climbs back out from under that feedback far faster than it does before puberty or in young adults.

[in puberty]

Around 70% of your daily GH already comes out in early sleep, during the window when somatostatin has naturally withdrawn and GHRH surges into the gap. That is the body's own largest pulse of the day, running without any help.

[the part that inverts]

If exogenous GH suppresses the axis for hours afterwards, then it competes with any secretagogue placed near it in time, and it duplicates the pulse you already produce at sleep onset without help. The arrangement that follows is spacing them so the GH is not sitting on the secretagogue window.
