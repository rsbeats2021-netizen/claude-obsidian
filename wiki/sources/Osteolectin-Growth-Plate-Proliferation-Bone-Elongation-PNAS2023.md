# Osteolectin: A Novel Growth-Plate Proliferation Factor with Human Genetic Evidence (PNAS 2023)

Introduces a genuinely novel growth factor to this domain's growth-plate literature: Osteolectin, previously known only for maintaining adult bone mass, is shown here to have a distinct juvenile function — promoting growth-plate chondrocyte proliferation and thereby increasing body length in young mice. Critically, the paper also identifies a human genetic variant in Osteolectin linked to a functional phenotype, supplying rare direct human genetic evidence (rather than only mouse-model inference) for the gene's role in growth — a higher evidentiary bar than most of this domain's animal-model-only mechanism papers meet.

> [!key-insight] Human genetic evidence for a specific growth-plate-proliferation gene is uncommon in this domain's corpus (most human-relevance claims here rely on extrapolation from animal models, or on rare-disease case studies like aromatase deficiency). This paper's combination of a clean mouse mechanism plus a corroborating human genetic variant makes it one of the stronger causal-evidence sources for a specific growth-promoting gene in the whole domain.
