# Endocrinology

Domain covering steroid hormone biology — androgens, estrogen, and the aromatase enzyme that converts one to the other — and their effects on growth plate biology, bone health, brain function, and oxidative stress. Seeded from a batch of 6 related papers (plus one unrelated nootropics paper), then expanded with a 9-paper batch from the `.raw/Studies yet to analyze/` backlog.

## Open Questions

> [!gap] Whether androgen's direct (non-aromatized) effect on osteoblasts and cartilage, demonstrated in vitro by Wiren et al. 1999, Picherit et al. 2000, Takahashi et al. 1984, and Somjen et al. 1991, is physiologically significant at the whole-body level remains unresolved — later clinical literature (Swerdloff et al. 2017) treats estrogen/aromatization as dominant without directly engaging most of this in vitro cluster.

> [!contradiction] Within the in vitro evidence itself: Picherit et al. 2000 found DHT rescues cultured fetal bone from glucocorticoid-induced growth suppression, while Chagin et al. 2009 — using a similar organ-culture model and explicitly citing Picherit's paper — found no effect of androgen receptor activation on baseline longitudinal growth rate. This is a rare case where two primary sources directly engage each other's findings rather than the tension being the wiki's own synthesis.

> [!gap] None of the growth-plate fusion mechanism theories (apoptosis, autophagy, hypoxia, transdifferentiation) has been conclusively confirmed in human tissue — several have been directly contradicted by human studies despite animal-model support.
