Vilon. The one in this family with a real animal outcome.

[cover card]

Female CBA mice were given 0.1 microgram subcutaneously, 5 consecutive days per month, starting at 6 months of age. Mean lifespan increased about 16%, and spontaneous tumour incidence was reduced against controls.

[the mouse data]

A tenth of a microgram, five days a month. Set that against the 1 to 10 milligram daily figures circulating for human use of these compounds, which is four to five orders of magnitude higher on a per-dose basis before you adjust for body weight.

[how small it is]

Vilon is thymus and immune targeted. The lifespan and tumour findings are consistent with an immune or anti-inflammatory effect in an ageing animal.

[the target]

Circulating human dose figures for this family are not grounded in pharmacokinetic studies. Every documented human regimen is a pulsed course of 10 to 30 days.

[again]

A 2022 review proposes that 2 to 4 residue peptides are small enough to use real transporters, the POT family PEPT1/2 and PHT1/2, plus LAT1/2. POT transporters are expressed in human nasal epithelium, which gives a transporter-level rationale for intranasal delivery rather than a marketing one.

[a real rationale]

The only member of this family with a hard animal outcome: 16% lifespan extension and reduced tumour incidence in mice, at 0.1 micrograms five days a month. That is worth knowing and it is worth not overstating.

[takeaway card]

A real result, in the wrong tissue, at a tiny dose.
