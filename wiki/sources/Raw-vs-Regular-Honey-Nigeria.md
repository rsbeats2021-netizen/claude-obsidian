# Source: Raw vs. Regular Honey — Nigerian Physicochemical/Pharmacological Comparison (Edo et al. 2022)

**Type**: Original comparative study, 30 honey samples (Chemistry Africa, 2022)

## Summary

Compares raw (unheated, unfiltered) vs. regular (commercially processed) Nigerian honey across physicochemical quality parameters, phytochemical content, and multiple pharmacological assays (antibacterial, antioxidant, anti-elastase, anti-melanogenic, anticancer). Useful as a "what's actually in the jar" reference distinct from the bone/glycemic mechanism papers elsewhere in this domain.

## Key Findings

- **Quality standards**: both honey types met Codex Alimentarius international standards on every measured parameter (pH 3.5-5.5, free acidity ≤50 meq/kg, EC ≤0.8 mS/cm, moisture ≤20%, ash ≤0.6%, proline ≥180 mg/kg, diastase ≥8 DN, HMF ≤40 mg/kg, F+G ≥60g/100g, sucrose ≤5g/100g) — no adulteration detected.
- **Processing paradox**: regular (heated/processed) honey had *higher* total phenolic content (1034 vs 875 µg GAE/g), tannin content, and flavonoid content than raw honey — the authors attribute this to Maillard-reaction melanoidin formation during heat processing, which can itself have antioxidant activity.
- **But raw honey won on bioactivity**: raw honey had significantly stronger free-radical scavenging (IC50 514 vs 624 µg/mL) and broader/stronger antibacterial activity (larger zones of inhibition against E. coli, Proteus, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Streptococcus, S. epidermidis clinical isolates and ATCC strains) than regular honey — in several cases exceeding standard antibiotics (ampicillin, streptomycin).
- **Antibacterial mechanism confirmed**: consistent with [[Honey-Nutrition-Health-Review-2008|Bogdanov et al. 2008]] — low pH, high sugar/osmotic effect, and hydrogen peroxide are implicated; heat processing (as used for "regular" honey) is known to deactivate peroxide-based activity, which likely explains raw honey's advantage here.
- **Other pharmacological findings**: raw honey showed higher anticancer activity (lower IC50) against MCF-7 breast cancer cells than regular honey; no significant elastase inhibition was found for either honey type at the concentrations tested.
- **Microbial safety**: no coliform bacteria detected in either honey type; total viable counts and yeast/mold counts were low and higher in raw honey (expected, since it's unprocessed) but still within safe ranges.

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] Regional study (Nigerian honey only); "regular honey" processing conditions (temperature, duration) aren't fully specified, making the raw-vs-processed mechanism explanation somewhat speculative rather than directly tested. Complements [[Honey Composition and Bioactivity]] with a direct raw-vs-processed comparison that the older [[Honey-Nutrition-Health-Review-2008|Bogdanov 2008]] review only touches on indirectly.
