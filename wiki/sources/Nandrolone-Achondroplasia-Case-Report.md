# Nandrolone in a Child with Achondroplasia (Al-Mosawi 2006)

A single-patient case report testing whether nandrolone decanoate (a non-17α-alkylated, less-virilizing anabolic androgen) could stimulate growth in a 4-year-old girl with achondroplasia and completely stalled growth velocity (0 cm over the prior 6 months), in a setting where growth hormone therapy was unavailable.

Three intramuscular doses (25 mg on days 1, 10, and 40) produced what the author calls a "dramatic" growth acceleration — height rose from 76.7 cm to 80.5 cm over 2 months (growth velocity 0 → 1.9 cm/month) — with no detectable bone-age advancement and normal liver/kidney/electrolyte values throughout. The author explicitly contrasts this favorably against published GH-therapy growth rates in achondroplasia (typically 0.16-0.3 cm/month in year one), while being careful to frame the result as preliminary, uncontrolled, and requiring confirmation.

> [!gap] Single-patient, uncontrolled case report with only 2 months of follow-up (the author states long-term follow-up was impossible due to "highly unusual social and economic circumstances" in the patient's location) — this is anecdotal evidence, not a clinical trial, and the well-documented risk of anabolic steroids causing premature epiphyseal closure with continued use is explicitly acknowledged but untested here given the short duration.

## Relevance to This Domain

A striking anecdotal counterpoint to [[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|Starrett et al. 2025]]'s modern, mechanistically-grounded FGFR3-targeted approach to achondroplasia growth stimulation — this 2006 case report instead used a repurposed anabolic androgen with no achondroplasia-specific mechanistic rationale, illustrating how the field's approach to this condition has evolved from general growth-promoting agents toward disease-specific molecular targeting.
