# Video script — Accutane

240 words, ~80s at 178 wpm. Target band 45-90s.

Spoken lines only. Visual cue in brackets.

---

Accutane. It is not the one on your prescription.

[cover card]

So the famous number is that isotretinoin crushes sebum production, and the source is Goldstein 1982: seven men with severe acne, 1 mg per kg per day, for 16 weeks. Sebum production fell 88.4%, with a marked histological reduction in sebaceous gland size confirmed on biopsy rather than inferred.

[everyone quotes]

In those identical seven patients, nodulocystic lesions fell 36.2% at the end of treatment. And two of the seven got worse or did not improve at all, despite what the paper describes as profound sebaceous gland inhibition.

[from the same seven men]

Across 0.1, 0.5 and 1 mg/kg/day over 20 weeks, no significant efficacy differences were observed between them. And comparing 20 mg against 40 mg daily, the 20 mg arm worked about as well.

[does more work better]

Hair loss ran at about 3.2% below 0.5 mg/kg/day and 5.7% at or above it. The efficacy curve flattens out and the adverse effect curve keeps climbing past the point where the benefit stopped increasing.

[do not plateau]

Female sex carried a hazard ratio of 1.43 for relapse, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.35 to 1.52. A substantially higher relapse rate, entirely independent of how the drug was dosed.

[from the same cohort]

Sebum suppression is real at around 88% and is not the full mechanism, by the original authors' own conclusion. And relapse tracks the cumulative total rather than the daily number, measured in nearly twenty thousand people.

[takeaway card]

Count the total, not the daily.
