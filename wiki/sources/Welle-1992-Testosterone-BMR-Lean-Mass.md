# Testosterone Raises BMR ~7-10%, Almost Entirely via Lean Mass (Welle et al. 1992)

The single most directly relevant human study for the question "how much does testosterone raise daily calorie burn." Thirteen men (4 healthy, 9 with muscular dystrophy) received testosterone enanthate **3 mg/kg/week IM** — roughly 210-270 mg/week for an 70-90 kg man, a mild-to-moderate supraphysiological dose — with BMR by indirect calorimetry and lean body mass by 40-K counting.

## Numbers

| Outcome | Healthy men (n=4) | Muscular dystrophy (n=9) |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma testosterone | 3-fold elevation | 3-fold elevation |
| BMR at 3 months | **+7%** | **+13%** |
| Lean body mass at 3 months | +11% | +10% |
| BMR adjusted for LBM | **no increase** | +7% |
| BMR at 12 months | — | +9% (sustained) |

Pooled BMR rise: **+10% (P < 0.01)**.

## The interpretive crux

The authors' own conclusion is the important part: the BMR rise *"for the most part is explained by the increase in LBM."* In the healthy men, regression-adjusting for lean mass left **zero** residual effect. Testosterone at this dose does not make existing tissue burn faster; it adds tissue that burns.

> [!gap] n=4 healthy men. The adjusted-BMR analysis in the normal group is badly underpowered, so "no residual hypermetabolic effect in healthy men" is a null in a tiny sample, not a demonstrated absence. The dystrophy group's +7% residual at 3 months (gone by 12 months) hints a small direct effect may exist in a catabolic state.

## Relevance to This Domain

Anchors the magnitude: at a real but modest supraphysiological dose, **+7-10% of BMR**, i.e. roughly **+120-180 kcal/day** on an 1,800 kcal BMR. Corroborated by [[Bauman-2011-Testosterone-SCI-Resting-Energy-Expenditure]] (+112 kcal/d) and by the mechanism-level null in [[Santosa-2010-Sex-Steroid-Withdrawal-REE]]. Contrast with [[Strawford-1999-Nandrolone-REE-Nitrogen-Balance]], where a 19-nor androgen produced large nitrogen retention with **no** REE change at all.
