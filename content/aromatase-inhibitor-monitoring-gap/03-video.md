# Video script — "The aromatase inhibitor monitoring gap"

60s · 177 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:07] HOOK
SPOKEN: The main drug used to delay growth plate closure has a documented list
        of side effects that nobody measures in the people taking it for
        height. Not because it's safe. Because nobody looked.
  [VISUAL: pill + empty chart]

### [0:07–0:16] SETUP
SPOKEN: Aromatase inhibitors block testosterone converting into estrogen. Less
        estrogen, slower bone maturation, more adult height in boys. That part
        is well documented.
  [ON-SCREEN: "T → ✕ → E2"]

### [0:16–0:36] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: The same drugs are standard therapy in postmenopausal breast cancer,
        often for years, and that population has been watched closely. What
        turns up there: joint and muscle pain, altered retinal function, and
        subtle effects on verbal and visual memory. Because the brain makes its
        own estrogen, and these drugs block that too.
  [VISUAL: body map — joints, retina, brain]

### [0:36–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: Now the gap. Paediatric height studies of these drugs generally don't
        screen for any of that. One major cohort ran a median twenty-five
        months with no cognitive endpoints recorded at all.
  [ON-SCREEN: two checklists — one full, one empty]

### [0:50–0:60] CAVEAT + CTA
SPOKEN: It might genuinely be fine — a developing brain may compensate
        differently than an ageing one. But that's an untested guess, and no
        reported harm isn't the same as no harm. Nobody was measuring. Don't
        forget, it's never over.
  [ON-SCREEN: "NOT MEASURED ≠ NOT THERE"]
