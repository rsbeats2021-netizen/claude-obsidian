# Video script — "The accidental overgrowth"

60s · 175 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:07] HOOK
SPOKEN: A child was given a cancer drug and started growing too much. That
        accident ended up confirming how an entire class of skeletal drugs
        works.
  [VISUAL: height chart overshooting expected range]

### [0:07–0:18] SETUP
SPOKEN: The drug was a pan-FGFR inhibitor, given for an unrelated tumour. It
        blocks FGFR signalling broadly. The skeletal overgrowth was an adverse
        effect — nobody was trying to make this child taller.
  [ON-SCREEN: "ADVERSE EFFECT, NOT THE GOAL"]

### [0:18–0:36] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: To see why that matters, look at achondroplasia. It's caused by a
        gain-of-function mutation in FGFR3 — the receptor is stuck partly on,
        and that constant signal suppresses chondrocyte proliferation in the
        growth plate. Too much FGFR3 signal means too little growth. So block
        FGFR broadly and you get the mirror image. Same axis, opposite
        direction.
  [VISUAL: one axis, two opposing arrows]

### [0:36–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: Which is why three drugs are now in trials. Two are purpose-built
        selective inhibitors. The third is meclizine — a decades-old
        over-the-counter motion sickness pill that happens to hit the same
        pathway, and it reached Phase 2 in Japan.
  [ON-SCREEN: "TYRA-300 · INFIGRATINIB · MECLIZINE"]

### [0:50–0:60] FRAMING + CTA
SPOKEN: But be clear who this is for. These treat a specific genetic skeletal
        dysplasia, in patients who have it. Correcting a broken receptor is not
        the same as improving a working one. Don't forget, it's never over.
  [ON-SCREEN: "CORRECTING ≠ ENHANCING"]
