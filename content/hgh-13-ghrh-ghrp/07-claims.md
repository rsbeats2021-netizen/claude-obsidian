# Claims — hgh-13-ghrh-ghrp

## Baldelli et al. 2001, Endocrine 14(1):95-9 (PMID 11322508) — review
Verified against the PubMed record, 2 Oct 2026.
QUOTE: "the GHS were developed as artificial tools to release GH. The interest in
these groups of compounds was rekindled when it was realized that they were not
surrogates of GHRH nor were they acting through the modulation of the release of
either GHRH or somatostatin."
→ **This corrects a common framing.** GHRPs are not somatostatin blockers. They
are a third, parallel input through their own receptor.
QUOTE: "With the subsequent cloning of the specific receptor of GHS, and today of
the natural ligand for that receptor, named ghrelin ... The dual control of GH
secretion became a trinity."
QUOTE: "GHS when administered together with GHRH exert a synergistic action on GH
secretion and that combined administration is the most potent GH releaser to date."
QUOTE: "the GHS-GHRH administration may be considered the new 'gold standard' test
of GH reserve in humans, as the GH secretion so elicited is not altered by gender,
adiposity, or age."

## Compendium — mechanism and desensitisation
- "GHRH-class agonists ... and ghrelin-receptor agonists ... act through distinct,
  synergistic pathways (Gs/cAMP vs Gq/PLC) and desensitize at very different rates"
- "Ghrelin acts on GH release at two levels: centrally in the hypothalamus, where
  it co-localizes with GHRH neurons and facilitates GHRH release by opposing
  somatostatin's inhibitory effect on those neurons, and directly on pituitary
  somatotrophs via GHS-R1a ... a parallel pathway rather than direct somatostatin
  antagonism"
- "14-day continuous GHRH infusion did not desensitize somatotrophs, while even 24h
  continuous GHRP infusion attenuated the bolus GH response, establishing GHS-R1a
  — not GHRH-R — as the rate-limiting receptor for desensitization in a GHRH+GHRP
  stack"
- "GHRP efficacy is gated by somatostatin tone, not independent of it" — blunted by
  "glucose, free fatty acids, glucocorticoids, exogenous rhGH, and exogenous
  somatostatin"
- "GHS-R1a forms heterodimers with SST5 receptors ... but this has been studied
  primarily in pancreatic beta cells ... the claim that GHS-R1a 'locally
  antagonizes' somatostatin at the pituitary is an unproven extrapolation"
  → stated as unproven on the slide.

## Carried forward
Bowers 2004 (content/hgh-05): GHRP-2 drove GH >3-fold on day 1 and >1.8-fold by
day 14 and 30 of continuous infusion.
