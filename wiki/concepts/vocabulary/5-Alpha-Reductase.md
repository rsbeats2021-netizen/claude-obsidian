# 5α-Reductase

5α-reductase is the enzyme that converts testosterone into DHT locally within target tissues (prostate, skin, cartilage). It is the pharmacological lever used clinically (via inhibitor drugs like finasteride) to selectively reduce DHT production while leaving testosterone's aromatization to estrogen intact — making it a key tool for isolating DHT-specific effects from aromatization-dependent ones in this domain's literature.

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Dihydrotestosterone-Clinical-Implications|Swerdloff et al. 2017]]** — the central mechanistic reference: DHT is synthesized locally by 5α-reductase inside target tissues, and intracellular concentration (not blood levels) drives androgenic action. Clinically, testosterone therapy co-administered with finasteride (a 5α-reductase inhibitor) still produced normal gains in lean mass and bone mineral density — used as evidence that DHT itself is not required for testosterone's bone-protective effect, since aromatization to estradiol proceeds unimpeded.
- **[[Testosterone-Metabolism-Rabbit-Cartilage|Takahashi et al. 1984]]** — demonstrates that prepubertal rabbit articular and growth-plate cartilage possess active local 5α-reductase (and other steroidogenic enzyme) activity, converting testosterone into DHT as the dominant intracellular metabolite — establishing the enzymatic machinery exists in cartilage itself, not just classic androgen-target tissues like prostate and skin.
- **[[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]** — explicitly contrasts S-equol's anti-androgen mechanism with 5α-reductase inhibitor drugs: equol does not alter DHT synthesis the way 5α-reductase inhibitors do, but instead binds directly to already-formed DHT — a mechanistically distinct route to the same end (androgen antagonism).

- **[[Dietary-Supplement-DHT-Testosterone-Estradiol-Dose-Response|Angwafor & Anderson 2008]]** — a commercially-funded supplement (astaxanthin + saw palmetto) is claimed to inhibit 5α-reductase (lowering DHT, raising testosterone) and aromatase (lowering estradiol at higher doses) simultaneously in vivo — contrasted with prescription 5α-reductase inhibitors, which typically raise estradiol as a side effect since more testosterone substrate remains available for aromatization.

> [!gap] No source in this domain directly measures 5α-reductase activity or expression in human growth-plate tissue specifically — its role is inferred from the rabbit cartilage data (Takahashi) and human clinical pharmacology (Swerdloff), not demonstrated together in one system.

- **[[Testosterone-Sexual-Dimorphism-5AlphaReductase-Raz2005|Raz et al. 2005]]** — from the same Boyan/Schwartz/Ornoy lab as the Ekstein/ElBaradie sex-dimorphism work: the sex-specific response of prehypertrophic and hypertrophic growth-plate chondrocytes to testosterone specifically *requires* local metabolism to DHT via 5α-reductase type 1 — the enzyme itself, not just testosterone, is necessary for the sex-differential response to appear.
