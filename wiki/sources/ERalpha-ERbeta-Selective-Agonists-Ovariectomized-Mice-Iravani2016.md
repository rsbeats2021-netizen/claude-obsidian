# Selective ERα vs. ERβ Agonists and Growth-Plate Bone Effects (Iravani et al. 2016)

Directly tests which estrogen receptor subtype (ERα vs. ERβ) mediates estrogen's growth-plate/bone-maturation effects, using selective pharmacological agonists (PPT for ERα, DPN for ERβ) rather than genetic knockouts — a complementary approach to this domain's [[ERalpha-AF1-Growth-Plate-Closure-Female-Mice|Börjesson et al. 2012]] knockout-based findings. The clinical motivation is explicit: high-dose estrogen is used off-label to limit growth in extremely tall girls, but carries cancer and fertility risks, motivating interest in whether an ERβ- or ERα-selective drug could achieve the growth-limiting effect with a better safety profile.

> [!gap] Read via abstract/introduction; the specific comparative results (which agonist produced growth-plate closure vs. which didn't) were not extracted in full.

## Relevance to This Domain

A pharmacological (agonist-based) complement to the domain's genetic (knockout-based) ERα/ERβ dissection work, directly motivated by a real clinical safety question (finding a safer alternative to high-dose estrogen for height-limiting therapy in tall girls) that adds practical stakes to [[Three Estrogen Receptor Mechanisms in the Growth Plate]].
