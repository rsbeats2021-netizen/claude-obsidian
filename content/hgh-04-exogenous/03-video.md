Exogenous HGH. The part your bloodwork cannot see.

[cover card]

So the growth genuinely broke. And the whole time, circulating IGF-1 was unchanged: 10.3 against 10.7 nmol/l at 4 days, which is noise.

[blocking gh instead of adding it]

Reserve cell cycle time with no pituitary: 50 days. Dividing cells: 11 days untreated, down to 4.5 with IGF-1 and 3 with GH.

[and gave us the numbers]

The numbers: terminal cell height went from 19.5 µm untreated to 26.5 µm on GH, with a normal rat at 29.8. Mean cell volume went from 6,370 to 11,140 µm³, where a normal rat sits at 10,246.

[hypertrophy, not just division]

Nwosu 2021 followed 71 children on growth hormone for between 2 and 14 years, mean 5.5, and tracked skeletal maturation the whole way. P = 0.09, which is not a significant change.

[the bone age question]

KIGS collected every child treated with that manufacturer's growth hormone between 1987 and 2012. GH deficiency: 1.79 in boys, 2.07 in girls.

[the biggest dataset that exists]

Mauras randomised GH-deficient adolescents to 0.3 versus 0.7 mg/kg per week and followed them to near-adult height. More than double the dose bought 4.6 cm, P < 0.001, n = 75.

[diminishing returns]

GH acts on the growth plate directly, not only through your liver, and that has been known since 1982. Bone age did not meaningfully run ahead at therapeutic doses, across 71 children followed up to 14 years.

[takeaway card]

Stop reading serum IGF-1 as your growth number.
