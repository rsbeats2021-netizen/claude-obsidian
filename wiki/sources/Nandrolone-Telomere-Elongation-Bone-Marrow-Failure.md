# Nandrolone Decanoate Elongates Telomeres in Telomeropathy Patients (Clé et al.)

A single-arm prospective clinical trial testing whether nandrolone decanoate — an anabolic androgenic steroid already known from retrospective studies to elongate telomeres in telomeropathies (a group of disorders involving pathologically short telomeres, causing bone marrow failure and/or interstitial lung disease) — produces measurable, durable telomere elongation and clinical benefit over 2 years of treatment.

17 patients received intramuscular nandrolone decanoate (5 mg/kg every 15 days). By 12 months, 77% of evaluable patients showed telomere elongation (average +0.87 kb), and hematologic response (improvement in marrow failure) was seen in half the cohort, rising to 63% by 24 months. Adverse events were common but mostly tolerable (liver enzyme elevation, acne, virilization — expected androgenic side effects); two of seven patients with baseline interstitial lung disease died of pulmonary failure during the study, while pulmonary function stabilized or improved in the remaining evaluable patients on treatment, though function tended to decline again after treatment stopped.

> [!gap] Single-arm design (no control group), small sample (n=17), and the mechanism by which an androgen elongates telomeres is not established in this study — it reports the clinical effect, referencing prior retrospective work for the underlying rationale.
