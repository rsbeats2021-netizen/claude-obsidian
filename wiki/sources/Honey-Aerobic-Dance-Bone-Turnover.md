# Source: Combined Aerobic Dance + Honey on Bone Turnover Markers (Ooi et al. 2011)

**Type**: Original human study, 37 young Malaysian women (18-29y), 4 groups (Asian Journal of Exercise & Sports Science, 2011)

## Summary

The first human (not animal) test of the "honey + weight-bearing exercise" combination this research group had previously studied in rats. 40 women were assigned to control, honey-only (20g Gelam honey/day), aerobic-dance-only, or combined aerobic-dance+honey groups for 6 weeks; serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP, bone formation) and C-terminal telopeptide of type 1 collagen (1CTP, bone resorption) were measured pre/post.

## Key Findings

- **ALP (bone formation)**: combined honey+exercise group showed the largest increase (+31.79%, p=0.002); honey-alone group also increased significantly (+15.91%, p=0.001); exercise-alone trended up but wasn't significant (+12.98%, p=0.062).
- **1CTP (bone resorption)**: no significant change in any group over 6 weeks.
- **Interpretation**: honey supplementation, with or without exercise, appears to boost a bone-formation marker in young human females within just 6 weeks — the first evidence of this at the human level for aerobic-style exercise (as opposed to jumping/impact exercise used in the group's rat studies).

## Confidence Note

> [!key-insight] First human confirmation (from this research group) that honey+exercise elevates a bone-formation biomarker, extending earlier animal findings — cited directly in [[Honey-Bone-Health-Review-2019|Kamaruzzaman et al. 2019]]'s review as human-level supporting evidence.

> [!gap] Short duration (6 weeks), small groups (8-10 per arm after dropouts), and no bone density (DXA/pQCT) measurement — only serum biomarkers. See [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] for the full picture across this research cluster.
