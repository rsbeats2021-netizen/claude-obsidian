How to read your bone age. One thing to check today.

[cover card]

Educational and research purposes only. Not medical advice.

[dnr this slide]

Bone age is read from a hand and wrist X-ray. That one number then gets used to estimate remaining growth for your whole skeleton.

[the basics]

Mice: epiphyseal fusion at 3 weeks phalanges, 4 weeks metacarpals, tibia and femur still open at 12 weeks. Rats: 12 weeks phalanges, 16 weeks metacarpals, legs still open at 16.

[dolan et al, plos biology]

At one week: metacarpal growth rate 49% of tibial. Proliferation per column 46%.

[hand vs leg]

MRI, 958 people, 14 to 21, wrist knee and ankle. Pubertal stage correlated with fusion at every site, 0.514 to 0.598 in males.

[kvist 2021]

BMI correlated with plate maturity, 0.186 to 0.384. Physical activity: no significant correlation in males at any site, one very low correlation at the female wrist (0.095), nothing else.

[what the study found]

Read bone age as an estimate from your fastest ageing bones, not your legs. Track pubertal stage.

[takeaway card]

The hand finishes first. Always.
