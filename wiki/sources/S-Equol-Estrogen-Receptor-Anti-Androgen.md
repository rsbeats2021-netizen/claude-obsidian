# S-Equol: A Selective ERβ Ligand and DHT Antagonist Produced by Gut Bacteria (Setchell et al. 2005)

Equol is a soy-isoflavone-derived metabolite produced exclusively by intestinal bacteria (not by plants directly) from dietary daidzein. It has a chiral center and can exist as S- or R-equol; this paper establishes that humans, rats, and several other species produce and circulate only the S-enantiomer, via chiral-phase HPLC/mass spectrometry analysis of human and rat urine/plasma and in vitro fecal bacterial culture experiments. Only 20-35% of adult humans are "equol producers" after consuming soy — a trait attributed to gut microbiome composition rather than genetics per se.

S-equol has selective, relatively high binding affinity for estrogen receptor beta (ERβ), roughly 20% as potent as estradiol at that receptor, while binding ERα far more weakly — making it a naturally-occurring selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM). Separately (and, per the authors, without known parallel among other single molecules), S-equol also acts as an anti-androgen: rather than blocking the androgen receptor or inhibiting 5α-reductase (the mechanisms used by clinical anti-androgen drugs), it binds directly to DHT itself, neutralizing its activity by sequestration rather than receptor blockade.

The paper notes prior findings that equol-producer status correlates with greater bone-mineral-density benefit from soy/isoflavone consumption in both a 2-year postmenopausal human trial and animal models.

> [!gap] This is a mechanistic/pharmacokinetic paper (chemistry, receptor binding, bacterial stereoselectivity) rather than a growth-plate or whole-body bone-outcome study; the bone-density findings it cites are drawn from other studies, not generated here.
