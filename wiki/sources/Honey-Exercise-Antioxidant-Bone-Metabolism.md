# Source: Combined Exercise + Honey on Bone Metabolism and Antioxidant Status (Tavafzadeh et al. 2015)

**Type**: Original animal study, 48 female Sprague-Dawley rats, 4 groups (Journal of Exercise, Sports & Orthopedics, 2015)

## Summary

Companion study to [[Honey-Jumping-Exercise-Bone-Gonadotropins|Mosavat et al. 2014]] from the same lab, testing 8 weeks of jumping exercise (40 jumps/day) combined with Tualang honey (1 g/kg/day) on bone mineral density (via pQCT), bone turnover markers, and — new in this paper — an oxidative-stress marker (F2-isoprostanes) alongside total antioxidant status (TAS).

## Key Findings

- **Bone mineral density**: no significant differences in tibial proximal total or trabecular BMD among any group (control, honey, jumping, combined) — 8 weeks may be too short to detect BMD changes via pQCT.
- **Bone turnover markers**: combined jumping+honey (JH) group had significantly higher serum calcium than all other groups, and significantly lower bone resorption marker (1CTP) than control, honey-alone, and jumping-alone groups.
- **Oxidative stress**: F2-isoprostanes did not differ significantly between any groups — i.e., the jumping exercise protocol used here did not measurably increase oxidative stress.
- **Antioxidant status**: both the honey-alone and combined JH groups had significantly higher total antioxidant status (TAS) than control, attributed to Tualang honey's phenolic/flavonoid content (kaempferol, quercetin, ellagic acid, gallic acid, hesperetin, catechin).
- **Takeaway**: honey's benefit here shows up in bone turnover markers (less resorption) and antioxidant capacity before any detectable change in bone density — suggesting BMD changes, if real, would need a longer intervention.

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] Animal model only; BMD itself was null in this study despite favorable turnover markers — a caution against over-interpreting biomarker changes as proof of a bone-density effect. See [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] for the aggregated evidence across this research group's related papers.
