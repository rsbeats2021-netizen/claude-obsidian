# Research brief — Stimulant medication and the growth plate

## Core claim / hook angle
One of the most widely prescribed paediatric medications affects the growth
plate, and it's neither a hormone nor a steroid — so this entire niche's
framework misses it.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Pazos-Pérez 2023 (IJMS, doi:10.3390/ijms24044175):
  methylphenidate promotes premature growth-plate closure **in vitro**. First
  non-hormonal, non-steroidal drug class shown to hit this endpoint.
- **Tier 1** — 410-patient longitudinal study, treatment 0.9–16.1 years:
  reduced body weight and a temporary growth halt, with implications for final
  stature. One of the larger clinical datasets in this domain.
- **Tier 2** — "Temporary halt" interacts with catch-up growth: suppression
  that slows division rate may conserve capacity rather than deplete it.
- **Tier 2 — limits** — In vitro dose may not reflect clinical prescribing;
  effect size on adult height is not established.

## ⚠️ Safety requirement — non-negotiable on this deck
Slide 7 must stay. Untreated ADHD carries substantial documented harm
(academic, social, injury risk). This deck reports a research signal; it is not
individual medical advice and must never read as "stop your medication."
If cut for length, cut a mechanism slide instead.

## Why this topic is worth doing
Genuinely under-covered, real data from two independent directions, and it
makes a larger point: the hormone-only framework this niche runs on is
incomplete. That framing is more durable than any single finding.
