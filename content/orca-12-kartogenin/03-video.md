---

Kartogenin. Right idea, wrong tissue, and possibly the wrong molecule.

[cover card]

The preclinical record is real. It promotes proliferation and chondrogenic differentiation of MSCs, repairs osteoarthritic lesions in mouse models, and prevents matrix loss.

[on cartilage]

Almost every efficacy study is articular cartilage repair, meaning the smooth cartilage on the ends of bones inside a joint. That is a different tissue from the growth plate with a different job.

[what it was tested on]

The research delivery route is overwhelmingly intra-articular, injected directly into a joint, and a whole literature exists on nanocarrier systems built specifically to keep it there. That tells you something: the compound is being engineered for local retention because getting it to a target tissue systemically is the hard part.

[how it was given]

Kartogenin carries a carboxylic acid group, and at oral mucosal pH around 6.8 to 7.2 that group is almost entirely ionised. So sublingual delivery is reasoned to be pharmacokinetically worse than swallowing it, which inverts the advantage sublingual usually has.

[the route trap]

A real compound with a clean mechanism and genuine preclinical support, for a different tissue, by a route you cannot use, with the active species at the target possibly being its metabolite. The chondrogenic logic is attractive and that is exactly why it needs stating plainly: nothing here was measured on a growth plate, in a human, or at a dose anyone can reproduce.

[takeaway card]

Good molecule. No evidence for this use.
