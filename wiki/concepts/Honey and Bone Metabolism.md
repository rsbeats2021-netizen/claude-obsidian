# Honey and Bone Metabolism

A cluster of research (spanning cell biology, rat models, and human trials) on whether honey — particularly Tualang honey, a Malaysian multi-floral variety — protects bone health by promoting formation and inhibiting resorption. This is the single largest evidence cluster in the [[Health and Nutrition]] domain, aggregating 9 sources rather than one page per paper, per the "update, don't duplicate" convention.

---

## Proposed Mechanism

Estrogen deficiency (menopause, or high-energy-expenditure athletes) and mechanical under-loading both increase oxidative stress and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6), which activate osteoclasts via the RANKL/RANK/OPG pathway and suppress osteoblasts. Honey's polyphenols — kaempferol, quercetin, ellagic acid, gallic acid, catechin — are proposed to:

1. **Scavenge free radicals** (antioxidant), reducing oxidative-stress-driven osteoclast activation
2. **Inhibit NF-κB and RANKL signaling** (anti-inflammatory), directly suppressing osteoclast differentiation
3. **Interact with estrogen receptors** (particularly ER-β, abundant in trabecular bone), possibly explaining why effects concentrate in trabecular over cortical bone
4. **Upregulate osteogenic genes** (osteocalcin, osteopontin, osteonectin) in osteoblasts — confirmed directly at the gene-expression level in [[Biocement-Honey-Composites|Medvecky et al. 2023]]

See [[Honey Hypoglycemic Mechanism]] for the parallel (and mechanistically overlapping) glycemic-effect literature — several of the same phenolic compounds are implicated in both.

---

## Evidence by Study Type

**Cell/molecular biology**: [[Biocement-Honey-Composites|Medvecky et al. 2023]] directly confirmed osteogenic gene upregulation (OCN, OP, ON) and increased ALP activity in mesenchymal stem cells cultured with honey-cement extracts — the strongest mechanistic evidence in this cluster.

**Ovariectomized rats (postmenopausal model)**: [[Tualang-Honey-Bone-Structure-Ovariectomized-Rats|Zaid et al. 2012]] found Tualang honey (0.2 g/kg/day, 6 weeks) improved trabecular bone structure *better* than 1% calcium supplementation.

**Exercise-combined rat studies** (same Universiti Sains Malaysia research group across three papers): [[Honey-Jumping-Exercise-Bone-Gonadotropins|Mosavat et al. 2014]] and [[Honey-Exercise-Antioxidant-Bone-Metabolism|Tavafzadeh et al. 2015]] consistently found honey + jumping exercise produced larger bone-mass/turnover-marker gains than either alone, and honey alone still beat sedentary controls. Mosavat additionally found honey protected against exercise-induced gonadotropin (FSH/LH) disturbance.

**Bone defect / fracture healing**: [[Honey-Hydroxyapatite-Bone-Healing|Bigham-Sadegh et al. 2018]] is the important counter-example — honey applied *locally to a bone defect* was the *worst*-performing treatment of all groups tested, contradicting a naive extrapolation from the systemic-supplementation studies above. See the `[!contradiction]` flag on that source page.

**Human studies**: [[Honey-Aerobic-Dance-Bone-Turnover|Ooi et al. 2011]] found honey + aerobic dance raised a bone-formation marker (ALP) in young women after just 6 weeks. [[Honey-Bone-Metabolism-Female-Athletes-Thesis|Ahmad 2017]]'s thesis (the most substantial human trial, n=45, 8 weeks) found improving *trends* in BMD and bone biomarkers but only reached significance on oxidative-stress reduction — likely underpowered/too short.

**Reviews**: [[Tualang-Honey-Bone-Metabolism-Postmenopausal-Review|Mohd Effendy et al. 2012]] and [[Honey-Bone-Health-Review-2019|Kamaruzzaman et al. 2019]] both synthesize the above into the mechanistic model described here, and both independently conclude: animal evidence is fairly consistent, human evidence is thin and short-duration.

---

## Evidence Strength

> [!gap] Nearly all the animal exercise-combination studies come from one research group (Ooi, Tavafzadeh, Mosavat at Universiti Sains Malaysia) using the same Tualang honey source and similar protocols — this is corroborating but not fully independent replication. Human evidence (Ooi 2011, Ahmad 2017 thesis) is promising but short-duration and mostly trend-level, not statistically robust on primary bone-density outcomes.

> [!contradiction] Route of administration matters: systemic/oral honey supplementation shows consistent bone-metabolism benefits across this cluster, but [[Honey-Hydroxyapatite-Bone-Healing|Bigham-Sadegh et al. 2018]] shows *topical* honey at a bone-defect site performs worse than no treatment at all. Don't generalize "honey helps bones" without specifying route.
