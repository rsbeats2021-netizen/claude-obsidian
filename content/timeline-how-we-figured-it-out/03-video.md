60 years. The current answer is only about 20 years old.

[cover card]

James Tanner proposed that growth slows because a central nervous system mechanism monitors body size and adjusts growth to follow a programmed trajectory.

[it's your brain]

Parfitt publishes a piece arguing the standard causal direction is inverted. Growth stops first because proliferative capacity runs out, and fusion follows as a consequence.

[somebody says it backwards]

Nilsson and Baron transplant growth plates between animals of different ages. Growth rate follows the donor, never the recipient.

[the transplant settles it]

Continuous labelling in rabbits shows resting zone proliferation collapsing from over 95% at birth to under 10% by 17 weeks, with cell number dropping too.

[the reserve is counted]

Estrogen turns out to do two separate things. Suppress growth rate, which reverses.

[reversible vs permanent]

Nobody has established how fusion actually executes at the cellular level in humans. That's the live edge of the field today.
