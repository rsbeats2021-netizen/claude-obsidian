# GPR30 (GPER-1) Knockout Increases Bone Mass and Growth-Plate Proliferation (Ford et al.)

A genetic complement to [[GPER1-Chondrocyte-Proliferation-PTHrP-Ihh|Chou et al. 2025]]'s pharmacological GPER-1 work already central to this domain: constitutive GPR30 (GPER-1) knockout in male mice produced *increased* bone mass, mineralization, and growth-plate proliferative activity — consistent with Chou et al.'s finding that GPER-1 activation increases proliferation (so removing the receptor entirely should shift the growth-plate phenotype in the corresponding direction, which is what's observed here, interpreted through a whole-life developmental lens rather than acute pharmacological activation).

> [!key-insight] Using two entirely independent experimental approaches — Chou et al.'s acute pharmacological agonist/antagonist manipulation and this paper's lifelong genetic knockout — both converge on GPER-1 as a genuine, non-redundant regulator of growth-plate proliferation, strengthening confidence in [[GPER1-Chondrocyte-Proliferation-PTHrP-Ihh|Chou et al. 2025]]'s findings via a completely different methodology.
