# DHT Alone and Combined with Estrogen on Bone in Ovariectomized Rats (Coxam et al. 1996)

A rigorous seven-arm dose-response study in ovariectomized (OVX) rats — a standard model of postmenopausal osteopenia — isolating DHT's effects from estrogen's by using DHT specifically because it cannot be aromatized to estrogen or otherwise converted to other steroid classes. This is exactly the same experimental logic used by [[Androgen-Receptor-Upregulation-Osteoblasts|Wiren et al. 1999]] and [[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]] already in this domain, but applied here at the whole-animal level with detailed histomorphometry rather than cell culture.

The clearest finding: DHT and estrogen have genuinely opposite direct effects on longitudinal (endochondral) bone growth — estrogen suppresses it (consistent with estrogen's established growth-plate-closing role), while DHT partially counteracts that suppression even when co-administered with estrogen. Yet on other bone parameters (mineral density, calcium content, periosteal formation), DHT and estrogen were often *synergistic* rather than opposing — combined treatment outperformed either hormone alone on several measures, and DHT specifically reversed estrogen's suppression of cancellous bone formation rate.

> [!key-insight] This paper adds unusually direct, whole-animal quantitative support to a nuance [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health|this domain's central androgen/estrogen comparison]] has mostly built from cell-culture and organ-culture evidence: androgen and estrogen are not simply redundant or competing signals on bone — they can be simultaneously synergistic on some bone parameters (mineral density, calcium accretion) while opposing on others (longitudinal growth-plate-driven elongation) within the same animals, in the same experiment. The authors explicitly frame this as "additional rationale for the treatment of postmenopausal osteoporosis with estrogen combined with an androgen-like steroid."

> [!gap] A 1996 study in female rats; translation to male human bone physiology (where DHT is the endogenous rather than pharmacological androgen) is not directly tested. Body-weight differences between treatment groups (DHT increased weight, estrogen decreased it) may partially confound some of the bone-density findings, a limitation the authors themselves discuss.

## Relevance to This Domain

Strong quantitative reinforcement for [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]]'s "androgen has a real, receptor-specific, non-aromatized effect on bone" thread, and a new wrinkle: unlike the domain's other androgen/estrogen sources, this paper shows the two hormones can be synergistic and opposing *simultaneously*, depending on which specific bone outcome is measured (density/mineralization vs. longitudinal growth).
