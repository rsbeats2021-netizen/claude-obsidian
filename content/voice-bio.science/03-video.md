which bones is your bone age. (its not your legs)

[cover card]

in mice, epiphyseal fusion occurs at approximately 3 weeks in phalanges and 4 weeks in metacarpals but has not yet occurred at 12 weeks in tibias or femurs. rats, same order: 12 and 16 weeks vs still open.

[plos biology]

at one week the metacarpal was growing at 49% of the tibial rate and proliferation per column was 46%. every structural and functional marker of growth plate senescence, ie the plate exhausting its divisions, was more advanced in the metacarpals and phalanges.

[its rate]

in humans, MRI of wrist, knee and ankle in 958 subjects aged 14 to 21. pubertal stage correlated with fusion at every site, 0.514 to 0.598 in males.

[958 people, mri]

no significant correlation in males at any site, one very low correlation at the female wrist (0.095), nothing else. BMI did correlate, 0.186 to 0.384.

[that should bother you]

treat bone age as an estimate from your fastest ageing bones. track pubertal stage, since that is what actually correlated.
