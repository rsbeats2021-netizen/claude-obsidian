# Research brief — Growth inhibition conserves capacity

## Core claim
Delayed senescence is a general consequence of growth inhibition from any
cause, not a thyroid-specific quirk. Senescence tracks cumulative proliferation,
not calendar age.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Original finding: hypothyroidism slows both chondrocyte
  proliferation and growth plate senescence in rats.
- **Tier 1** — Generality test: delayed senescence follows growth inhibition
  from unrelated causes. What matters is that proliferation slowed, not what
  slowed it.
- **Tier 1** — Confirms the load-bearing assumption behind the whole
  exhaustion model: senescence timing tracks proliferation history.
- **Tier 1** — Explains catch-up growth mechanistically.
- **Tier 1** — Asymmetry: slowed proliferation is recoverable; structural
  progenitor depletion (as estrogen causes) is not.

## Relationship to `catch-up-growth`

## Why slide 8 matters
"Anything accelerating growth velocity also accelerates spending a finite
reserve" is the single most counter-intuitive and most useful conclusion in the
entire library. It is well supported and should not be softened.
