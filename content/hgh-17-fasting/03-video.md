Fasting raises GH. Both are true at the same time.

[cover card]

The signal keeps arriving, in larger amounts than normal, and there is progressively less on the liver surface to catch it with. GH rises and IGF-1 falls anyway, in the same animal at the same time, because the step connecting them has been broken rather than because the signal got weaker.

[acquired gh resistance]

Four-week-old male rats, 3 days of fasting, then bone elongation measured directly at the growth plate rather than inferred from hormones. The proximal tibial plate dropped to 30% of control.

[measured at the plate]

Both growth plates were back to control growth rates by 7 days after the fast ended, and weight was back to 95% of controls by 28 days. It ran 10 to 15% above control for the rest of the experimental period.

[and it overshot]

IGF-1 fell in prepubertal children after just six days of either 50% calorie restriction or 33% protein restriction. Six days, not six months, and in the population where it matters most.

[six days]

Fasting genuinely raises GH and genuinely lowers IGF-1, because it breaks the step between them rather than because it weakens the signal. The growth plate follows IGF-1, not GH, so the plate slows while the headline hormone climbs.

[takeaway card]

A higher GH number can mean less growth, not more.
