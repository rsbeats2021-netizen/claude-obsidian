# Tualang Honey

A wild, multi-floral honey produced by *Apis dorsata* bees that build hives high on Tualang trees (*Koompassia excelsa*) in the Malaysian rainforest. It is the single most-studied honey variety across the [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] evidence cluster in this vault — nearly all the bone-related animal and human studies here used Tualang honey specifically, rather than generic honey.

---

## Why It's Distinct

Compared to other honeys, Tualang honey has been specifically characterized as having:

- **Higher total phenolic and protein content** than Gelam, Indian forest, or Pineapple honey (other Malaysian varieties)
- **Highest ascorbic acid content** among compared Malaysian honeys, potentially explaining elevated free-radical scavenging
- **Highest DPPH radical scavenging activity** among compared honey sources
- Key phenolic/flavonoid compounds: kaempferol, quercetin, ellagic acid, gallic acid, hesperetin, catechin

## Role in This Wiki

Central substance across the [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] concept — used in:
- Ovariectomized-rat (postmenopausal) bone-structure studies
- Combined exercise + honey studies (jumping, aerobic dance)
- The one substantial human thesis study on female athletes with low bone mineral density
- A cited human RCT (in [[Honey-Bone-Health-Review-2019]]) comparing Tualang honey to hormone replacement therapy for postmenopausal bone density

## Typical Doses Used in Studies

- Rat studies: 0.2–2.0 g/kg body weight/day
- Human studies: 20 g/day (postmenopausal bone trial), 0.75–1.5 g/kg body weight (female athlete thesis)

---

## Caveats

> [!gap] Nearly all Tualang-honey bone research traces back to one Malaysian research cluster (Universiti Sains Malaysia / Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia groups) using honey from the same or similar suppliers (e.g. FAMA — Federal Agricultural Marketing Authority). Independent replication with other honey sources/labs is limited.
