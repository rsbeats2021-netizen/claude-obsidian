---

Vosoritide. The only approved drug on this list that grows bone.

[cover card]

FGFR3 puts a brake on chondrocyte proliferation, and achondroplasia is caused by an FGFR3 that is permanently switched on. The obvious target is FGFR3 itself, which is a hard and unselective drug problem.

[the mechanism]

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 121 children with achondroplasia across 24 sites in seven countries, 52 weeks of daily injections at 15 µg/kg. Adjusted mean difference in annualised growth velocity: 1.57 cm per year over placebo, 95% CI 1.22 to 1.93, p < 0.0001.

[what it bought]

Straight from the paper: it is not known whether final adult height will be increased, or what the harms of long-term therapy might be. A rate can rise and then be given back later through earlier fusion, and 52 weeks cannot tell you which happened.

[their own caveat]

It is approved for achondroplasia, in children, with open growth plates. The label does not support adult use and there is no trial in anyone with a normal FGFR3.

[who it is for]

NPR2 loss of function causes acromesomelic dysplasia and gain of function causes skeletal overgrowth. Carriers of a single broken copy run a height z-score of -1.8 against -0.4 in non-carriers, P < 0.0005.

[why this is not speculative]

The best-evidenced compound on this entire list: randomised, placebo-controlled, in humans, on a pathway with independent genetic support in both directions. What it does not have is a final-height result, a licence outside achondroplasia, or any data in a normal FGFR3.

[takeaway card]

Approved, randomised, and narrow.
