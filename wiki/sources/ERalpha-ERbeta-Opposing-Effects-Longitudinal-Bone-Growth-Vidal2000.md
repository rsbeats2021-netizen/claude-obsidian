# ERα and ERβ Have Opposing Effects on Longitudinal Bone Growth (Vidal et al.)

A foundational genetic dissection using the full ERKO/BERKO/DERKO knockout panel (ERα-only, ERβ-only, and double knockout female mice): longitudinal bone growth was **decreased** in ERα-knockout mice, **increased** in ERβ-knockout mice, and **intermediate** in the double knockout — direct proof that ERα and ERβ are not simply redundant estrogen receptors but actively oppose each other in regulating skeletal growth. The bone-growth phenotype tracked serum IGF-I levels, tying this ER-subtype dissociation to the GH/IGF-1 axis. A second, cleanly dissociated phenotype in the same mice: endogenous estrogen's fat-reducing effect requires ERα specifically, with no role for ERβ.

> [!key-insight] This is the clearest and most direct evidence in this domain that ERα and ERβ are not interchangeable "estrogen receptor" isoforms but a genuine antagonistic pair — one (ERα) restrains growth, the other (ERβ) apparently restrains ERα's restraint (since removing ERβ *increases* growth). This adds a mechanistic backbone underneath [[Three Estrogen Receptor Mechanisms in the Growth Plate]]'s already-established finding that ERα drives closure (Börjesson et al. 2012) — here showing that finding is only half the picture, since ERβ pulls in the opposite direction.
