# Testosterone Metabolism in Prepubertal Rabbit Cartilage (Takahashi et al. 1984)

Takahashi et al. (1984) incubated prepubertal rabbit articular and growth-plate cartilage with radiolabeled testosterone and measured its metabolites. Both cartilage types converted testosterone locally into DHT (the dominant intracellular metabolite), androstenedione, and androstanediols — demonstrating that cartilage possesses its own local steroidogenic enzyme activity (5α-reductase and others) rather than merely responding to circulating hormone levels.

This is a biochemical/mechanistic paper: it establishes *that* local androgen metabolism occurs in cartilage, not *whether* the resulting DHT drives a specific growth or maintenance effect — that functional question is addressed (with conflicting answers) by [[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]] and [[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]].

> [!gap] A 1984 rabbit study; whether the same local-metabolism capacity and its magnitude are conserved in human growth-plate cartilage is not addressed here.

## Relevance to This Domain

Provides the biochemical substrate for the [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] debate: local DHT production machinery clearly exists in cartilage, which is a necessary (though not sufficient) precondition for any direct, non-aromatized androgen effect on growth-plate biology.
