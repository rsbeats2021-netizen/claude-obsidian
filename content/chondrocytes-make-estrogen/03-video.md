Your growth plate. And vitamin D is one of the things controlling that.

[cover card]

Made in a gland, shipped through blood, received by tissue. Tissue as a passive endpoint waiting for instructions.

[as delivered goods]

Measured in isolated rat costochondral growth plate cells. The cartilage is an endocrine site, not just a target of one.

[produce 17-beta-estradiol]

1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 regulates that local estradiol production — putting vitamin D upstream of local estrogen signalling in cartilage.

[is active vitamin d]

Blood hormone levels often correlate poorly with what a tissue is actually experiencing. Local production and local conversion can diverge from what a blood test shows.

[something confusing]

Both articular and growth plate cartilage actively convert testosterone into DHT locally, with DHT the dominant metabolite that accumulates there.

[with androgens too]

Asking what your circulating hormone levels are may be asking the wrong thing. What matters is what the tissue makes and converts where the growth happens.

[the whole question]

This is rat cell-culture work. It establishes the machinery exists and is vitamin-D-responsive.

[takeaway card]

Mechanism demonstrated. Magnitude unknown.
