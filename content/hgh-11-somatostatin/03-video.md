Somatostatin. It decides whether GH is a pulse at all.

[cover card]

GHRH sets how big any given pulse gets. Somatostatin sets how often pulses happen and how low the gaps fall between them.

[amplitude versus rhythm]

They gave two GHRH stimuli 90 minutes apart to six adult males, on three different background infusions. On plain saline, the pituitary answered both stimuli without the second one being any weaker, which tells you the pituitary itself was not the limiting factor.

[six men, ghrh, somatostatin]

Around 70% of daily GH output in men comes out during early sleep, and roughly 70% of sleep GH pulses land during slow-wave sleep. Those two numbers are the most important timing facts in this whole axis.

[the somatostatin window]

If somatostatin gates the entire system, then anything that lowers it changes what every other input is capable of doing. That is the lever the next few posts are about, and it is the reason GHRH and GHRP both behave differently at different times of day.

[takeaway card]

Nothing in this axis works without asking what the brake is doing.
