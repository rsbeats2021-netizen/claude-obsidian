# Claims — hgh-17-fasting

## Farnum et al. 2003, Pediatr Res 53(1):33-41 (PMID 12508079) — rat
Verified against the PubMed record, 3 Oct 2026. 4-week-old male rats, 3-day fast.
QUOTE: "A 3-d fast caused an immediate and profound decrease in rate of growth in
the proximal tibial growth plate to only 30% of that of control animals, while
stopping growth in the distal tibial growth plate."
QUOTE: "Animals fasted for 3 d reached a weight approximately 60% of the control
littermates. By 28 d postfasting, fasted animals had regained weight to 95% of
control levels."
QUOTE: "growth rate in both growth plates reached that of the control littermates
by 7 d postfasting. The proximal tibial growth plate then maintained rates that
were 10-15% higher than control over the rest of the experimental period."
QUOTE: "Changes in elongation rates were reflected by dramatic changes in growth
plate morphology in all cellular zones."
→ Two plates in the same leg responded differently. Catch-up overshot control.

## Compendium — acquired GH resistance
QUOTE: "despite increased GH levels, IGF-1 was significantly down-regulated, and
the inhibition of bone growth during fasting appears secondary to low circulating
IGF-1"
QUOTE: "In male rabbits undergoing 48-hour fasting, proliferative and hypertrophic
chondrocyte numbers significantly declined; despite increased GH levels, hepatic
IGF-1 expression and circulating IGF-1 were significantly reduced, indicating
fasting-induced hepatic GH resistance"
QUOTE: "Energy deficit downregulates hepatic GH receptor (GHR) expression,
producing acquired GH resistance in which GH rises but IGF-1 falls; low insulin
elevates IGFBP-1, reducing free IGF-1."
QUOTE: "IGF-1 levels were reduced in prepubertal children after just six days of
either 50% calorie restriction or 33% protein restriction"
QUOTE: "Protein-calorie malnutrition can induce nutritionally responsive proteins
that block hepatic GH signal transduction by inhibiting the JAK/STAT pathway"
QUOTE: "it takes approximately 3 days of fasting, not an overnight fast, to
meaningfully collapse growth-plate output"

## THE COUNTERPOINT, kept on a slide
QUOTE: "a 2-year, 25%-calorie-restricted human trial in adults found sustained
restriction did not significantly lower IGF-1, attributed by the study authors to
the trial's high protein" intake.
→ The effect is not universal. Protein intake appears to matter independently of
calories, and the slide says so.
