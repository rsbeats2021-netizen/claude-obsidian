# Research brief — Magnesium deficiency and the growth plate

## Core claim
Six months of moderate dietary magnesium restriction produced disordered
chondrocyte columns, thinner growth plates and reduced Sox9 — with body weight
unchanged, so this is a specific cartilage deficit rather than general
malnutrition.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — 10% of nutrient requirement for six months in rats: disordered
  chondrocyte column formation, thinner growth plates, reduced articular
  chondrocyte density, reduced ECM proteoglycan.
- **Tier 2** — Sox9 immunolocalisation reduced in both growth plate and
  articular chondrocytes. Sox9 is the master transcription factor for
  chondrocyte identity.
- **Tier 1 — the control that matters** — Body weight was normal between
  groups. Without this the finding would be indistinguishable from
  undernutrition.

## The reasoning constraint — slides 7 and 8
This is a **deficiency** model. It shows what a shortfall damages. It does not
show that supplementing above adequacy helps. Slide 8 generalises that into the
rule this audience most needs: deficiency studies describe what happens below
the line and are routinely quoted as if they demonstrated benefit above it.

Do not edit this deck into a supplement recommendation. The evidence does not
support one and the deck explicitly says so.
