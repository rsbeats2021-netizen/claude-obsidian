# Long-Term Letrozole for McCune-Albright Precocious Puberty (Estrada et al. 2016)

A direct companion to [[Fulvestrant-Precocious-Puberty-McCune-Albright|Sims et al. 2012]] already in this domain — both treat the same underlying condition (McCune-Albright syndrome precocious puberty, caused by recurrent estrogen-secreting ovarian cysts from GNAS mutations) but via opposite pharmacological strategies: Sims et al. used fulvestrant to block/degrade the estrogen receptor itself, while this NIH-based study (from the same Section on Skeletal Disorders that produced several other sources in this domain) uses letrozole to block estrogen *synthesis* via aromatase inhibition upstream of the receptor.

With the longest follow-up of any MAS-PP letrozole study to date, the results were clearly favorable: skeletal maturation rate declined more than threefold, growth velocity normalized from a highly accelerated baseline to slightly below average, and predicted adult height improved substantially — with actual adult-height outcomes in the 4 patients who completed treatment measurably better than untreated historical controls. No adverse events were reported, including no effect on uterine or ovarian size (a specific concern with estrogen-pathway interventions in this population).

> [!key-insight] Sims et al. 2012 (fulvestrant, receptor-level blockade) and this paper (letrozole, synthesis-level blockade) treat the identical patient population and pathology with two different points of intervention in the same estrogen-signaling pathway — a rare direct, same-condition comparison of receptor-blockade vs. synthesis-blockade strategies within this domain, though the two studies don't directly compare outcomes to each other.

> [!gap] Retrospective cohort design (not randomized), and only 4 of 28 treated subjects had reached final adult height at the time of analysis — the height-outcome conclusion rests on a small subset even though the growth-velocity and bone-age findings are robust across the full cohort.
