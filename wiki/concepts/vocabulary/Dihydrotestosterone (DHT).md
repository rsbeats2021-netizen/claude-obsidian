# Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)

DHT is a potent androgen metabolite of testosterone, produced locally in target tissues by 5α-reductase. Unlike testosterone, DHT **cannot** be converted to estrogen by aromatase — which makes it the domain's standard tool for isolating "pure," non-aromatization-dependent androgen effects from estrogen-mediated ones. DHT is the single most cross-referenced pharmacological term in this domain, appearing in 8 of its 17 on-topic sources.

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Upregulation-Osteoblasts|Wiren et al. 1999]]** — DHT specifically (matched by testosterone, not by estradiol) up-regulates androgen receptor levels 2-4 fold in human osteoblastic cell lines, amplifying transcriptional responsiveness to androgen — a direct, non-aromatized mechanism.
- **[[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]]** — DHT added to cultured fetal rat metatarsal bones prevented glucocorticoid-induced suppression of growth/mineralization and had a modest stimulatory effect alone — because DHT can't be aromatized, this was interpreted as a genuine direct-AR effect.
- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]]** — tested non-aromatizable androgens (oxandrolone, alongside testosterone) in a similar culture system and found **no** effect on baseline longitudinal growth, directly disputing the growth-promoting interpretation of Picherit's DHT result (while noting rescue-from-suppression and baseline-growth-promotion are different claims).
- **[[Dihydrotestosterone-Clinical-Implications|Swerdloff et al. 2017]]** — the domain's most authoritative DHT review: argues *circulating* DHT levels are clinically near-irrelevant (intracellular, locally-regulated DHT is what matters), and that endogenous DHT is not required for maintaining bone density in men — estradiol (from aromatization) is what "maximizes" male bone health, not DHT.
- **[[Testosterone-Metabolism-Rabbit-Cartilage|Takahashi et al. 1984]]** — shows both articular and growth-plate cartilage in prepubertal rabbits locally metabolize testosterone into DHT, which is the dominant accumulated intracellular metabolite — establishing that cartilage actively produces its own local DHT rather than only receiving it from circulation.
- **[[Sex-Steroid-Regulation-Cartilage-Bone-Proliferation|Somjen et al. 1991]]** — DHT stimulates proliferation only in bone cells from male-donor rats (sex-specific), but stimulates cartilage cell proliferation regardless of donor sex — a tissue-specific divergence in DHT sensitivity.
- **[[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]]** — notes DHT directly stimulates chondrocyte proliferation and proteoglycan synthesis in vitro, but attributes androgen's role in growth-plate *fusion* specifically to aromatization rather than to DHT/AR signaling.
- **[[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]** — S-equol acts as a potent anti-androgen by binding directly to DHT itself (sequestering it), rather than blocking the androgen receptor or inhibiting 5α-reductase — a third, distinct mechanism of DHT antagonism.

- **[[11-Oxygenated-Androgens-PCOS-Hirsutism-Thesis|Varausová 2023]]** — reviews 11-oxygenated DHT/testosterone derivatives produced from adrenal 11β-hydroxy-androstenedione, a distinct, high-potency androgen route beyond the classical gonadal 5α-reductase pathway, potentially relevant to PCOS/hirsutism symptom severity.

- **[[DHEA-and-the-Skeleton-Review|Gordon et al. 1999]]** — DHT (alongside DHEA and testosterone) suppresses the pro-resorptive cytokine IL-6 in bone marrow stromal cells, but only in AR-expressing cells — a specific cytokine-level mechanism for how androgens (including DHT specifically) protect against bone resorption, distinct from the proliferation/differentiation mechanisms covered elsewhere in this vocabulary entry.

> [!contradiction] The Picherit-2000-vs-Chagin-2009 disagreement over what DHT actually does at the growth plate is the sharpest unresolved tension in this domain — see [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]].
