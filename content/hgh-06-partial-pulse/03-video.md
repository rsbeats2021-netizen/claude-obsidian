Half pulsed GH. Same dose. It is a dial, not a switch.

[cover card]

So the pulsatile versus continuous finding is settled in rats. Gevers 1996 built the experimental arm that answers exactly that.

[the next question]

The combined half-and-half infusion was more effective than the same dose all given continuously, and less effective than the same dose all given in pulses, P < 0.05 for that second comparison. So it landed cleanly in between the two extremes rather than failing to register.

[the result]

The 36 pulsed plus 36 continuous arm was much more effective than 36 continuous alone, P < 0.01, which you would expect since it is double the dose. But it was not significantly better than 36 pulsed alone.

[the arm that isolates it]

Put those two findings next to each other and the conclusion is forced. They also confirmed the headline across the whole range they tested: pulsatile infusion increased weight gain more than continuous, and this was apparent over the entire range of doses tested, with nose-to-anus length gain also favouring pulses at P = 0.014.

[the pulse is the ingredient]

Pattern is a gradient rather than a switch. More of your dose arriving as a pulse moved growth further, every time it was tested, across the full dose range.

[takeaway card]

More pulse, more growth. Flat adds nothing.
