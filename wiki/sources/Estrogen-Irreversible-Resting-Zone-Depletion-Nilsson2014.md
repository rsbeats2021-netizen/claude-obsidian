# Estrogen Irreversibly Depletes Resting-Zone Progenitor Cells in Female Rabbits (Nilsson et al. 2014)

This paper directly resolves the open question this vault flagged when ingesting [[Depletion-Resting-Zone-Chondrocytes-Growth-Plate-Senescence|Schrier et al. 2006]]: that earlier paper (same NICHD lab, same Ola Nilsson and Jeffrey Baron authorship) found estrogen accelerates growth-plate senescence WITHOUT increasing resting-zone proliferation or measurably depleting cell number — but that study used MALE rabbits specifically to avoid confounding by natural female reproductive cycling. This 2014 paper runs the equivalent experiment in FEMALE rabbits and gets a different, mechanistically clarifying answer: estrogen *does* accelerate resting-zone progenitor cell depletion in females, and — critically — this depletion is irreversible, persisting for 5 weeks after estrogen treatment stops, while growth-plate *function* (proliferation rate, growth rate) recovers normally once treatment ends.

This establishes a genuinely elegant two-component model: estrogen has (1) an irreversible structural effect — permanently depleting the finite resting-zone progenitor pool that underlies [[Growth-Plate-Senescence-Epiphyseal-Fusion-Review|Nilsson & Baron 2004]]'s whole finite-capacity framework — layered on top of (2) a separate, reversible functional suppression of growth-plate activity. The depletion mechanism is explicitly NOT classical apoptosis, consistent with [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]]'s human-tissue finding (Emons et al., cited via Shim 2015) that apoptosis doesn't explain fusion in unmodified human growth plates.

> [!key-insight] Reading Schrier et al. 2006 (male rabbits, no cell-number depletion) and this paper (female rabbits, clear irreversible depletion) side by side reveals the sex-specificity of estrogen's resting-zone effect was hiding in plain sight — the two studies used different sexes specifically to control for reproductive confounds, and that methodological choice is exactly what produced the apparently contradictory findings. This isn't a contradiction at all once you notice both papers deliberately chose single-sex designs.

> [!gap] Rabbit model; whether this irreversible-depletion/reversible-suppression dual mechanism translates directly to human growth-plate biology (where fusion timing and mechanism differ from rabbits in other ways per this domain's other sources) is not established here.
