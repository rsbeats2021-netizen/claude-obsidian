# Research brief — Different bones, different clocks

## Core claim
Growth plate senescence runs earlier in smaller bones than larger ones — and
bone age is read from a hand X-ray, i.e. from the bones furthest along.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Senescence occurs earlier in metacarpals and phalanges than in
  femurs and tibias (Dolan 2022).
- **Tier 1** — MRI of 958 healthy people aged 14.0–21.5: fusion timing varied
  by skeletal site *within the same individual* across wrist, knee and ankle
  (Kvist 2021).
- **Tier 1** — Pubertal stage correlated with fusion score at ρ = 0.514–0.598,
  stronger than chronological age — supporting hormonal exposure rather than
  time elapsed as the direct driver.
- **Tier 1** — Complete fusion roughly 2 years earlier in girls than boys
  across all measured sites.

## Why this deck is worth making
It undermines a number everyone treats as physical fact. Bone age is a useful
clinical estimate produced from the bones that finish first, used to reason
about the bones that determine height. Slide 8 lands it as "useful estimate,
not a physical fact" rather than dismissing the measure.

## Editing note
Don't overstate slide 4. The claim is that hand bones senesce earlier, so a
hand-derived index is an imperfect proxy for leg growth potential. It is *not*
a claim that bone age is clinically worthless.
