# Research brief — Trenbolone and long bone length

## Core claim
Trenbolone numerically shortened femur and humerus length in young rats over
four weeks, with no structural benefit found. Not proof of harm; definitively
not evidence of benefit.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Young rats, 4 weeks, four groups (trenbolone / exercise /
  trenbolone+exercise / control). Trenbolone **numerically shortened** femur
  and humerus length; the difference **did not reach statistical significance**.
- **Tier 2** — No significant differences in bone corpus thickness, cortex
  thickness or medullary diameter between any group.

## Precision required — this is the whole deck
The finding is *no benefit found, with the length trend pointing the wrong
way*. It is **not** "trenbolone shortens bones." Slide 4 and slide 8 both carry
the non-significance explicitly and must keep it. Overstating this would be the
same error the deck criticises, in the opposite direction.

## Why it belongs in the library
Trenbolone appears in height stacks justified entirely by receptor-level
reasoning — aromatase inhibition, IGF-1 sensitivity, delayed closure. This is
one of the few places someone measured the actual endpoint in a growing animal.
Slide 7 states the general principle: plausible mechanisms lose to measured
outcomes when they disagree.
