# Pan-FGFR Inhibitor Causes Skeletal Overgrowth — A Clinical Mirror Image of Achondroplasia Therapy (Majlessipour et al. 2024)

A striking real-world clinical case directly complementing this domain's FGFR3/achondroplasia drug-development thread: a pre-pubescent child treated with a pan-FGFR inhibitor (for an unrelated oncologic indication) developed skeletal overgrowth — essentially the opposite clinical phenotype from achondroplasia, where a FGFR3 gain-of-function mutation constitutively suppresses growth. This is direct clinical (not animal-model) confirmation that FGFR signaling normally acts as a brake on bone growth, and that removing that brake broadly (rather than correcting the specific achondroplasia mutation) produces overgrowth as a predictable, mechanistically consistent adverse effect.

> [!key-insight] This is a natural human experiment validating the entire rationale behind [[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|TYRA-300]] and infigratinib (FGFR3-selective inhibitors developed specifically to treat achondroplasia): if broad FGFR inhibition causes overgrowth, then FGFR3-specific inhibition should relieve achondroplasia's growth suppression by the same mechanism in reverse — this case report supplies real clinical evidence for the drug class's underlying logic, from an adverse-effect angle rather than a therapeutic trial.
