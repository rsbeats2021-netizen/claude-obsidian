# Keenan et al. 1996 — DHT-hp and GHBP

*Metabolism* 45(12):1521–6. PMID 8969286. Full text obtained 2026-10-17; dosing quoted from Methods, which the abstract omits.

14 boys with delayed puberty and short stature: **testosterone enanthate (n=7)** or **DHT heptanoate (n=7)**, across two protocols.

## Dosing — recovered from full text

**Protocol A (8 subjects: 3 TE, 6 DHT-hp)** — the [[Keenan-1993-DHT-Heptanoate-Pubertal-Growth]] regimen:

> "three subjects received T enanthate and six DHT heptanoate intramuscularly, **100 mg initially and 200 mg after 1 and 2 months**. Repeat studies were at 2.5 months (2 weeks after the third dose). The total androgen dose received by subjects in protocol A before the second study ranged from **360 to 500 mg/m²**."

**Protocol B (6 subjects: 4 TE, 1 DHT-hp)** — a body-surface-area regimen:

> "the treatment regimen was **80 mg/m² per dose** of either T (four subjects) or DHT (one subject), **monthly for six doses**. The follow-up study was at 3.25 months (1 week after the fourth dose). The total androgen dose received before the second study was **320 mg/m²**."

> Two internal inconsistencies in the paper itself, not in this summary: the Methods say protocol A had "eight subjects" but name 3 + 6 = 9, and protocol B "six subjects" but name 4 + 1 = 5 (the overall total of 14 is right, and Table 2 lists 7 DHT patients). Take the per-patient table as authoritative.

## Plasma DHT achieved

> "plasma DHT levels at 1 week posttreatment were **24.2 ± 8.4 nmol/L** (P<0.01, n = 4), and at 2 weeks, **29.2 ± 7.7** (P<0.01, n = 4). Thus, the T concentration during T treatment was similar to the DHT level during DHT treatment."

**703 and 848 ng/dL** respectively. The 703 figure sits inside the 300–800 ng/dL band the patent reports for its 10 further adolescents; 848 is just above it. These are the highest blood DHT values found in this sweep of the DHT ester literature, though the sweep covers only accessible sources.

Peak LH response to LHRH was suppressed in both arms (T: 27.6 ± 5.9 → 5.9 ± 2.5 IU/L, P<0.10; DHT: significant). Note [[Keenan-1993-DHT-Heptanoate-Pubertal-Growth]] gives the same pre-treatment T-arm value as 27.6 ± **10.7**; both are reproduced as printed in their respective papers.

## Why this matters

**GHBP fell on both androgens**, so the decline is androgen-receptor-dependent rather than estrogen-mediated — DHT is non-aromatisable and reproduced it. IGF-1 behaves the opposite way: it tracks GH, which tracks estrogen. This splits the GH axis into an AR-driven component (GHBP) and an ER-driven component (GH secretion, IGF-1).
