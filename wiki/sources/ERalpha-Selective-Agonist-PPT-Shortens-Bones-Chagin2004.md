# Selective ERα (Not ERβ) Agonism Reproduces Estrogen's Bone-Shortening Effect (Chagin et al.)

A pharmacological complement to [[ERalpha-ERbeta-Opposing-Effects-Longitudinal-Bone-Growth-Vidal2000|Vidal et al.]]'s knockout-genetics finding: using selective agonists rather than knockouts, PPT (ERα-selective) shortened ovariectomized mice's tibia/femur bones just like estradiol itself, while DPN (ERβ-selective) did not — confirming via a second, independent methodology (agonist pharmacology instead of gene knockout) that ERα specifically drives estrogen's growth-limiting, growth-plate-closing effect.

> [!key-insight] Motivated explicitly by clinical concern: the abstract notes high-dose estrogen is used to limit growth in extremely tall girls but carries cancer/fertility side-effect risk — this paper's ERα-vs-ERβ dissection raises the possibility of an ERβ-sparing (or ERα-selective) approach that could achieve height-limiting effects with a different side-effect profile, though this paper doesn't itself test that clinical hypothesis.
