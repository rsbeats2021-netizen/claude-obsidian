# Androgen Receptor Modulation Does Not Affect Longitudinal Bone Growth (Chagin et al. 2009)

Chagin et al. (2009) tested whether direct androgen receptor stimulation (testosterone and oxandrolone, a non-aromatizable synthetic androgen) affects longitudinal growth rate in cultured fetal rat metatarsal bones — the same class of ex vivo model used by [[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]]. Neither androgen changed growth rate relative to untreated controls, leading the authors to conclude that AR activation does not have a direct effect on longitudinal bone growth in this system.

Notably, this paper explicitly cites and discusses Picherit et al. 2000 (cited as reference 27 in the original), acknowledging the apparent tension: Picherit found DHT *rescued* bones from glucocorticoid-induced suppression, while Chagin found androgens had *no baseline effect* on growth. The authors' own resolution is that "preventing a suppressive effect" and "actively promoting growth" are mechanistically distinct claims, and their negative result does not necessarily overturn Picherit's rescue finding — though it does undercut any strong claim that AR activation is a general positive driver of growth-plate chondrogenesis.

> [!contradiction] This is a rare case in the vault where two primary sources on the same topic directly engage each other's findings, rather than the tension being an artifact of the wiki's own synthesis. See [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] for how this is reconciled alongside the broader androgen-vs-estrogen bone literature.
