# Dietary Supplement Effects on DHT, Testosterone, and Estradiol (Angwafor & Anderson 2008)

An open-label (no placebo control), industry-funded dose-response study testing a proprietary combination supplement (astaxanthin + saw palmetto lipid extract, marketed as Alphastat/Mytosterone) in 42 healthy men aged 37-70, on the premise that this combination inhibits both 5α-reductase (which converts testosterone to DHT) and aromatase (which converts testosterone to estradiol) simultaneously — based on prior in vitro work from the same commercial funder.

Both the 800 mg/day and 2000 mg/day dose groups showed significant serum testosterone increases and DHT decreases within 3 days of starting treatment, with no significant difference between the two doses on these two measures — suggesting a ceiling effect at the lower dose. Estradiol, however, decreased significantly only in the higher (2000 mg/day) dose group, in a clearly dose-dependent pattern. The authors frame this as evidence the supplement achieves simultaneous inhibition of both enzymes in vivo, potentially useful for age-related testosterone decline (sarcopenia/andropause) without the gynecomastia risk associated with prescription 5α-reductase inhibitors (which typically raise estradiol as a side effect).

> [!gap] This study has substantial methodological limitations: no placebo/control group, no blinding, funded directly by the supplement manufacturer (Triarco Industries) with one author being the company's Director of R&D, and only 14 days of follow-up — far too short to assess any clinically meaningful outcome (muscle mass, strength, symptoms) beyond the biochemical hormone-level changes themselves.
