Somatropin. Why the high arm was the right arm.

[cover card]

Mauras randomised GH-deficient adolescents to 0.3 or 0.7 mg/kg per week, given as daily subcutaneous injections, and followed them to near-adult height. Note what 0.7 per week actually is when you divide it by seven: 0.1 mg/kg per day.

[the trial design]

The high dose group were significantly taller at near-adult height by 4.6 cm, P < 0.001, n = 75, 95% CI 2.6 to 6.5. First-year growth rate was 9.8 cm/yr against 8.2 on standard dose, P = 0.001.

[the primary result]

The main argument against pushing the dose is that you spend your plates faster. Nwosu followed 71 children up to 14 years: BA minus CA was -1.05 at start and -0.69 at end, P = 0.09.

[the objection that fails]

Lundberg randomised children to 33 or 67 µg/kg/day and found pubertal height gain and adult height SDS greater on the high dose. Then their own conclusion: the high dose was set at 67 even though the present results suggest that nearly 50% of the children could have benefitted from an even higher dose.

[the trialists' own words]

The high arm won on adult height by 4.6 cm, won by more the longer it ran, did not advance bone age in either study that checked, and produced no carbohydrate adverse event in either trial. The trialists themselves said half their patients needed more.

[takeaway card]

High dose, bedtime, subcutaneous. Watch free T4 and insulin.
