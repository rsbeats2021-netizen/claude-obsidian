# Keenan et al. 1987 — DHT Heptanoate PK

*J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 64(3):557–62. PMID 3546348. DOI 10.1210/jcem-64-3-557. **Full text paywalled; dose not stated in the abstract.** The formulation is in [[Keenan-1990-DHT-Heptanoate-Patent-US4956357]] (190 mg/mL in sesame oil).

## Dosing as reported

Men and pubertal boys, IM. Chronic administration to pubertal boys **at 3-week intervals for 15 weeks**. The per-injection mg dose is not in the abstract.

## Pharmacokinetics

- Plasma DHT rose markedly by **24 h**, peaked **during the first week**, returned to baseline at **4–6 weeks**.
- **43–55% of DHT-hp was converted to DHT** by 4–6 weeks post-injection.
- Plasma testosterone, estradiol, LH and FSH all fell by **day 4**, were lowest in the **second week**, back to baseline at **4–6 weeks**.
- LH and FSH responses to GnRH were **diminished** by chronic q3wk dosing over 15 weeks.

## Receptor binding — why the ester is inert

- DHT-hp's affinity for the human androgen receptor is **100× lower** than DHT's.
- **No** demonstrable affinity for the estrogen receptor in breast tissue.
- Conclusion: unesterified DHT is the active principle; the heptanoate moiety is a depot device only.

Because DHT is non-aromatisable and neither DHT nor DHT-hp binds the ER, the authors attribute LH/FSH suppression to an **androgen-dependent** mechanism — the central argument for using DHT-hp as a hormonal probe.
