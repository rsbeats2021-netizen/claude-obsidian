# Source: Tualang Honey on Bone Metabolism of Postmenopausal Women (Mohd Effendy et al. 2012)

**Type**: Review article (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2012)

## Summary

Reviews the biological rationale for using Tualang honey (a wild Malaysian multi-floral honey) as an alternative to hormone replacement therapy (HRT) for postmenopausal osteoporosis, given HRT's breast-cancer and cardiovascular risks. Focuses on honey's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory mechanisms as they relate to the RANKL/RANK/OPG bone-remodeling pathway.

## Key Findings

- **Mechanism 1 — antioxidant**: estrogen loss after menopause increases oxidative stress (ROS, H2O2, superoxide), which activates NF-κB and promotes osteoclast differentiation/activity while triggering osteoblast apoptosis. Tualang honey's flavonoids/phenolics (kaempferol, quercetin, ellagic acid, gallic acid, catechin) scavenge these free radicals.
- **Mechanism 2 — anti-inflammatory**: postmenopausal estrogen loss also raises pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6) that stimulate osteoclastogenesis via RANKL and suppress osteoprotegerin (OPG). Honey has been shown to reduce PGE2 and inhibit nitric oxide production, contributing anti-inflammatory action.
- **Prior evidence cited**: Zaid et al. found 2 weeks of Tualang honey increased bone density in ovariectomized rats to levels comparable to non-ovariectomized controls (see [[Tualang-Honey-Bone-Structure-Ovariectomized-Rats|Zaid et al. 2012]]); a human trial (Lily et al. 2010) found 20g/day Tualang honey for 4 months produced bone densitometry results comparable to HRT.
- **Practical note**: recommended honey dose ~20g/day (within the "sweetener" limit of 40-50g/day); honey may also help diabetic postmenopausal patients since it lowers blood glucose vs. control in a streptozotocin rat model cited here — though the same honey inhibits CYP2C8 in vitro, raising a theoretical drug-interaction concern with CYP2C8-metabolized drugs (thiazolidinediones, amodiaquine).
