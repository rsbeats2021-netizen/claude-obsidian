# Aromatase Inhibitors in Men: Effects and Therapeutic Options (de Ronde & de Jong 2011)

A comprehensive review of aromatase-inhibitor (AI) use across the full range of male indications: precocious puberty, idiopathic short stature, constitutional delay of puberty, late-onset hypogonadism (andropause), gynecomastia, obesity-related hypogonadism, and male infertility — grounding all of it in the foundational observation that estradiol, not testosterone, drives epiphyseal closure, bone mass maintenance, and gonadotropin feedback in men (established via the phenotypes of aromatase-deficient and estrogen-resistant men, who have unfused epiphyses and low bone mass despite normal-to-high androgen levels).

**Height/puberty indications** (the strongest evidence base): AIs combined with GnRH agonists or antiandrogens reliably delay epiphyseal maturation and increase predicted adult height in boys with various forms of precocious/delayed puberty and idiopathic short stature. Letrozole treatment produced a documented 5.9 cm gain in predicted adult height in one RCT of boys with idiopathic short stature.

**Andropause indication** (weaker, more equivocal): AIs raise testosterone in older hypogonadal men by relieving estrogen's negative feedback on the pituitary (increasing LH/FSH), positioning them as a potential oral alternative to testosterone injections. However, multiple studies found this testosterone rise did **not** translate into improved muscle strength, body composition, or quality-of-life — the review attributes this partly to the resulting *drop* in estradiol itself blunting expected benefits, since estradiol (not just testosterone) drives some of those outcomes.

**Safety concerns specific to boys**: vertebral deformities were observed in AI-treated boys during puberty — a finding the review flags as a genuine, unresolved safety signal not clearly present in short-term adult male studies, explaining the overall cautious "not yet recommended for routine use" conclusion despite genuine efficacy.

> [!key-insight] This review makes explicit and detailed the "estrogen resistant man / aromatase deficiency" natural-experiment evidence base that other sources in this domain (e.g., [[ERalpha-AF1-Growth-Plate-Closure-Female-Mice|Börjesson et al. 2012]]) reference more briefly — 8 documented aromatase-deficient men worldwide, all showing unfused epiphyses and low bone mass until treated with estradiol.

> [!gap] Most adult-male AI studies are small, and the review is explicit that long-term skeletal safety in both boys and adult men "remains an issue of concern" — this is a review calling for caution, not endorsing routine use.
