# Thyroid Hormone Receptor Mutations and Contrasting Skeletal Phenotypes (O'Shea et al. 2005)

This paper introduces thyroid hormone as a distinct hormonal axis affecting growth-plate biology — the first thyroid-focused source in this domain, which had previously only covered sex steroids (estrogen/androgen) and the GH-IGF-1 axis directly.

The same identical dominant-negative "PV" mutation (derived from a human resistance-to-thyroid-hormone patient) produces opposite skeletal phenotypes depending on which thyroid hormone receptor isoform it targets. Targeted to TRβ (TRβPV), it disrupts negative feedback in the pituitary — which predominantly expresses TRβ — causing systemic thyrotoxicosis (high circulating T3/T4/TSH) that then acts on bone via the still-intact TRα1, producing accelerated growth, advanced bone age, and craniosynostosis (premature skull suture fusion). Targeted instead to TRα1 (TRα1PV mice), pituitary feedback is undisturbed (normal circulating hormone levels — "biochemically euthyroid") but bone itself, where TRα1 is the dominant functional receptor, becomes locally hypothyroid: severe (15-25%) postnatal growth retardation, 3-4 week delayed endochondral ossification, reduced trabecular bone mineralization, and delayed skull suture closure.

Mechanistically, growth-plate GH receptor and IGF-I receptor mRNA expression and downstream signaling (pSTAT5, pAkt) were both reduced in TRα1PV mice but increased in TRβPV mice — establishing that thyroid hormone acts on the growth plate significantly through local modulation of GH/IGF-I receptor sensitivity, not just through effects on systemic GH production.

> [!key-insight] The core lesson generalizes beyond thyroid biology: whether a receptor-inactivating mutation produces a "toxic" or "deficient" tissue phenotype depends entirely on which receptor isoform is functionally dominant in that specific tissue (TRβ in pituitary, TRα1 in bone) — the same mutation, same protein family, opposite consequences by tissue. The paper explicitly extends this framework to heart (TRα-responsive, thyrotoxic phenotype from TRα mutation) and liver (TRβ-responsive).

> [!gap] Mouse genetic model; human thyroid hormone resistance syndromes show a broad, variable spectrum of skeletal phenotypes that this clean two-receptor mouse dichotomy likely oversimplifies.
