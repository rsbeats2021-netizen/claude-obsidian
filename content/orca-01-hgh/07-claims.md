# Claims — orca-01-hgh (high-dose case)

## THE DOSE: Mauras et al. 2000, JCEM (PMID 10843176 / verified in hgh-02 ledger)
QUOTE: "All patients were randomly assigned to continued treatment with GH at
either 0.3 mg/kg·week (standard dose) or 0.7 mg/kg·week (high dose), sc, daily."
→ 0.7 mg/kg/week delivered daily = **0.1 mg/kg/day**. The dose in question is a
tested trial arm, not an extrapolation.
QUOTE: "subjects in the high dose group were significantly taller at near-adult
height than subjects in the standard dose group by an average of 4.6 cm (n = 75;
P < 0.001; 95% confidence interval, 2.6–6.5 cm)."
QUOTE: "For subjects who received at least 4 yr of rhGH treatment, the difference
between dose groups at that time point was 5.7 cm (by ANCOVA, P = 0.024; n = 20)."
QUOTE: "The mean month 0–12 growth rate was 9.8 cm/yr in the high dose group
compared with 8.2 cm/yr in the standard dose group (P = 0.001)."

## THE OBJECTION THAT FAILS: bone age
QUOTE (Mauras): "An interesting and important finding of these studies is the
normal pace of bone maturation, with bone ages advancing approximately
1 yr/chronological yr in both the standard and high dose groups."
QUOTE (Mauras): high dose "did not increase the rate of skeletal maturation"
QUOTE (Nwosu 2021, PMID 33860132): "Bone age minus chronological age (BA-CA) did
not differ significantly between baseline and the end of rhGH therapy
(-1.05 ± 1.42 vs -0.69 ± 1.63, P = 0.09)."
→ The main argument against pushing the dose is not supported by either study.

## HIGHER STILL: Lundberg et al. 2015, BMC Endocr Disord (PMID 26341577)
`.raw/.../Growth Hormone (GH) Dose-Dependent IGF-I Response Relates to Pubertal - Lundberg et al - 2015.pdf`
QUOTE: "In this randomized GH-treatment trial, pubertal height gainSDS and AHSDS
were greater in children randomized to a high GH dose (67 μg/kg/d) than a standard
dose (33 μg/kg/d)."
QUOTE: "The high dose was set at 67 μg/kg/d even though the present results suggest
that nearly 50 % of the children could have benefitted from an even higher dose."
QUOTE: "The GH dose given during puberty did not maintain the mean prepubertal
IGF-I level, suggesting that some children may have benefitted from a higher dose
in order to undergo a pubertal growth spurt of normal magnitude"
QUOTE: 63% of adult height SDS variance explained by variables including
"'high GH dose' (+)"
QUOTE: "data show the greater the increase in IGF-I, the greater the gain in height"
→ This is the strongest single statement in the literature for dosing above the
conventional high dose, and it is the trialists' own conclusion about their own
trial.

## SAFETY AT HIGH DOSE
QUOTE (Lundberg): "In the present study no dose-dependent adverse event involving
carbohydrate metabolism was observed, which confirms previous studies with high GH
doses [15, 16, 42, 43]."
QUOTE (Mauras): "High dose rhGH was well tolerated, with a similar safety profile
as standard dose treatment and no difference in hemoglobin A1c or glucose
concentrations between groups."
→ Both trials: markers moved, adverse events did not.
COUNTERWEIGHT, kept on a slide: Mauras also found "a greater increase in fasting
insulin (P = 0.011) and C peptide (P = 0.019) in the high dose" group, and 28% vs
9% had IGF-1 above the normal range.

## TIMING
Subcutaneous bedtime dosing is the administration used in the trials. Example:
Naeraa et al. 2001, Acta Paediatr (PMID 11430712) — "GH 0.1 IU/kg/d sc at bedtime".
NOTE ON UNITS: that study's 0.1 IU/kg/d is NOT 0.1 mg/kg/d. ~3 IU ≈ 1 mg, so it is
roughly 0.033 mg/kg/d. Cited here only as evidence that bedtime sc is the standard
route and timing, not as a dose comparison.

## THE CEILING
No trial in this set tested above 0.7 mg/kg/week to an adult-height endpoint.
Lundberg's statement supports going higher than 67 µg/kg/d; it does not establish
an optimum, and the deck says so.
