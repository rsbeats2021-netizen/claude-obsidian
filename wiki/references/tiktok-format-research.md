# TikTok Format Research — Short-Form Science Content

Researched 2026-09-03. Platform behaviour changes fast; re-check before
committing to a format shift.

## Headline finding: carousels beat video right now

Photo Mode (carousel/slideshow) is reportedly pulling **~5x the reach** of
regular video for the same creators, with independent analysis finding **81%
higher engagement and 82% more likes** than comparable video posts.

Three reasons, and they compound:
1. **Less saturated.** Most creators still default to video.
2. **Swipe = engagement signal.** Every swipe tells the algorithm the viewer is
   actively engaged. Video has no equivalent per-second confirmation.
3. **Saves.** Carousels get saved at higher rates, and saves are a strong
   ranking signal.

Meanwhile science content views are up **~300% YoY**, and TikTok has become a
primary search surface for Gen Z — nearly half use it instead of Google.

**Strategic read:** slideshow-first, video second. That's the opposite of where
most effort usually goes.

## What the carousel algorithm actually measures

- **Swipe-through rate** — how far through the deck people get
- **Dwell time per slide**
- **Reverse swipes** — going *back* to re-read a slide

## Correction to earlier guidance

Generic TikTok advice says 15–25 words per slide. **For this niche that is
wrong**, and the reverse-swipe signal is why: a dense slide that makes someone
swipe back to re-read is sending a *positive* ranking signal, not a negative
one. Mechanism density also does double duty as the credibility marker that
separates rigorous content from supplement-marketing content.

Revised guidance:
- **Hook slide: sparse.** It competes with autoplaying video for the scroll-stop.
- **Body slides: dense is fine, even good.** 60–120 words works. Keep the
  bold-label-then-explanation cadence.
- **Every slide needs a reason to swipe** — an open loop, a "but", a number
  that isn't explained until the next slide.
- **Build for saves.** A deck someone wants to keep as reference outperforms a
  deck they merely agree with.

## Platform policy — real account risk

TikTok's health rules, as of the Sept 2026 guidelines update:

- Health claims must be **supported by recognised studies**; unproven health
  claims are prohibited.
- **Prescription drugs, steroids, and unlicensed supplements** are restricted
  categories.
- Enforcement escalates video removal → suspension → permanent ban, and
  **severe violations can mean immediate permanent removal with no prior
  warning**. Fact-checking partnerships expanded in 2026.

Practical consequence: cited-mechanism and myth-teardown content sits safely
inside policy. Dosing/protocol content is the category that carries
build-it-then-lose-it risk — losing an account also loses the audience that
took years to build.

## Format spec

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Aspect | 9:16, 1080x1920 |
| Deck length | 8–12 slides (swipe-through completion matters) |
| Text size | 60pt-equivalent minimum |
| Slide 1 | Minimal words, strong visual |
| Video length | 30–60s for reach; longer only when the payoff needs it |
