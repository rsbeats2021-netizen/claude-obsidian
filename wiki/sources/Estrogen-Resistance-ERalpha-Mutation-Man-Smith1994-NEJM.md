# The Foundational Human Case: ERα Mutation Causes Estrogen Resistance in a Man (Smith et al. 1994, NEJM)

This is very likely the single most foundational paper underlying this domain's entire estrogen-vs-androgen debate: the first documented human case of a disruptive estrogen-receptor gene mutation. A 28-year-old man, homozygous for a premature-stop-codon mutation in exon 2 of the ERα gene (his parents were heterozygous carriers, with consanguinity in the family), presented with tall stature (204 cm), incomplete/unfused epiphyses, and continuing linear growth into adulthood — despite completely normal, even slightly elevated, testosterone and normal pubertal masculinization (axillary/pubic hair, regular shaving). Serum estradiol and estrone were elevated (consistent with a receptor-level block rather than a ligand-production problem), and six months of transdermal estrogen treatment, despite a 10-fold rise in free estradiol, produced no detectable clinical response whatsoever — no epiphyseal fusion, no bone density improvement.

> [!key-insight] This is the cleanest possible human evidence separating "androgen's bone effect works through the estrogen receptor" from "it's really the androgen receptor": here the estrogen receptor itself is non-functional while androgen levels and the androgen receptor are entirely normal, and the growth plate simply never closes. Every source in [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health|this domain's "it's really estrogen" camp]] is, in effect, standing on the shoulders of this single case report — it is the direct human proof-of-concept that later aromatase-inhibitor trials, 5α-reductase-inhibitor studies, and animal ERα-knockout work all elaborate on.

> [!gap] A single case report (n=1) — extraordinarily rare mutation, so statistical generalization is inherently limited. The paper's own framing draws heavily on a companion mouse ERα-knockout study (cited in its introduction) for confirmation that estrogen-receptor loss is viable, meaning the human and animal literatures were mutually reinforcing from the very beginning of this domain's estrogen-receptor research program, not developed independently.
