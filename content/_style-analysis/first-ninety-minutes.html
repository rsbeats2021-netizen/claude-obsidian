03MECHANISM

How GH is actually released

It's pulsatile, not continuous.

Growth hormone isn't a steady drip. It's released in discrete bursts across the day, and in healthy adults the largest share of 24-hour output arrives in pulses locked to slow-wave sleep.

The dominant pulse of the night lands shortly after you fall asleep, riding your first block of N3.

05THE TWIST

Everyone gets this backwards

Losing the end of your night is not the expensive part.

Deep sleep is front-loaded; REM stacks toward morning. So an alarm that steals 06:00–08:00 mostly costs you REM.

A fragmented, alcohol-soaked, scrolling 23:00–01:00 costs you the pulse itself, and you can't make it up by sleeping in.

FIX THE FRONT OF THE NIGHT

06WHAT BLUNTS IT

Four things that flatten the first pulse

  • AlcoholSuppresses slow-wave sleep and fragments the back half, even when you "slept through".
  • A big meal near bedElevated glucose and insulin blunt GH secretion. Give it ~3 hours.
  • Bright light lateDelays melatonin onset, pushing sleep onset and the whole architecture later.
  • Irregular bedtimeThe pulse is tied to sleep onset. A moving onset is a moving pulse.

ALL FOUR ARE FREE TO FIX

07PROTOCOL

Tonight, free

The five-step version

  1. Same wake time daily, weekends included.
  2. Last real meal ~3h before lights out.
  3. Dry nights when you're actually optimizing.
  4. Dim everything 60–90 min before bed.
  5. Cool, dark, boring room. 18–19°C.
