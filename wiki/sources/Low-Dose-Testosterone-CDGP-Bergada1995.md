# Low-Dose Testosterone for CDGP: 20-Month Follow-Up (Bergadá et al. 1995)

A rigorous, long-follow-up complement to [[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]'s testosterone-undecanoate findings in this domain: low-dose testosterone enanthate (monthly IM injection) in boys with constitutional delay of growth and puberty produced a substantially earlier and larger growth spurt (10.1 vs. 4.0 cm/yr) without impairing predicted adult height, despite an initial bone-age acceleration in both treated and control groups.

> [!contradiction] This finding — accelerated growth WITHOUT a final-height cost — sits in tension with this domain's other anabolic-androgen sources, most notably [[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]'s oxandrolone arm and [[Oxandrolone-Pediatric-Growth-Disorders-Review|Soliman et al. 2025]], both of which flag skeletal-maturation acceleration as a real tradeoff against final height. The difference may reflect dose (this study used low-dose testosterone specifically, versus the higher relative anabolic potency of oxandrolone), population (CDGP boys who are constitutionally delayed, versus familial-short-stature or Turner-syndrome populations), or genuine drug-specific pharmacology — this domain doesn't yet have a source directly reconciling the two patterns.

> [!gap] Non-randomized comparison to a control group (not stated as randomized); testicular-volume delay, while reported as reversible, wasn't followed long enough to confirm no lasting reproductive effect.

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a fifth data point to [[Testosterone]]'s anabolic-androgen risk-benefit catalog, and specifically complicates the emerging "growth now, skeletal maturation cost later" pattern established by [[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]] — this paper found accelerated growth without that cost, at least for low-dose testosterone specifically in CDGP.
