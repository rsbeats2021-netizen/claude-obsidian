# Source: Tualang Honey on Bone Structure in Ovariectomized Rats (Zaid et al. 2012)

**Type**: Original animal study, 40 female Sprague-Dawley rats, 5 groups (Clinics, 2012)

## Summary

Directly compares Tualang honey (0.2 g/kg body weight) against 1% calcium supplementation for preventing trabecular bone loss in ovariectomized rats — the standard animal model for postmenopausal osteoporosis. This is the primary study cited by both bone-health reviews already in this vault ([[Tualang-Honey-Bone-Metabolism-Postmenopausal-Review]] and [[Honey-Bone-Health-Review-2019]]).

## Key Findings

- **Trabecular structure** (histomorphometry of distal femur): the Tualang honey group (TH) had significantly higher bone volume/tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular thickness, and trabecular number, and significantly lower inter-trabecular space, compared to the untreated ovariectomized (OVX) control group.
- **Honey vs. calcium**: TH group's trabecular thickness was *higher* and inter-trabecular space *narrower* than the calcium-supplemented (PC) group — i.e., honey outperformed calcium on these specific structural parameters in this model.
- **Body weight/food intake**: no adverse effect from honey; TH rats' weight changes were similar to other OVX groups.
- **Proposed mechanism**: honey's flavonols (quercetin, kaempferol) may interact with estrogen receptors (particularly ER-β, more abundant in trabecular than cortical bone) to inhibit osteoclast activity; honey's gluconic acid may also enhance intestinal calcium absorption more effectively than direct calcium supplementation.

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] Animal model only (6-week duration); histomorphometric endpoints, not whole-bone density (DXA/pQCT). This is the foundational primary study behind claims repeated across [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] — later reviews cite this same dataset rather than adding independent confirmation, so its findings carry more collective weight than a typical single study but are still one experiment from one lab.
