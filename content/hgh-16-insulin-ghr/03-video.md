Your liver cannot hear GH. This is why the same dose gives different numbers.

[cover card]

So in the IGF-1 ceiling post we saw that the same GH dose produced IGF-1 all over the range, with the dose ranges for the high and low IGF-1 groups overlapping almost completely at 0.01 to 0.10 mg/kg/day. That spread has a mechanism and it is not genetics or luck or measurement error.

[a few posts ago]

IGFBP-1, the binding protein that takes IGF-1 out of circulation, rose from 32 to 205 ng/mL, a 6.4-fold increase. And free IGF-1 fell below the detection limit in 15 of the 17 patients.

[type 1 diabetes]

A single dose of insulin, 0.2 units per kilogram. Two hours after that, free IGF-1 had rebounded to 0.54 ng/mL from undetectable, against a baseline of 0.72.

[two hours later]

Switching patients from subcutaneous insulin to continuous intraperitoneal delivery, which sends insulin to the liver first the way the pancreas does, dropped IGFBP-1 by 100.7 µg/L, p < 0.01. And there was no meaningful relationship between the change in HbA1c and the change in IGFBP-1, r = -0.3, p = 0.33.

[the route result]

Your serum IGF-1 is simultaneously reporting on your GH dose, your hepatic insulin exposure, the route anything you take reaches the liver by, your body composition, and your pubertal stage. It is a composite of at least five inputs.

[takeaway card]

IGF-1 is a composite, not a GH readout.
