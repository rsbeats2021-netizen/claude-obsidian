# GH/IGF-1 Reverses Age-Related Bone Marrow and Thymus Hypocellularity (Kelley et al. 1996)

Extends this domain's GH/IGF-1 axis literature to hematopoiesis (blood cell production in bone marrow), a tissue context distinct from growth-plate cartilage: GH implantation or infusion dramatically restores bone-marrow and thymus cellularity in aged, hypophysectomized, or dwarf rodents, and locally-produced (macrophage-derived) IGF-1 — induced by CSF-1 and IL-3 during myeloid differentiation — both increases hematopoietic progenitor proliferation and cuts their apoptotic death rate roughly in half.

## Relevance to This Domain

Extends [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]] to a non-skeletal tissue (bone marrow hematopoiesis), complementing [[GH-IGF1-Longevity-Review|this domain's GH/IGF-1-longevity review]] with a specific mechanism (locally-produced IGF-1 as a hematopoietic-progenitor survival factor) for how GH/IGF-1 signaling affects aging tissue beyond bone growth itself.
