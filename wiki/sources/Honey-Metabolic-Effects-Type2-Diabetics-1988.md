# Source: Metabolic Effects of Honey in Type II Diabetics (Katsilambros et al. 1988)

**Type**: Original clinical study, 2 protocols (Acta Diabetologica Latina, 1988) — one of the earliest sources in this domain

## Summary

Early (1988) human study testing whether honey is a suitable sweetener for type II diabetics compared to bread, and whether combining honey with fat/protein-rich foods changes its glycemic impact. Protocol A: 12 diabetics received isoglucidic honey (33g) vs. bread (50g) meals. Protocol B: 19 diabetics received honey alone, honey+almonds, or honey+cheese/butter/bread meals.

## Key Findings

- **Honey vs. bread**: areas under the blood-glucose curve were statistically equal (bread 29,607 vs honey 29,214 mg·min); honey caused higher glucose at 30 min (p<0.01) but lower at 90 min (p<0.05) — an earlier, sharper, but not larger overall response.
- **Honey + fat/protein**: adding almonds or butter/cheese/bread to honey did not increase total postprandial hyperglycemia versus honey alone, but did produce higher serum triglycerides and insulin (fat slows/flattens the glucose curve).
- **Conclusion (authors')**: honey and bread produce similar overall hyperglycemia in type II diabetics; combining honey with fat-rich foods doesn't worsen glycemic response but does raise lipid/insulin levels.

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] Very small samples (n=12, n=19), 1988-era methodology (no modern statistical corrections reported), single acute-meal design — flagged `confidence: low`. Useful mainly as an early historical data point that [[Honey Hypoglycemic Mechanism]] research has built on since (compare with the more rigorous [[Honey-vs-Glucose-Fructose-Solution|Münstedt et al. 2008]] crossover trial).
