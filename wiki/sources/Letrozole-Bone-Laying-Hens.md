# Letrozole (Aromatase Inhibitor) Increases Bone Strength in Laying Hens

This poultry-science study found that letrozole, an aromatase inhibitor that reduces circulating estrogen, *increased* structural bone mineral density and mechanical strength in laying hens — the opposite of what aromatase inhibition typically does to bone in mammals.

The explanation offered is specific to avian reproductive physiology: laying hens maintain a specialized estrogen-dependent tissue called medullary bone, a calcium reservoir that is resorbed to supply calcium for eggshell formation during the laying cycle. This process competes with and can deplete structural (cortical/trabecular) bone. By suppressing estrogen, letrozole reduces the hen's investment in medullary bone turnover, leaving more calcium available for structural bone — a mechanism with no real analogue in non-egg-laying mammals.

> [!gap] Single-species poultry study; the medullary-bone mechanism proposed is specific to oviparous (egg-laying) reproductive biology and should not be generalized to mammalian bone physiology.
