01Week 1
Foundation

Fix the spine before you pour anything in

Every later workstream writes into this structure. Getting it wrong now means re-filing thousands of pages later, and you already have 52 unverified duplicates as a preview of that pain.

Three additions to the folder scheme

wiki/
  concepts/        23 → this is what should grow
  entities/        compounds, hormones, proteins, people
  sources/         398 · one page per study, stays a receipt
  comparisons/     9 · X vs Y, already working well
  pathways/        NEW one page per signalling cascade
  quotes/          NEW verbatim extracts, filed by topic
  figures/         NEW study images, filed by what they show
  claims/          NEW one page per contested claim
  creators/        NEW what each account has asserted
  decks/           NEW one page per post you publish

One canonical ID, everywhere

Use the DOI as the filename stem for every study, with a readable slug after it. Your duplicate problem exists because filenames came from whatever the download was called. A DOI-keyed name makes a duplicate impossible to create by accident and lets any page cite a study by an address rather than a title.

wiki/sources/10.1038-s41586-023-06611-6--haemoglobin-body-chondrocyte.md

Addition — provenance tiers

This is the one I would not skip. You are about to mix peer-reviewed papers with claims scraped off TikTok in the same vault. If those look alike in six months, the vault is worse than useless, because you will cite a creator's guess as though it were a finding.

Put a evidence_tier on every claim-bearing page and never let it be blank:

TierMeansCan it go on a slide?
AHuman RCT or meta-analysisYes, state it plainly
BHuman observational / cohortYes, with the design named
CAnimal or in vitroOnly if labelled as such on the slide
DMechanistic inference — plausible, untestedOnly as an open question
XCreator assertion from the scrapeNever as fact. It is data about the niche.
02Week 1–2
Accounts

Split the accounts by audience, not by topic

Topics that share an audience belong together. Topics that don't will drag each other's distribution down, because the algorithm is learning who to show you to and mixed signals slow that down.

  • Keep harm reduction separate. Different risk profile, different moderation exposure, and you do not want a takedown on that account to cost you the other three.
  • Sleep and peptides are open ground. Sleep is covered by 2 of 13 accounts; peptides by 3 and badly. Both are cheap to own.

Warming up, the version that actually works

Warm-up here means giving each account a coherent identity before it starts publishing, so the recommender has something to work with. It does not mean engagement pods, bought interaction, or anything that fakes a signal — those get accounts limited, and on a health-adjacent topic that limitation tends to be permanent.

  • Days 1–7: use the account like a person. Watch the niche, follow 20–40 relevant accounts, comment where you have something real to say. No posting.
  • Days 8–14: post 3–4 low-stakes pieces on the account's single topic. Consistency of subject matters more than quality here; you are teaching the classifier.
  • Day 15 on: normal cadence. Now the deck library earns its keep.
  • One topic per account, visibly. Bio, first three posts and pinned post should all say the same thing about what this account is.
03Week 2–4
Ingest

Four pipelines, run in this order

Each one feeds the next. Running them out of order means synthesising from an incomplete base and redoing it.

Pipeline AFinish the study backlog. 74 unsorted files, 52 of them duplicates. Dedupe by DOI first, then ingest the real papers into sources/.
Pipeline BSlideshows → creator pages. 2,353 OCR'd slides you already have. Extract every claim, tier it X, file under creators/.
Pipeline CVideos → transcripts. 1,445 mp4s, none transcribed. This is your largest untapped input by far.
Pipeline DAcquire what's missing. Once B and C name the compounds and pathways, go find the papers that actually cover them.

B and C: what a creator page should be

You asked for a well-written paper per video. The useful shape is not one document per video — it is one page per topic, assembled from every video that touched it, with the overlaps preserved rather than merged away. That is exactly the case where two creators disagreeing becomes visible, and disagreement is your best content.

---
type: creator-claim
topic: "Growth plate hypoxia"
evidence_tier: X            # creator assertion, never fact
creators:
  - acct: _sox9
    posts: ["7631636275824430368"]
    position: "Hypoxia is beneficial; breath-holding may extend growth"
    views: 149300
  - acct: rpo3212
    posts: ["7592703587365555464"]
    position: "ATP limitation is the constraint; hypoxic/hyperoxic training"
    views: 304900
overlap_with: ["[[Hypoxia and the Growth Plate]]"]
verified_against: ["10.1038-s41586-023-06611-6"]
verdict: "Mechanism sound. Intervention claim unsupported."
---

Addition — the disagreement index

Once B and C are done you will have, for the first time, a queryable record of what this entire niche believes and where it splits. Nobody else has that. Two things fall out of it for free: a list of claims where the top accounts contradict each other, which is a content pipeline on its own, and a list of claims everybody repeats that nobody has ever cited — which is the more interesting list.

04Week 3–6
Synthesis

The enumerated review pages

This is the "every growth factor, with footnotes" format you described, and it is the single highest-value thing in the plan. It is also where the 398→23 ratio gets fixed.

Three page types, each with a fixed skeleton so they stay comparable and so I can generate and extend them without asking you what shape to use.

Page typeFolderFixed sections
Compound monograph entities/ Identity & class · Mechanism · What it does in humans · Dose used in trials · Kinetics · Adverse effects · Interactions · Evidence tier per claim · Open questions · Sources
Pathway page pathways/ One-line summary · Upstream inputs · Node-by-node cascade · Downstream effects · Cross-talk · Where it is measured · Known modulators (with tiers) · Sources
Enumerated review concepts/ Scope · A numbered entry per member of the family · Per entry: what it is, where it acts, what the evidence is, footnote to source · Comparison table · Gaps

Concrete first targets, chosen because you already hold the sources and they anchor the biggest accounts: Growth factors of the growth plate (IGF-1, FGF/FGFR3, PTHrP, IHH, BMPs, VEGF, TGF-β, CNP), Androgen pathway end to end, The GH/IGF-1 axis, Hair follicle cycling, Photoageing.

Addition — every claim carries its footnote inline

Write the footnote as a wikilink to the source page plus the exact quote ID, not as a bare citation. Then a broken link is a broken claim, and wiki-lint will find it for you. That is what makes this a web rather than a filing cabinet.

05Ongoing
Quotes

The quote library

One folder, one file per topic, verbatim extracts only. This is the layer that makes a deck defensible in ten minutes instead of an afternoon.

wiki/quotes/growth-plate-hypoxia.md

> "Massive cell death occurred in the inner zones of the
> developing growth plates."
  — Zhang F et al., Nature 2023 · [[10.1038-s41586-023-06611-6]]
  tier: C (mouse knockout) · fig: [[fig-2023-zhang-04]]
  used_in: [[deck-no-blood-supply]] slide 08
  • Verbatim or nothing. A paraphrase in the quote library defeats its purpose. Paraphrase belongs on the concept page.
  • Every quote gets a tier and a figure link if the paper has one that shows it.
  • Every quote records where you used it. When a paper is retracted or superseded you can find every slide that leaned on it.
06Week 6+
Figures

The figure library, filed by what it displays

You said organise by what they show, and that is the right axis — you will search for "a dose-response curve I can put on slide 4", never for "figure 3 of the Zhang paper".

Display typeWhat you reach for it for
mechanism-schematicA pathway slide. The most reusable class you will own.
dose-responseAny "more is not better" argument.
time-courseOnset, decay, recovery, cycles.
histology / micrographTissue-level claims. High credibility per pixel.
anatomicalWhere a thing physically is.
comparison-barsGroup A vs group B outcomes.
survival / KM curveLong-run risk.
forest plotMeta-analysis. The strongest single image in an argument.
blot / gel / flowMolecular evidence. Looks technical, rarely reads on a phone.

Addition — describe each figure in words when you file it

Store a one-line shows: field written as you would say it out loud: "chondrocyte death concentrated in the central zone after HIF-1α deletion". That line is what makes the library searchable by me, and it doubles as the alt text and the slide caption when you use it.

On reuse: file the figure with its licence status. Original journal figures are the author's or publisher's; most are not yours to republish on a slide. In practice the library's job is to tell you what to draw — you have a renderer that makes better slide graphics than a screenshotted PDF panel anyway.

07Continuous
Output

Vault to deck, in one chain

The point of all of it. Every slide should be traceable backwards without you remembering anything.

Step 1Pick a claim from claims/ that is tier A–C and has an unused quote.
Step 2Pull the quote and the figure spec it points to.
Step 3Draw the graphic from the figure's shows: line, in the account's style.
Step 4Render and post, then write the deck page back into decks/ with the claim IDs it used.

Step 4 is what closes the loop: the deck page updates every quote's used_in, so the vault always knows what you have already said, on which account, and on what evidence. That kills accidental repetition across four accounts, which is otherwise inevitable.

Addition — the receipts chain, and what it is worth

slide → claim → quote → figure → DOI. When someone argues with you in the comments, you answer in under a minute with the exact sentence and the exact study. Your corpus analysis says only 17% of claims in this niche cite anything at all. Being the account that can always produce the receipt is a defensible position, and this chain is what makes it cheap enough to sustain.

08Guardrails

Failure modes worth designing against

Every one of these has already happened to a vault this size. Cheap to prevent now, expensive later.

  • The dumping ground. 398 sources and 23 concepts is the early stage of it. Rule: no ingest session ends without at least one concept page created or extended. Collecting without synthesising feels productive and compounds nothing.
  • Tier drift. A mouse study quoted often enough starts to feel like a human finding. The tier field on every claim is the fix, and it only works if it is never blank.
  • Orphan sprawl. Run wiki-lint every 10–15 ingests, not every 100. Orphans are trivial to fix the week they appear.
  • Silent supersession. A 2011 paper you built three decks on gets overturned in 2024 and nothing tells you. The used_in field is the tripwire.
  • Cross-account repetition. Four accounts, one brain, same three favourite facts. The decks/ ledger is the only reliable guard.
  • Transcription debt. 1,445 videos is a weekend of compute and a month of manual. Batch it once, properly, rather than dipping in.
