# DHT Prevents Glucocorticoid Effects on Fetal Rat Metatarsal Bone

Picherit et al. (2000) cultured fetal rat metatarsal bones ex vivo and treated them with dexamethasone (a glucocorticoid known to suppress bone growth and mineralization) with or without co-administered DHT. DHT co-treatment prevented dexamethasone's suppressive effects on both longitudinal growth and mineral apposition; DHT alone modestly stimulated growth even without glucocorticoid present.

Because DHT is the one androgen that cannot be converted to estrogen by aromatase, this is presented as direct evidence of an androgen-receptor-mediated, non-aromatization-dependent effect on growth-plate/bone tissue — a finding that later became a specific point of contention (see [[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]], which used a very similar culture system and explicitly could not replicate a *growth-promoting* AR effect, though they were testing longitudinal growth rate specifically rather than rescue from glucocorticoid suppression).

> [!gap] This is an ex vivo organ-culture result in fetal rat tissue; whether the same direct AR mechanism operates in postnatal or human growth plates is not established here.

## Relevance to This Domain

Directly bears on the [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] comparison — this is one of the few sources in the vault showing a *positive, direct* androgen effect on bone/cartilage tissue not mediated by aromatization, and it is now in explicit tension with [[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]], which cites this paper directly and reaches a different conclusion in a similar model system.
