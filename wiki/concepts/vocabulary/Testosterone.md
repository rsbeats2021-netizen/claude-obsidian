# Testosterone

Testosterone is the parent androgen from which both of the domain's two "isolating" tools are derived: aromatase converts it to estrogen (isolating the estrogenic pathway), while 5α-reductase converts it to DHT (isolating the non-aromatizable androgenic pathway). Nearly every mechanistic paper in this domain uses testosterone as the baseline against which a modified or blocked pathway is compared — it is the most-mentioned single molecule in the vault's Endocrinology domain (9 of 17 on-topic sources).

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]]** — tested testosterone directly (alongside the non-aromatizable oxandrolone) in cultured fetal rat bone and found no effect on baseline longitudinal growth rate.
- **[[Dihydrotestosterone-Clinical-Implications|Swerdloff et al. 2017]]** — testosterone replacement therapy (with or without a 5α-reductase inhibitor blocking its conversion to DHT) increased lean mass and bone mineral density in hypogonadal men, attributed to aromatization to estradiol rather than to DHT.
- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Upregulation-Osteoblasts|Wiren et al. 1999]]** — testosterone matched DHT's effect in up-regulating androgen receptor levels in human osteoblastic cell lines (estradiol did not), confirming the effect is androgen-specific rather than a generic steroid response.
- **[[Hormones-and-Antioxidant-Systems|Mancini et al. 2010]]** — testosterone shows *prooxidant* effects in vascular and prostate tissue but *protective, antioxidant-enzyme-boosting* effects in neuronal cell models — a tissue-dependent duality distinct from its bone/growth-plate role covered elsewhere in this domain.
- **[[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]]** — uses DHT rather than testosterone specifically to rule out any aromatization-mediated contribution, using testosterone's non-aromatizable metabolite as the experimental tool.
- **[[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]]** — testosterone (along with DHT) directly stimulates chondrocyte proliferation and proteoglycan synthesis in vitro, but the paper attributes testosterone's role in growth-plate fusion specifically to its aromatization to estrogen.
- **[[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]** — testosterone is the biosynthetic precursor to daidzein-derived S-equol's target, DHT; equol's anti-androgen action works by binding DHT, one step downstream of testosterone.
- **[[Sex-Steroid-Regulation-Cartilage-Bone-Proliferation|Somjen et al. 1991]]** and **[[Testosterone-Metabolism-Rabbit-Cartilage|Takahashi et al. 1984]]** — both study testosterone (and its local conversion to DHT and other metabolites) directly in cartilage/bone cell culture systems, the biochemical foundation for the domain's direct-vs-aromatized androgen debate.
- **[[Testosterone-Dose-Age-Conversion-Estradiol-DHT-Lakshman2010|Lakshman et al. 2010]]** — a foundational human pharmacokinetic study: both injected testosterone dose and patient age affect the proportion converted to estradiol (via aromatase) versus DHT (via 5α-reductase), showing this conversion ratio is not fixed but varies systematically — a parameter many of this domain's other mechanism papers implicitly hold constant.
- **[[IGF1-IGFBP-Diaphragm-Nandrolone-Lewis2002|Lewis et al. 2002]]** and **[[Nandrolone-Cell-Cycle-Rat-Soleus-Muscle-McClung2005|McClung et al. 2005]]** — map nandrolone's anabolic mechanism across two distinct skeletal muscle types (diaphragm, soleus), via IGF-I/IGFBP signaling and cell-cycle regulation respectively.
- **[[Nandrolone-Craniofacial-Growth-Rats-Fischman|Fischman et al.]]**, **[[Anabolic-Steroids-Mandibular-Growth-Rats-Gebhardt|Gebhardt & Pancherz]]**, and **[[Anabolic-Steroids-Craniofacial-Growth-Rat-BarrettHarris1993|Barrett & Harris 1993]]** — establish nandrolone's craniofacial growth effects across three specific sites (cranial vault/skull length, mandible, and whole craniofacial complex), with Barrett & Harris additionally showing abuser-level doses distort facial shape rather than merely enlarging it proportionately. See [[Anabolic Steroid Effects Across Craniofacial Sites]] for the full four-study comparison including TMJ.

- **[[Trenbolone-Extremity-Bones-Running-Rats|Sarı & Lök 2019]]** — trenbolone, a potent non-testosterone anabolic-androgenic steroid, numerically (but not statistically significantly) shortened femur/humerus length in young rats; the paper's conclusion that this indicates early epiphyseal closure outruns its own statistics, illustrating a broader pattern of mixed/inconsistent anabolic-steroid-and-bone findings in the cited literature.

- **[[Nandrolone-Telomere-Elongation-Bone-Marrow-Failure|Clé et al.]]** — nandrolone decanoate (an anabolic androgen) elongated telomeres and improved hematologic outcomes in 77% and 63% of telomeropathy patients respectively over 2 years — a clinical androgen application entirely outside this domain's usual bone/growth-plate focus (bone marrow/telomere biology instead).
- **[[11-Oxygenated-Androgens-PCOS-Hirsutism-Thesis|Varausová 2023]]** — reviews 11-oxygenated androgens (11β-hydroxy-androstenedione-derived testosterone/DHT variants), a less-classical, adrenal-origin potent-androgen pathway relevant to PCOS/hirsutism, distinct from the classical gonadal testosterone-to-DHT route covered by [[5-Alpha-Reductase]].

- **[[Dietary-Supplement-DHT-Testosterone-Estradiol-Dose-Response|Angwafor & Anderson 2008]]** — a commercially-funded, weakly-controlled supplement study reporting simultaneous testosterone increases with DHT and (at higher dose) estradiol decreases.
- **[[Testosterone-Liver-Kidney-Function-Rabbits|Elesawi et al. 2019]]** — supraphysiological testosterone doses (comparable to athletic misuse levels) caused dose-dependent liver enzyme and kidney marker elevations in rabbits, an organ-toxicity angle distinct from this domain's usual bone/receptor focus.

- **[[Nandrolone-Achondroplasia-Case-Report|Al-Mosawi 2006]]** and **[[Deca-Durabolin-Reproductive-Organ-Damage-Rats|Zabiba 2017]]** sketch opposite ends of the same drug's (nandrolone/Deca-Durabolin) risk-benefit spectrum: a single case report of dramatic short-term growth benefit at low therapeutic-range dosing in a child with achondroplasia, versus severe testicular/epididymal damage from high-dose, prolonged abuse-pattern dosing in rats — alongside [[Nandrolone-Telomere-Elongation-Bone-Marrow-Failure|Clé et al.]]'s beneficial telomere-elongation findings at yet another dose/context, these three sources together sketch a genuinely dose- and context-dependent risk/benefit profile for the same molecule.
- **[[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]** — a controlled clinical trial adds a fourth data point to this risk-benefit spectrum: the anabolic androgen oxandrolone (alone or combined with rhGH) accelerated short-term growth in peri-pubertal boys more than rhGH alone, but also caused earlier puberty onset and faster skeletal maturation — the clearest controlled (not single-case) clinical evidence in this domain that anabolic-androgen growth promotion trades speed now for earlier growth-plate closure later. A third study in the same thesis found oral testosterone undecanoate matched rhGH exactly for growth outcomes in boys with puberty delay, with no added benefit from combining them.

- **[[DHT-Estrogen-Combined-Bone-Density-Growth-Ovariectomized-Rats|Coxam et al. 1996]]** — a whole-animal (OVX rat) demonstration that DHT and estrogen are simultaneously synergistic on some bone parameters (mineral density, calcium accretion) and opposing on others (longitudinal growth-plate-driven elongation, where DHT partially counteracts estrogen's suppression) — real quantitative support for treating androgen and estrogen as mechanistically distinct rather than redundant bone signals.
- **[[Oxandrolone-Pediatric-Growth-Disorders-Review|Soliman et al. 2025]]** — a broader (though lower-confidence, likely predatory-journal-published) survey of oxandrolone across CDGP, Turner syndrome, and burn recovery, reinforcing the same dose-dependent growth/virilization/skeletal-maturation tradeoff already established by [[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]'s more rigorous controlled trial.
- **[[Trestolone-Human-Metabolism-Doping-Analysis|Piper et al. 2025]]** — trestolone (MENT), a potent synthetic androgen resistant to 5α-reduction but still aromatizable, the mirror image of DHT as an experimental tool for isolating androgen-pathway contributions; characterized here purely for anti-doping detection purposes, not physiological effect.
- **[[Nandrolone-TGFbeta-IGF1-Muscle-Wound-Healing|Al-Abdullah & Al-Mashhadane 2023]]** — a fourth context for nandrolone's risk-benefit spectrum: local application at muscle wound sites improved healing via TGF-β1/IGF-1 upregulation, a growth-factor-mediated mechanism distinct from classical AR signaling.
- **[[Low-Dose-Testosterone-CDGP-Bergada1995|Bergadá et al. 1995]]** — a genuine complication for the growth/skeletal-maturation tradeoff pattern: low-dose testosterone in CDGP boys produced a substantially larger, earlier growth spurt *without* impairing predicted adult height, unlike the oxandrolone findings elsewhere in this domain.
- **[[MENT-Trestolone-Pharmacokinetics-Monkeys-Kumar2013|Kumar et al. 2013]]** — the original developmental rationale for trestolone (MENT): resistance to 5α-reduction was meant to avoid DHT's amplified prostate-stimulatory effects, making it a candidate safer long-term androgen replacement — now the subject of dedicated anti-doping detection work per [[Trestolone-Human-Metabolism-Doping-Analysis|Piper et al. 2025]].
- **[[MENT-Human-PK-Suvisaari1997|Suvisaari et al. 1997]]** — first human quantification of MENT's tissue-selective potency: 10-12x testosterone's potency on muscle/pituitary, only 4-5x on prostate/seminal vesicles, confirming the animal-model rationale in humans.
- **[[Fluoxymesterone-Linear-Growth-Weight-Laron|Laron]]**, **[[Anabolic-Steroids-Growth-Review-Prader|Prader]]**, and **[[Anabolic-Steroids-Osteoporosis-Dequeker-Geusens|Dequeker & Geusens]]** — three classic reviews/trials establishing anabolic androgens (fluoxymesterone specifically for Laron, with an explicit 0.25mg/kg/day virilization threshold) as historically the primary growth-promoting and osteoporosis-treating androgen option before modern GH therapy.
- **[[Nandrolone-Humerus-Fracture-Delayed-Union-Oliveira|Oliveira et al. 2020]]** — nandrolone for delayed-union fracture healing, a further context in the nandrolone risk-benefit catalog alongside muscle-wound healing.
- **[[Oxandrolone-Neuroregenerative-Case-Report-Bianchi2015|Bianchi & Marbini 2015]]** — an unusual single-case report of apparent oxandrolone neuroregenerative effect in Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, an entirely novel (peripheral nerve) tissue context.
- **[[Combined-Nandrolone-Testosterone-Femur-Morphometry-Sefa2011|Sefa et al. 2011]]** — combined nandrolone+testosterone femur morphometry, a rare two-agent combination test from the same group behind [[Trenbolone-Extremity-Bones-Running-Rats|Sarı & Lök 2019]].

> [!key-insight] Testosterone itself is rarely the "interesting" variable in this domain's papers — nearly every source uses it as the input to a pathway (aromatization or 5α-reduction) whose downstream product is what's actually being studied. This is why [[Aromatase]], [[Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)]], and [[Androgen Receptor (AR)]] each have richer, more contested "in what way" entries than testosterone does.
