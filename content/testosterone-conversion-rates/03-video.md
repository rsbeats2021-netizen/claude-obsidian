The same testosterone dose. Which means two people on identical protocols get different hormones.

[cover card]

Part becomes estradiol through aromatase. Part becomes DHT through 5-alpha-reductase.

[is a starting material]

In men, directly measuring how testosterone dose and patient age affect the proportion converted to estradiol versus DHT.

[the actual fractions]

The conversion fractions are not fixed. Raising the dose does not scale all three hormones by the same multiplier.

[the split]

Patient age independently affects the conversion pattern — so the same milligram amount produces a different hormonal profile at different ages.

[separately]

Estradiol is the dominant driver of growth plate closure. Anything raising the aromatised fraction is spending skeletal maturation, whatever the intention was.

[is specific]

When you see any androgen protocol, the question isn't the milligrams. It's what fraction becomes estradiol in that person — and that isn't on the label.

[takeaway card]

Ask what it becomes, not what it is.
