Insulin degludec. Why it is on a height list, and why that is dangerous.

[cover card]

It forms soluble multi-hexamer depots under the skin that dissociate slowly, giving an extremely flat, peakless profile rather than a curve. Mean terminal half-life 25 hours, roughly twice insulin glargine's 12.1.

[mechanism]

Take 17 type 1 diabetics off basal insulin for 8 hours: IGFBP-1 rose from 32 to 205 ng/mL and free IGF-1 fell below the detection limit in 15 of 17. Give one dose back and free IGF-1 rebounded to 0.54 ng/mL within two hours.

[the human data]

Every one of those numbers comes from people who were insulin deficient and had it restored. That is the entire evidence base for insulin and the IGF-1 axis.

[what it actually shows]

A 30-year-old bodybuilder injected 70 IU of short-acting insulin before a competition to stimulate GH. Degludec has a 25-hour half-life and over 42 hours of action, so a mistake cannot be waited out.

[hypoglycaemia]

The most interesting insulin result is about route rather than amount. Switching patients from subcutaneous to intraperitoneal delivery, which reaches the liver first, dropped IGFBP-1 by 100.7 µg/L, p < 0.01, with no relationship to HbA1c change.

[the route finding]

Real mechanism, excellent drug for its licensed purpose, and the weakest risk-to-evidence ratio of anything in this axis for the use people want it for. The human data shows restoring deficient insulin restores IGF-1.

[takeaway card]

Proven mechanism. Unproven use. Unforgiving kinetics.
