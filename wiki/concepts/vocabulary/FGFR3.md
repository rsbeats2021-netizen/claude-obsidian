# FGFR3 (Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptor 3)

FGFR3 is expressed in chondrocytes and mature osteoblasts, where it functions as a *negative* regulator of bone growth — normal FGFR3 signaling restrains chondrocyte proliferation. Gain-of-function mutations (via receptor stabilization, enhanced dimerization, and enhanced tyrosine kinase activity) cause achondroplasia, the most common form of human dwarfism, and its more severe related chondrodysplasia syndromes (hypochondroplasia, SADDAN, thanatophoric dysplasia). This makes FGFR3 this domain's most actively drug-targeted single gene, with at least three independent therapeutic strategies now tracked here.

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[FGFR3-Achondroplasia-Chondrodysplasia-Foundational-Review|Foundational review]]** — details the specific mutation mechanisms causing excess FGFR3 signaling in achondroplasia and related syndromes.
- **[[Snail1-FGFR3-Chondrogenesis-Achondroplasia-DeFrutos2007|de Frutos et al. 2007]]** — identifies Snail1 as the downstream transcriptional effector executing FGFR3's growth-suppressing signal in chondrogenesis.
- **[[IGF1-Rescues-FGFR3-Mutant-Apoptosis-Koike2003|Koike et al. 2003]]** — IGF-1 rescues chondrogenic cells expressing the achondroplasia-causing FGFR3(G380R) mutation from apoptosis via PI3K/MAPK, a mechanistic bridge between the GH/IGF-1 axis and FGFR3 pathway.
- **[[TYRA-300-FGFR3-Inhibitor-Chondrodysplasia|TYRA-300]]** and **[[Infigratinib-FGFR-Achondroplasia-Savarirayan2024|infigratinib]]** ([[Infigratinib-FGFR-Achondroplasia-Mouse-Model-Preclinical|preclinical mouse model]] preceding its human NEJM trial) — two purpose-built, selective FGFR3-inhibitor drugs in active development for achondroplasia.
- **[[Meclizine-Achondroplasia-Phase2-Japan-Trial|Meclizine]]** — a third, strategically distinct approach: repurposing a decades-old, cheap, already-approved antihistamine shown to inhibit the same pathway, tested in a Japanese Phase 2 trial.
- **[[Skeletal-Overgrowth-Pan-FGFR-Inhibitor-Case-Report|A 2024 case report]]** — a real clinical case of a pan-FGFR inhibitor (given for an unrelated tumor) causing skeletal *overgrowth* in a child, the mirror-image adverse effect validating the mechanistic logic behind the achondroplasia drugs above: block FGFR3, and the growth brake is released.

> [!key-insight] This is one of the few genes in this domain with a complete "mechanism → mutation → disease → multiple independent drug strategies → real-world adverse-effect confirmation of the mechanism" arc, spanning from a 2003 basic-science apoptosis-rescue paper to 2024-2025 clinical trials. See [[Three Achondroplasia Drug Strategies - FGFR3 Inhibition vs Repurposing]] for a detailed side-by-side of the three drug approaches.
