# Chromosomal and Hormonal Factors in Human Sexual Dimorphism (Fukami et al. 2026)

A review that steps back from this domain's usual receptor-and-enzyme-level focus to ask a broader question: what actually causes male/female differences in body structure, disease susceptibility, and life expectancy? The answer, per this paper, is not purely gonadal-hormone-driven — sex-chromosome mechanisms contribute independently.

Most directly relevant to this domain: the paper links sex-specific epigenetic regulation of SHOX (short stature homeobox gene, located in the pseudoautosomal region shared by X and Y chromosomes) to females' shorter average height relative to males — a growth-plate-relevant height determinant operating through chromosome dosage/epigenetics rather than through the androgen-aromatization or estrogen-receptor pathways this domain's other sources focus on. The paper also references a recently characterized "alternative steroidogenic pathway" producing novel non-aromatizable androgens in humans, which appears to be the same broader research area as [[11-Oxygenated-Androgens-PCOS-Hirsutism-Thesis|Varausová 2023]]'s coverage of 11-oxygenated adrenal androgens.

> [!key-insight] This paper introduces a genuinely new causal category to this domain: chromosome-dosage and epigenetic mechanisms (X-inactivation, SHOX regulation, mosaic chromosome loss) as direct contributors to sex differences in height and disease, running in parallel to — not through — the hormone-receptor pathways (aromatase, ERα/β, AR, GPER-1) that dominate every other source in this domain. None of this domain's other sources address chromosomal mechanisms at all, making this the first source to note that "sexual dimorphism" and "sex hormone action" are not fully synonymous.

> [!gap] A review article; the SHOX-epigenetics-height link and the "alternative steroidogenic pathway" are both described only briefly, pointing to primary literature this vault has not yet ingested.
