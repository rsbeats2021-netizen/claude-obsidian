# Central Stimulant (ADHD Medication) Treatment and Growth: 410-Patient Longitudinal Study

Directly extends [[Methylphenidate-Premature-Growth-Plate-Closure-Pazos-Perez|the in vitro finding]] (methylphenidate promotes premature growth-plate closure in cell culture) with real clinical longitudinal data: 410 children/adolescents treated with central stimulants for up to 16 years showed reduced body weight and a temporary growth halt, with direct implications for final height and stature — moving this domain's stimulant-medication-and-growth-plate concern from an in vitro mechanism to actual clinical growth-outcome data in a large pediatric population.

> [!key-insight] Given central stimulants' extremely widespread pediatric use, this large longitudinal dataset (16+ years of follow-up in some patients) is one of the higher-quality clinical-evidence sources in this domain's entire growth-plate-closure-risk literature — most other risk-factor sources here (anabolic steroids, aromatase inhibitors) rely on much smaller trials or animal models.
