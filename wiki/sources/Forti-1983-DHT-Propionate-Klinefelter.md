# Forti et al. 1983 — DHT Propionate in Klinefelter Syndrome

*J Endocrinol Invest* 6(4):297–300. PMID 6417219. DOI 10.1007/BF03347593.

The **only published human dose of DHT propionate**. Vehicle and concentration not stated.

Six men with Klinefelter syndrome, crossover with a one-month washout:
- **Testosterone propionate 100 mg IM daily × 4 days**
- then, one month later, **dihydrotestosterone propionate 100 mg IM daily × 4 days** (= 83.8 mg DHT-equivalent per day)

Plasma LH, FSH, PRL, testosterone and DHT measured for 8 days during and after each arm.

| | TP 100 mg × 4 d | DHTP 100 mg × 4 d |
|---|---|---|
| LH | transient reduction only | **late and transient** reduction |
| FSH | more prolonged reduction | **very late** reduction |
| PRL | rose significantly during and after | no rise reported |

Two conclusions the authors draw:
1. **DHT is less effective than testosterone at suppressing gonadotropin concentrations** in Klinefelter patients.
2. The prolactin rise on TP is **not a pure androgenic effect** — it tracks the increased T → estradiol conversion rate. DHTP, being non-aromatisable, does not produce it.

This is the human mirror of the pattern throughout [[DHT Propionate Animal Dose Ledger]]: the ester was kept in service precisely because it fails where aromatisation is required.
