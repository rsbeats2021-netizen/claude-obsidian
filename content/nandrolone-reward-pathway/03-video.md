Steroids act on. And it showed up in adolescent animals specifically.

[cover card]

The discussion is always about physical risk — liver, heart, hormones. Dependence potential barely enters the conversation.

[as rewarding drugs]

Animals learn to associate a location with a drug. If they then choose to spend time there, the drug was rewarding.

[is place preference]

In adolescent male mice — not only adults. The compound registered as rewarding in animals at the developmental stage that matters most here.

[produced that preference]

Altered D1 dopamine receptor expression in the nucleus accumbens — the core reward structure. So the behaviour came with a matching change in the circuit.

[a neural correlate]

A compound with reward properties is one people continue past their original intention. That changes the risk calculus from a single decision into a repeated one.

[practically]

Mouse behavioural work. Conditioned place preference indicates reward, not clinical addiction, and rodent dosing isn't human use.

[takeaway card]

Abuse-potential signal. Not a diagnosis.
