# Video script — Faster aspart

RAPID MEALTIME INSULIN · SUBCUTANEOUS · NO HEIGHT TRIAL

220 words, ~74s at 178 wpm. Target band 45-90s.

Spoken lines only. Visual cue in brackets.

---

Faster aspart. The mealtime half of the insulin question.

[cover card]

Same mechanism as every insulin: portal insulin drives hepatic GH-receptor expression, and IGFBP-1 falls when insulin is present, which raises free IGF-1 rather than total. IGFBP-3 in complex with the acid-labile subunit holds roughly 75 to 80% of circulating IGF-1, and that pool does not move acutely with insulin.

[why it is here]

Not all insulins do this equally. In head-to-head comparison, protaminated biphasic mixes performed measurably worse than plain aspart or regular insulin on the IGF-system response.

[a measured difference]

Four insulin preparations at identical doses produced parallel decreases in IGFBP-1 despite marked differences in peripheral insulin concentration and plasma glucose. The authors concluded that glycemic response cannot predict IGF-system response.

[the prediction problem]

Rapid insulin without matching carbohydrate is how hypoglycaemia happens. Which means the compound dictates your eating schedule rather than fitting around it, and a missed or delayed meal becomes a medical event rather than an inconvenience.

[the practical trap]

As with every insulin here, the human IGF-1 data comes from insulin-deficient patients having insulin restored. Free IGF-1 fell below detection in 15 of 17 when it was withdrawn, and rebounded when given back.

[what is not known]

The mechanism is real and better characterised than for most things on this list. The free-versus-total IGF-1 distinction is genuinely useful and almost nobody knows it.

[takeaway card]

Real mechanism, no feedback signal, self-conflicting protocol.
