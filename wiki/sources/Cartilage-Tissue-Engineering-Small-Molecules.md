# Small Molecule Compounds Promoting Chondrocyte Proliferation and Chondrogenic Differentiation (Li et al. 2020)

A review of the cartilage tissue-engineering problem: primary chondrocytes are difficult to expand in culture without losing their phenotype (dedifferentiation), and cartilage's own limited self-repair capacity motivates lab-grown alternatives. The review surveys small-molecule compounds, cytokines, gene-based approaches, and proteins used to either expand existing chondrocytes while preserving phenotype, or to drive chondrogenic differentiation of stem cells toward a cartilage-forming fate.

> [!gap] This is an applied tissue-engineering review (biomedical/translational focus) rather than a study of native growth-plate biology; its relevance to this domain is at the level of "what regulates chondrocyte identity and proliferation," shared with the transcription-factor mechanisms in [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]], rather than growth-plate hormone signaling specifically.

## Relevance to This Domain

Complements [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]] from an applied angle — the same Sox9/Runx2-type transcriptional control this review's small molecules often target is the native biology described by [[Sox9-Growth-Plate-Chondrocyte-Fate|Dy et al. 2012]] and [[Dlx5-Chondrocyte-Differentiation|Ferrari & Kosher 2002]].
