# Research brief — The man who couldn't make estrogen

## Core claim
An aromatase-deficient adult man with normal-to-high testosterone had unfused
epiphyses, open growth plates and low bone mass. Estrogen therapy substantially
increased his bone mineral density.

## Why it pairs with `estrogen-sets-final-height`
That deck uses Smith 1994 — a broken *receptor*. This is a broken *enzyme*.
Same phenotype from opposite failures in the same chain, and this one has a
treatment arm the receptor case could not have (his receptors worked, only the
supply was missing).

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Aromatase-deficient man: normal/high testosterone, unfused
  epiphyses, open plates, low bone mass (Bilezikian 1998).
- **Tier 1** — Estrogen therapy substantially increased BMD. Direct clinical
  proof that estrogen, not androgen, is what the male skeleton needs here.
- **Tier 1** — Normal androgen levels did not protect the skeleton on their own.
- **Tier 2** — Likely an early instance of the aromatase-deficient-man case
  series later summarised by de Ronde & de Jong 2011.

## Honest limits
Single-patient case report. Informative because genetics isolates the variable,
not because the sample is large. Slide 8 states this.
