# Shared evidence note — Khavinson bioregulator family

Applies to Vesugen (KED), Cartalax, Vilon (KE), Livagen (KEDA), Epitalon (AEDG),
Pinealon (EDR). Quoted on the LIMIT slide of each monograph so the shared problem
is stated every time rather than assumed.

From `wiki/chats/Research Compendium.md`:

- "essentially **no peer-reviewed efficacy evidence outside a small Russian
  literature that has not been internationally replicated**, and dosing figures
  circulating for them are **not grounded in pharmacokinetic studies**"
- "The core mechanistic claim — that short di- or tri-peptides can bind DNA with
  sequence specificity — is considered **biologically implausible by Western
  molecular biology standards**, since standard transcription factors require
  DNA-binding domains of 30-60+ amino acids for sequence specificity, and **no
  independent laboratory has replicated the core mechanism claims**."
- "At typical doses (1-10 mg/day) these peptides are expected to be **hydrolyzed
  into common dietary amino acids** and are therefore considered **low risk of
  harm**"
- "The Khavinson-family peptides (Epitalon, Cartalax, Pinealon, Vesugen) have
  **zero direct evidence in a growing animal or on craniofacial tissue**; all
  claimed relevance is extrapolated from adult/aged/ovariectomized models"
- "Every human dosing regimen located across Epitalon, Pinealon, Cartalax, and
  Vesugen is a **pulsed course of 10-30 days** (longest 60 days)"
- Documented sequences: Epitalon AEDG, Vilon KE, Livagen KEDA, Vesugen KED,
  Pancragen KEDW. **Cartalax's sequence is ambiguous.**
- Khavinson holds 196 patents, 6 peptide pharmaceuticals and 64 supplements over
  ~40 years. Clinical efficacy has been *reported* for Timalin, Thymogen, Vilon,
  Epithalamin, Prostatilen, Cortexin, Retinalamin.
- A 2022 review proposes these 2-4 residue peptides are small enough to use POT
  transporters (PEPT1/2, PHT1/2) and LAT1/2, with POT expression in human nasal
  epithelium giving a transporter-level rationale for intranasal delivery —
  but **zero head-to-head injectable-vs-intranasal comparisons exist**.

## The honest verdict shape for this family
Not "dangerous". Not "proven". The evidence problem and the safety profile point
in different directions, and a monograph that collapses them into one answer is
wrong either way.
