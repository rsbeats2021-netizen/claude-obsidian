# Research brief — Population-scale skeletal growth

## Core claim
Four hunter-gatherer populations show significant differences in bone growth,
best explained by growth environment rather than ancestry. A useful corrective
to a field that thinks only in individual hormone optimisation.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Femur/humerus cortical thickness and diaphyseal length compared
  across Late Stone Age, Sadlermiut, Indian Knoll and Point Hope populations.
- **Tier 2** — Significant inter-population differences, best explained by
  population-level growth environment (nutrition, activity load, disease
  burden) rather than genetic ancestry.
- **Tier 1** — Secular height trends: populations gained substantial height
  across generations with no genetic change.

## Honest limits — slide 8
Skeletal remains can't measure diet or activity directly; those are inferred.
Population-level findings don't predict individual outcomes. The deck says
"useful for perspective, useless as a personal protocol" and that framing must
survive editing.

## Why include it in a hormone-focused channel
Variety, and honesty. The historically largest levers on human height were
load, food and disease — not receptor optimisation. A channel that never says
this is selling a narrower story than the evidence supports.
