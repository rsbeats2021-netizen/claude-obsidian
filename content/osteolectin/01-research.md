# Research brief — Osteolectin

## Core claim
Osteolectin, known for maintaining adult bone mass, has a distinct juvenile
function: it increases growth plate chondrocyte proliferation and bone
elongation. And there's a human genetic variant linked to a functional
phenotype.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 2** — Osteolectin promotes bone elongation via increased growth plate
  chondrocyte proliferation, increasing body length in juvenile mice (PNAS 2023).
- **Tier 1** — A human genetic variant in osteolectin was identified and linked
  to a functional phenotype — direct human genetic evidence, not mouse
  inference.
- **Tier 1** — Rodents don't undergo true epiphyseal fusion at sexual maturity
  as humans do, which is why the human variant matters disproportionately.

## Why this deck is really about evidence quality
Slide 8 is the payload: when you hear a growth claim, ask whether it rests on a
mouse, a cell culture, or a human genetic variant. Those are different kinds of
knowing, and this field mixes them constantly. Osteolectin is the example that
clears the highest bar, which makes it the right vehicle for the lesson.

## Deliberately stated — slide 7
There is no osteolectin intervention. Well-evidenced and unavailable are
compatible, and the deck says so rather than implying a lever exists.
