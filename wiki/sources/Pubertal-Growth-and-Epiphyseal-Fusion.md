# Source: Pubertal Growth and Epiphyseal Fusion (Shim 2015)

**Type**: Review article (Annals of Pediatric Endocrinology & Metabolism, 2015) — the foundational overview for this domain's growth-plate cluster

## Summary

Reviews the cellular, paracrine, and endocrine mechanisms of longitudinal bone growth and its cessation at puberty's end. Growth occurs via endochondral ossification at the growth plate (resting → proliferative → hypertrophic chondrocyte zones); growth *stops* when chondrocyte proliferative capacity is exhausted, a process called epiphyseal senescence/fusion, whose exact cellular trigger remains unknown.

## Key Findings

- **Four competing theories of epiphyseal fusion**, none conclusively proven in humans:
  1. **Apoptosis** — most widely held, but a study by Emons et al. found *no* classical apoptosis signs in fusing human growth plates, directly contradicting animal (rat) evidence.
  2. **Autophagy** — observed in avian/newborn mouse chondrocytes, but not found in human growth plate studies.
  3. **Hypoxia** — a dense bone border may physically block oxygen/nutrients to the fusing plate, causing non-classical cell death.
  4. **Transdifferentiation** — oldest theory (hypertrophic chondrocytes become osteoblasts directly); based on organ/cell culture models, lacks direct human evidence.
- **Paracrine factors**: PTHrP (maintains proliferative chondrocytes), Indian hedgehog (IHH, positively regulates PTHrP and independently affects differentiation), BMP signaling, and FGF/FGFR system (FGFR1/3 growth-inhibiting, FGFR2 growth-promoting) all coordinate chondrocyte proliferation/differentiation/vascularization.
- **GH-IGF-I axis**: growth hormone stimulates IGF-I synthesis in liver and *locally in the growth plate*; local growth-plate IGF-I is considered more important than circulating IGF-I for chondrocyte proliferation/hypertrophy.
- **Estrogen's role**: acts via ERα and ERβ, both expressed across all growth plate zones. In cartilage-specific ERα knockout mice, estrogen's effect on growth plate thickness was unremarkable in early puberty but pronounced in late puberty — suggesting low-dose estrogen drives the pubertal growth spurt (via GH-IGF-I activation) while high-dose estrogen later triggers fusion. ERα appears to be the dominant mediator in bone; ERβ's role, especially in males, remains unclear.
- **Androgen's role is indirect**: androgens (DHT, testosterone) directly stimulate chondrocyte proliferation and proteoglycan synthesis in vitro, but the paper explicitly states that androgen's role *in epiphyseal fusion specifically* is due to peripheral **aromatization of androgens to estrogens** in growth plate cartilage — not direct androgen receptor signaling.
- **Species caveat**: rodents don't undergo true epiphyseal fusion at sexual maturity (bone growth continues), unlike humans — a persistent translational gap in growth-plate research.
