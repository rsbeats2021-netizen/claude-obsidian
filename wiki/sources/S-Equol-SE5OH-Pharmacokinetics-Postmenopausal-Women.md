# S-Equol (SE5-OH) Pharmacokinetics in Postmenopausal Women

A direct pharmacokinetic follow-up to [[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]] already in this domain: SE5-OH, a supplement form of S-equol made by fermenting soy germ isoflavones with the bacterium *Lactococcus garvieae*, achieves 82% systemic bioavailability in postmenopausal women — notably higher than the parent isoflavones (daidzein, genistein) it's derived from. Critically, only 25% of people are natural "equol-producers" (whose gut bacteria convert daidzein to equol), but this direct-supplementation approach bypasses that bottleneck, delivering consistent equol pharmacokinetics regardless of a person's native gut-conversion capacity.

> [!key-insight] This solves a practical translational problem for [[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]'s selective-ERβ-agonist/DHT-antagonist mechanism: since most people can't reliably produce their own S-equol from dietary soy, a fermented direct-supplement form makes the compound's ERβ/anti-androgen mechanism actually deliverable as a therapeutic, not just a dietary curiosity dependent on gut microbiome composition.

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a practical pharmacokinetic/delivery dimension to [[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]'s mechanistic finding — S-equol's therapeutic potential depends on supplementation strategies like SE5-OH specifically because most people can't produce it naturally in adequate amounts.
