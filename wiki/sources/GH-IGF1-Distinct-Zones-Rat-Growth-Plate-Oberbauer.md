# GH and IGF-I Act on Distinct Growth-Plate Zones (Oberbauer & Peng 1995)

An early (1995) but still-relevant demonstration that GH's direct action on the growth plate is not uniform: using discrete chondrocyte populations isolated by zone, low-dose GH and IGF-I preferentially stimulated proliferation in proliferative-zone cells specifically, with minimal mitogenic effect on immature proliferative or resting-zone cells. Combining GH and IGF-I produced no additional proliferative benefit over either alone, arguing against simple additivity within the growth plate.

The paper also traced a local autocrine loop: IGF-I exposure increased IGF-I mRNA within resting-zone cells themselves, and both GH and IGF-I specifically increased IGF-I receptor mRNA in the most immature proliferative-zone cells — evidence GH acts directly and zone-specifically on the growth plate, not merely through systemic liver-derived IGF-I.

> [!gap] A 1995 in vitro study using isolated rat chondrocyte fractions; more recent single-cell approaches in this domain (e.g., [[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]]) offer far higher-resolution confirmation of zone-specific GH action in human tissue, making this paper primarily of historical/foundational interest now.

## Relevance to This Domain

An early foundational data point for [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]]'s zone-specific, direct (non-liver-IGF-I) action on the growth plate — the same basic finding later confirmed and extended to human tissue and single-cell resolution by [[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]].
