Cartalax. The least documented compound in its own family.

[cover card]

The claimed mechanism is substantial: chondroprogenitor to chondrocyte transition, osteoblast reinforcement at the cartilage-bone interface, increased type I collagen, decreased MMP9, decreased TNF-α and IL-6, increased CD98HC and mTORC1 signalling, and increased paracrine IGF-1 alongside SIRT6. An independent source, Linkova 2023, attributes its action to NF-κB, IGF1 and TNKS2 modulation in ageing MSCs, which at least partly corroborates the list.

[the mechanism list]

This is the objection that matters more than any dosing or purity question. Growth plate closure is a hormonally timed developmental event driven by estradiol at the chondrocyte.

[the decisive one]

Of Epitalon, Cartalax, Pinealon and Vesugen, Cartalax is the least documented, with 12 total Europe PMC hits. Not 12 trials.

[the literature]

There is roughly a 100-fold gap between them, with about a 10-fold gap in vitro: 2000 ng/mL Sigumir against 200 ng/mL Cartalax. Epitalon is 100 to 1000 times more potent than Epithalamin, its crude parent.

[the analogue gap]

At 1 to 10 mg a day these peptides are expected to be hydrolysed into common dietary amino acids, which is why even sceptics rate the family as low risk of harm. So the case against Cartalax is not that it will hurt you.

[the fair point]

The weakest-documented compound in a family with a disputed mechanism, with an ambiguous sequence, unquantified capsule content, a self-contradictory mechanism claim, and source documents that confuse it with its relatives. And the structural objection stands regardless of all that: it targets degeneration, and growth plate closure is a timed developmental programme, not degeneration.

[takeaway card]

No. And not for a close reason.
