# Trenbolone raises metabolic rate

**Verdict: unsupported.** No human data exists. The only calorimetry in any species shows fasting metabolic rate **8-11% lower** on trenbolone acetate, intake-adjusted.

## Evidence for

None at the level of measured energy expenditure. The claim rests on:

- Subjective reports of elevated body temperature, night sweats, and heat intolerance — real and consistent reports, but thermal *sensation* and sweating are not calorimetry.
- Observed recomposition (fat loss alongside muscle gain) attributed to thermogenesis rather than to partitioning, training, or concurrent energy restriction.
- Tier X creator assertion. No primary source traced.

## Evidence against

- "Fasting metabolic rate (76.9 v. 70.7 kJ/kg per d) was significantly (P less than 0.05) lower in implanted steers" — [[Hunter-Vercoe-1987-Trenbolone-Fasting-Metabolic-Rate-Steers]], tier C. Replicated across two experiments in the same animals: −10.6% and −8.1%, the first still significant after adjusting for the lower feed intake.
- The agricultural research programme that produced trenbolone's metabolic data was explicitly aimed at **reducing** the maintenance energy requirement of cattle so they gain weight on poorer feed. Lower metabolic rate was the intended, achieved effect.
- Free T3 was unchanged in implanted steers, ruling out the thyroid-mediated mechanism usually invoked.
- No androgen tested in humans shows a direct thermogenic effect: testosterone withdrawal and repletion change REE by nothing ([[Santosa-2010-Sex-Steroid-Withdrawal-REE]]), and nandrolone — trenbolone's closest studied structural relative, both 19-nor androgens — produced large nitrogen retention with **zero** REE change ([[Strawford-1999-Nandrolone-REE-Nitrogen-Balance]]).

## What would settle it

Indirect calorimetry in humans on trenbolone. This will never be run — no ethics committee approves administering a veterinary androgen with no human pharmacokinetic data to volunteers. **The claim is therefore permanently unfalsifiable in humans**, which is itself a reason to stop asserting it.

The nearest tractable study would be a crossover measuring REE and core temperature in existing users during on- and off-cycle phases. Observational, confounded, but it would at least separate "feels hot" from "burns more."

## The plausible alternative explanation

Trenbolone binds the progesterone receptor and glucocorticoid receptor with meaningful affinity in addition to the androgen receptor. Night sweats, insomnia, elevated resting heart rate and heat intolerance are consistent with sympathetic and neuroendocrine disruption, which produces the *sensation* of a raised metabolism without a correspondingly raised whole-body energy expenditure. Thermal discomfort is being read as a calorie-burning signal.
