# OPG/RANKL Axis

Osteoprotegerin (OPG) and RANKL (receptor activator of NF-κB ligand) are a paired signaling system regulating osteoclast activity and bone resorption: RANKL promotes osteoclast formation/activity, while OPG is a decoy receptor that binds and neutralizes RANKL, suppressing resorption. The OPG/RANKL ratio, rather than either molecule alone, is this domain's key readout — a lower ratio favors resorption, a higher ratio favors bone retention.

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[ERKO-BERKO-DERKO-OPG-RANKL-Male-Mice-Windahl2001|Lindberg et al. 2001]]** — the domain's central OPG/RANKL mechanism paper: estradiol treatment of orchidectomized male mice increased trabecular BMD 143% and decreased the OPG/RANKL ratio in vertebral bone. Using the ERKO/BERKO/DERKO knockout panel, baseline OPG/RANKL ratio regulation (and serum IL-6/TRAP 5b) was shown to require ERα specifically, not ERβ — establishing this as an ERα-mediated pathway.
- **[[PEPITEM-Anabolic-Osteopeptide-Osteoblast-Osteoclast-Coupling|PEPITEM]]** — a novel endogenous peptide that acts directly on osteoblasts to promote bone formation, and separately stimulates an inhibitory paracrine loop via OPG to limit resorption — converging on the same OPG/RANKL axis through a completely different (non-steroid-hormone) signal.
- **[[Androgen-Effects-Bone-Muscle-Notelovitz|Notelovitz]]** — situates OPG/RANKL-mediated osteoclast/osteoblast coupling within the broader androgen-receptor-distribution picture across bone cell types (AR dominance on osteoblasts/osteocytes).

> [!key-insight] The OPG/RANKL axis is this domain's clearest example of a single downstream bone-remodeling pathway that multiple, mechanistically unrelated upstream signals (estrogen via ERα, and the non-hormonal peptide PEPITEM) both converge on — suggesting it functions as a common final pathway for diverse pro-bone-formation signals, not a pathway exclusive to any one hormone system.
