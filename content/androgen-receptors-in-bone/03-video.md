# Video script — "Androgen receptors in bone"

60s · 177 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:08] HOOK
SPOKEN: Bone cells have androgen receptors. That was proven in human tissue in
        1989, and it settled one argument while starting a bigger one that
        still isn't resolved.
  [VISUAL: osteoblast with receptor highlighted]

### [0:08–0:18] SETUP
SPOKEN: The estrogen camp has the stronger case — human genetics and clinical
        trials both point to estrogen as the dominant lever on closure and bone
        density. Which raises an obvious objection.
  [ON-SCREEN: "SO WHY THE RECEPTORS?"]

### [0:18–0:38] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: Human osteoblasts carry functional androgen receptors, later localised
        in human bone tissue. And DHT — which cannot be aromatised into
        estrogen at all — specifically increased androgen receptor levels in
        human bone cells and amplified their response. Estradiol did not. A
        non-convertible androgen produced an effect estrogen couldn't.
  [ON-SCREEN: "DHT ✓  ESTRADIOL ✗"]

### [0:38–0:52] THE COUNTER
SPOKEN: But then testosterone and the non-aromatisable androgen oxandrolone
        were tested in organ culture, and had no effect on baseline
        longitudinal growth rate. Receptors present, growth rate unmoved. And
        these papers argue with each other directly — one says preventing
        suppression is a different claim from promoting growth.
  [VISUAL: two papers, arrows between them]

### [0:52–0:60] CTA
SPOKEN: So the honest answer is two-part. Direct androgen action on bone is
        real. Whether it drives longitudinal growth is contested at the bench.
        Receptors existing doesn't tell you what they accomplish. Don't forget,
        it's never over.
