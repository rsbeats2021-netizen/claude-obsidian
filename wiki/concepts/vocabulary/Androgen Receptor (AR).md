# Androgen Receptor (AR)

The androgen receptor is the nuclear hormone receptor that androgens (testosterone, DHT) bind to directly, triggering AR-mediated gene transcription. Because AR activation does not require aromatization to estrogen, "does AR activation itself matter, or is androgen's effect really estrogen's doing after conversion" is one of the most contested distinctions running through this domain's growth-plate and bone literature.

---

## Studies Mentioning This Term

- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Upregulation-Osteoblasts|Wiren et al. 1999]]** — the domain's clearest direct-AR-mechanism paper: DHT specifically up-regulates AR protein 2-4 fold in human osteoblastic cell lines, amplifying the cell's transcriptional response to androgen; blocked by the AR antagonist hydroxyflutamide, confirming receptor-dependence. Testosterone matched DHT's effect; estradiol did not.
- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Modulation-No-Effect-Growth-Plate|Chagin et al. 2009]]** — tested AR activation (testosterone and the non-aromatizable androgen oxandrolone) in cultured fetal rat metatarsal bones and found **no effect** on baseline longitudinal growth rate — a negative result that directly engages and complicates [[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]]'s earlier finding in a similar model.
- **[[DHT-Prevents-Glucocorticoid-Effects-Fetal-Bone|Picherit et al. 2000]]** — DHT (acting via AR, since it cannot be aromatized) prevented glucocorticoid-induced growth/mineralization suppression in cultured fetal bone and had a modest independent growth-stimulating effect.
- **[[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]]** — explicitly downplays direct AR signaling in growth-plate fusion: androgens stimulate chondrocyte proliferation in vitro, but the paper attributes androgen's role in fusion specifically to peripheral aromatization to estrogen, not direct AR action.
- **[[Dihydrotestosterone-Clinical-Implications|Swerdloff et al. 2017]]** — reviews AR's role in prostate, skin, and cardiovascular tissue (where DHT acts via AR to promote nitric oxide synthesis), but concludes AR-mediated DHT action is not required for maintaining bone density in men within the normal testosterone range.
- **[[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]** — notes that S-equol's anti-androgen action does **not** involve AR blockade at all (equol has no affinity for the androgen receptor); instead it binds DHT directly, distinguishing it mechanistically from AR-blocking anti-androgen drugs.

- **[[AR-Overexpression-Chondrogenic-Rabbit-Articular-Chondrocytes|Liu et al. 2021]]** — lentiviral AR overexpression in rabbit *articular* (not growth-plate) chondrocytes reduced apoptosis, increased Sox9/collagen II/aggrecan, decreased matrix-degrading MMP-13, and improved cartilage-defect regeneration in vivo — via mTOR inhibition, not increased proliferation. Explicitly notes this contrasts with AR's reported growth-plate proliferation-promoting role, attributing the difference to articular vs. growth-plate chondrocyte biology rather than a contradiction.

- **[[Androgen-Metabolism-Human-Fetal-Epiphyseal-Cartilage-Audi1984|Audi et al. 1984]]** and **[[DHT-Binding-Sites-Human-Fetal-Chondrocytes-Carrascosa1990|Carrascosa et al. 1990]]** — foundational human-tissue pair from the same Barcelona group: human fetal epiphyseal cartilage and its chondrocytes actively metabolize androgens (1984), and specific DHT-binding sites (functional AR) exist in these same cultured cells (1990) — establishing local androgen-metabolizing and receptor machinery in human growth-plate tissue directly, complementing the largely rabbit/rodent AR evidence base elsewhere in this domain.
- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Localization-Human-Bone-Abu1997|Abu et al.]]** — directly localizes AR within human bone tissue itself (not just cultured osteoblast-like cell lines), confirming [[Androgen-Receptors-Human-Osteoblasts-Colvard1989|Colvard et al. 1989]]'s cell-culture discovery translates to actual human bone in situ.

- **[[DHEA-and-the-Skeleton-Review|Gordon et al. 1999]]** — DHEA's mitogenic (proliferation-stimulating) effect on human osteoblasts is blocked by the AR antagonist hydroxyflutamide, confirming a direct AR-mediated mechanism distinct from DHEA's separate aromatization-to-estrogen and IGF-I-stimulating pathways.
- **[[Androgen-Receptors-Human-Osteoblasts-Colvard1989|Colvard et al. 1989]]** — the foundational discovery that normal human osteoblast-like cells express functional AR at all, a decade before [[Androgen-Receptor-Upregulation-Osteoblasts|Wiren et al. 1999]]'s regulatory mechanism work.
- **[[Androgen-Ectopic-Chondrocyte-Penile-Body-Hiremath|Hiremath et al.]]** — supraphysiological androgens caused ectopic chondrocyte accumulation in mouse penile tissue, displacing smooth muscle — striking proof that AR-driven chondrogenic potential isn't tissue-restricted to the skeleton.
- **[[Androgen-Receptor-Structure-Prostate-Cancer-Tan2015|Tan et al. 2015]]** — structural-biology review of AR's domain architecture (N-terminal, DNA-binding, ligand-binding domains), foundational structural context (though framed around prostate cancer drug discovery) for understanding AR's mechanism generally.
- **[[AR-Osteoblast-Stage-Dependent-Action-Russell2014|Russell et al. 2014]]** — genetic AR-replacement-stage mapping in Global-ARKO mice found two temporally distinct AR actions in the same osteoblast lineage: AR in proliferating osteoblasts drives growth-phase trabecular/cortical accrual, while AR in mineralizing osteoblasts maintains trabecular bone post-puberty — both predominantly by lowering the RANKL/OPG ratio to inhibit resorption rather than by directly stimulating formation.

> [!contradiction] AR's role in growth-plate longitudinal growth specifically is genuinely disputed within this domain's own primary sources: Picherit et al. 2000 found a rescue effect via AR-active DHT, while Chagin et al. 2009 — explicitly citing Picherit — found no baseline growth effect from AR activation. See [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] for the full reconciliation attempt. Liu et al. 2021 adds a further wrinkle: AR has a clear positive effect on *articular* chondrocyte phenotype/survival, a tissue context distinct from growth-plate longitudinal growth.
