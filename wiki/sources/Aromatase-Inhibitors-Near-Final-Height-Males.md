# Source: Near Final Height in Males Treated with Aromatase Inhibitors (Bouliari et al. 2026)

**Type**: Retrospective chart review, 72 male patients, Weill Cornell Medicine pediatric endocrinology clinic, 2007–2022

## Summary

Aromatase inhibitors (AIs) — letrozole and anastrozole — are prescribed off-label to boys with advanced bone age and compromised predicted adult height, on the theory that blocking estrogen production delays growth plate closure (see [[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]] for why estrogen drives fusion). This study compares near-final/final height outcomes between the two agents and identifies factors associated with better outcomes.

## Key Findings

- **Letrozole beat anastrozole on height gain**: median gain in height 4.2 cm (letrozole) vs. 0.8 cm (anastrozole), p=0.013; this gap persisted (7.5 cm greater gain, 95% CI 1.8–13) after adjusting for concurrent growth hormone (GH) therapy.
- **Letrozole patients also ended closer to mid-parental height** (final adult height − mid-parental height: −0.5 cm vs. −5.8 cm for anastrozole, p=0.036).
- **Positive predictors of height gain**: longer AI treatment duration (p=0.005), earlier pubertal stage at treatment start — i.e. lower testicular volume (p=0.012), and concurrent GH therapy (+5.9 cm on average, p=0.012).
- **No effect of underlying diagnosis**: growth hormone deficiency, idiopathic short stature, early puberty, overweight/obesity, and other diagnoses all responded similarly — AI benefit didn't depend on why the patient had short stature.
- **Adverse effects**: mostly mild — acne (30 patients), some liver enzyme elevations (mostly transient), a minority with reported mood/behavior concerns (8.5%). No DXA bone density scans were performed in this cohort, so BMD impact is unknown here.
- **Why letrozole fell out of favor despite better height results**: the authors note their letrozole group is small in part because prior studies (cited: Zegarra et al., Hero et al.) found letrozole caused greater BMD Z-score declines and more vertebral deformities than anastrozole — a real efficacy/safety trade-off this study can't resolve since it didn't measure bone density directly.

## Confidence Note

> [!gap] Retrospective chart review, not an RCT; the letrozole group (n=11) was much smaller than anastrozole (n=59), which the authors themselves flag as a bias risk; no bone density data collected despite AI's known BMD risk being the central safety concern in the literature.

> [!key-insight] This paper and [[Aromatase-in-the-Human-Brain|Azcoitia et al. 2021]] both concern aromatase inhibitors, but for entirely different reasons and in different populations — one to *increase height* in boys by blocking bone-maturing estrogen, the other studying AI side effects (cognitive, arthralgia) in breast cancer patients from blocking brain-protective estrogen. See [[Aromatase Inhibitors - Height Therapy vs Neuro-Sensory Side Effects]].
