# Letrozole, Bone Age, and Adrenal Suppression (Jacobson et al. 2005)

A clinical letrozole trial in adolescent males with limited growth potential, largely confirming this domain's established pattern (significant bone-age deceleration, meaningful predicted-adult-height improvement) — but adding a genuinely new safety finding: roughly a quarter of patients showed subnormal responsiveness to ACTH stimulation testing, leading the authors to conclude letrozole causes mild adrenal suppression.

> [!key-insight] This is a distinct safety signal from the one already flagged via [[Aromatase-Inhibitors-Short-Stature-Cochrane-Review|McGrath & O'Grady 2015]]'s Cochrane review (vertebral morphological abnormalities in letrozole-treated boys). Between these two sources, this domain now has two independent, specific safety concerns for pediatric letrozole use — vertebral effects and adrenal suppression — that aren't mentioned in the more optimistic [[Aromatase-Inhibitors-Near-Final-Height-Males|Bouliari et al. 2026]] source, worth checking whether more recent literature addresses either.

> [!gap] Small cohort (24 patients, 6 further stratified by androgen co-treatment); the clinical significance of "subnormal" ACTH responsiveness (whether it caused symptomatic adrenal insufficiency or was a subclinical lab finding) isn't detailed in the extracted abstract.

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a second, independent safety signal to [[Aromatase]]'s AI-for-height literature (adrenal suppression, alongside the Cochrane review's vertebral-abnormality finding), reinforcing that this domain's off-label pediatric AI-for-height literature carries real, still not fully characterized safety considerations that the more clinically optimistic sources don't address.
