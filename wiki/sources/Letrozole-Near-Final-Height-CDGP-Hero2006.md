# Letrozole Increases Near-Final Height in CDGP Boys (Hero et al. 2006)

Directly addresses the specific evidentiary gap [[Aromatase-Inhibitors-Short-Stature-Cochrane-Review|McGrath & O'Grady's 2015 Cochrane review]] (already in this domain) identified as the central weakness of the AI-for-height literature: most trials showed short-term growth-velocity benefit but almost none reported actual final or near-final adult height. This 2006 paper — predating the Cochrane review by 9 years — reports exactly that outcome: near-final height improvement with letrozole in constitutional delay of puberty.

> [!contradiction] This creates an interesting evidentiary puzzle: this paper reports near-final height data supporting AI efficacy, yet the 2015 Cochrane review (published 9 years later, presumably aware of this study) still concluded there was "no evidence to support an increase in final adult height, based on limited data, with only one of four trials publishing final height data." Either this paper didn't meet the Cochrane review's inclusion criteria (possibly due to study design, e.g., non-randomized or insufficient control group), or its findings were judged insufficiently robust to shift the review's conclusion — this domain doesn't yet have enough detail to resolve which.
