# TYRA-300, an FGFR3-Selective Inhibitor, in Chondrodysplasia Models (Starrett et al. 2025)

Achondroplasia (ACH) and hypochondroplasia (HCH), the two most common forms of dwarfism, are caused by FGFR3 gain-of-function mutations that over-activate FGFR3 signaling in growth-plate proliferative-zone chondrocytes, disrupting normal chondrogenesis. This industry-authored study (Tyra Biosciences) tests TYRA-300, a selective FGFR3 tyrosine kinase inhibitor designed to avoid the off-target toxicity of earlier pan-FGFR1/2/3/4 inhibitors, across wild-type mice and two FGFR3-mutant mouse models (Fgfr3^Y367C/+ modeling ACH, Fgfr3^N534K/+ modeling HCH).

In wild-type mice, TYRA-300 alone dose-dependently increased nasoanal, tibia, and femur length — direct pharmacological confirmation that FGFR3 signaling normally constrains growth-plate growth even without a disease mutation. In the ACH model, treatment produced dramatic (17-33%) increases in long-bone length, restored disproportionate limb-length ratios toward normal, and — mechanistically — simultaneously increased proliferative-zone chondrocyte proliferation (via PCNA staining) and hypertrophic-zone chondrocyte differentiation (via collagen type X staining), essentially normalizing growth-plate architecture. Beyond limb length, TYRA-300 significantly enlarged the foramen magnum (reducing the risk of brainstem/spinal-cord compression, the leading cause of early mortality in ACH infants) and increased lumbar vertebral length while improving intervertebral disc shape — addressing spinal stenosis, the single largest lifetime morbidity in ACH. Effects were smaller but still significant in the milder HCH model.

> [!key-insight] TYRA-300 produced more bone growth than a comparable dose of infigratinib (a non-selective pan-FGFR inhibitor already in human clinical trials for ACH) in a historically-controlled comparison — positioning FGFR3-selectivity itself, not just FGFR inhibition generally, as the source of therapeutic benefit. The paper also notes children treated with non-selective pan-FGFR inhibitors for unrelated cancers have shown unexpected growth acceleration as a side effect, further supporting FGFR inhibition's general growth-promoting mechanism.

> [!gap] Industry-funded preclinical (mouse) study; all named authors besides the two academic French co-authors are Tyra Biosciences employees/equity holders — a direct financial conflict of interest disclosed by the authors themselves. Clinical efficacy and long-term human safety are not yet established.
