# Chondroinductive Peptides for Cartilage Regeneration (Liao et al. 2024)

A review of peptide-based (rather than small-molecule or full growth-factor) approaches to inducing chondrogenesis for osteoarthritis treatment, organized into three classes: growth-factor-derived peptides (BMP peptide, B2A2-K-NS), cell-adhesion-derived peptides (N-cadherin mimetic, RGD, GFOGER), and ECM-component-targeting peptides (collagen mimetic peptide, link protein N-terminal peptide, type II collagen-binding and aggrecan-binding peptides).

Nearly every peptide surveyed shows the same pattern: positive in vitro results (increased Sox9, collagen II, aggrecan expression; enhanced glycosaminoglycan content) but a consistent, explicitly acknowledged gap in in vivo validation. One exception with a defined mechanism is the link protein N-terminal peptide (LPP), which selectively binds BMPR-II (not BMPR-I), triggers endogenous BMP-7 production, activates p-Smad1/5, and induces Sox9 — with Runx2 and collagen X levels unaffected, indicating LPP promotes chondrogenic but not osteogenic differentiation specifically.

The review's second half addresses the field's practical bottleneck: peptides have short half-lives and struggle to penetrate avascular, negatively-charged cartilage matrix from the joint space, motivating carrier systems (chitosan, PLGA, hyaluronic acid, polyester amide) as the actual site of ongoing innovation, more than the peptides themselves.

> [!key-insight] The review explicitly contrasts peptides with steroid hormones on one specific axis: "unlike steroid hormones, which persist in the body for an extended duration, peptides have a short half-life, contributing to a favorable safety profile" — a rare direct comparison between this domain's usual pharmacological class (steroids) and an alternative one, framed as a safety/mechanism tradeoff rather than an efficacy claim.

> [!gap] Almost every specific peptide discussed lacks in vivo efficacy data — this is a review of promising in vitro mechanisms and delivery engineering, not of established therapies.
