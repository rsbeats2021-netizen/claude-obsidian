# GH and IGF-I: Related but Independent Roles (Butler & Le Roith 2001)

A major, highly-cited synthesis directly challenging the traditional "somatomedin hypothesis" — the idea that GH's growth-promoting effects work almost entirely through liver-derived IGF-I — using conditional knockout mouse models to show both hormones have substantial actions independent of each other. This is the conceptual framework underlying nearly every other GH-IGF-1-axis finding already in this domain (local GH action at the growth plate, GH's direct effects on hematopoiesis and MSC commitment, etc.).

> [!key-insight] This review is the theoretical foundation this domain's entire "local, not just systemic, GH/IGF-I action" thread ultimately rests on — from [[IGF1-GH-Differential-Effects-Growth-Plate-Hunziker|Hunziker et al. 1994]] through [[Human-Growth-Plate-Transcriptional-Atlas-GH-Stem-Cells|Chu et al. 2025]], every paper finding GH or IGF-I acting somewhere other than through the classical liver-IGF-I-endocrine pathway is a specific instance of the general principle this 2001 review established.
