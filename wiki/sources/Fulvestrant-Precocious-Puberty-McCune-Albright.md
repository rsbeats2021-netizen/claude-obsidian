# Fulvestrant for Precocious Puberty in McCune-Albright Syndrome (Sims et al. 2012)

McCune-Albright syndrome (MAS) — caused by a post-zygotic gain-of-function mutation in Gsα — produces gonadotropin-independent precocious puberty via autonomous ovarian estrogen production, alongside fibrous bone dysplasia and café-au-lait spots. This trial tests fulvestrant, a "pure" estrogen receptor antagonist that binds ER and triggers its degradation (unlike tamoxifen, a selective ER modulator with partial agonist activity), in 30 girls with progressive precocious puberty from MAS — the first reported pediatric use of this drug, previously used exclusively for breast cancer.

Over 12 months of monthly intramuscular injections, median vaginal bleeding fell from 12 to 1 day/year, and the rate of bone-age advancement (a proxy for how fast the growth plates are maturing/closing) dropped from 1.99 to 1.06, progressively over the treatment period. Because fulvestrant is a pure antagonist rather than a partial agonist like tamoxifen, no significant change in uterine or ovarian volume was observed — addressing a specific safety concern raised by prior tamoxifen trials in this population (progressive uterine enlargement, with theoretical links to endometrial tumor risk in adult tamoxifen users).

Despite these gains, growth velocity and predicted adult height did not improve significantly, and only 35% of patients achieved complete bleeding cessation — the authors characterize the drug as "moderately effective" rather than a definitive solution, calling for longer follow-up.

> [!gap] Industry-sponsored trial (AstraZeneca) with several authors reporting financial conflicts of interest (employment or consulting relationships with the sponsor); no formal power calculation was feasible given MAS's rarity, and each girl served as her own historical control rather than being randomized against a placebo group.
