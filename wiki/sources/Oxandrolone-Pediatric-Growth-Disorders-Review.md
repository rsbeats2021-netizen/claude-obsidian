# Oxandrolone in Pediatric Growth Disorders: Benefits, Risks (Soliman et al. 2025)

A 2025 narrative review surveying oxandrolone use across three distinct pediatric contexts — constitutional delay of growth and puberty, Turner syndrome, and burn-injury metabolic recovery — reporting the same core tradeoff already established in this domain's clinical literature: real short-term growth acceleration (4.5-9.6 cm/yr) and modest additional final-height gain when combined with GH, set against dose-dependent virilization and skeletal-maturation-acceleration risk concentrated specifically in Turner syndrome patients at doses above 0.06 mg/kg/day.

> [!gap] **Confidence flagged low**: this review was published in *World Journal of Advanced Research and Reviews*, a journal with characteristics of a pay-to-publish/lower-editorial-rigor outlet, and its reference list shows patterns consistent with templated or low-scrutiny citation generation (suspiciously round, evenly-spaced numeric findings across studies; generic author-name patterns; a citation format that doesn't fully match standard PubMed-indexed conventions for several entries). The specific numeric findings reported here (growth-velocity ranges, adverse-effect percentages) should be treated as a rough synthesis of the pediatric-oxandrolone literature's general shape rather than as precisely verified figures — the general direction (real short-term benefit, dose-dependent androgenic risk concentrated in Turner syndrome) is consistent with what's independently established elsewhere in this domain (e.g., [[Growth-Promotion-Short-Normal-Child-Thesis|Stirling 1994]]'s controlled oxandrolone trial), but the specific percentages here are not independently verified against primary sources by this ingest.
