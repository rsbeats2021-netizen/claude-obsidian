# Trenbolone Supplementation and Extremity Bones in Running Rats (Sarı & Lök 2019)

A small (n=7/group) 4-week study in young male Wistar rats testing intraperitoneal trenbolone enanthate (a potent, non-testosterone anabolic-androgenic steroid used illicitly in both human doping and livestock production), with or without treadmill exercise, on femur and humerus dimensions.

Femur and humerus length were numerically shorter in the trenbolone group than in exercise, trenbolone+exercise, or control groups, but neither difference reached statistical significance (femur: p=0.637; humerus: p=0.355). No group differences were found in corpus thickness, cortex thickness, or medullary diameter for either bone. Despite the non-significant result, the authors conclude trenbolone "may lead to early epiphyseal closure," leaning on cited prior literature (including the same group's earlier testosterone enanthate study reaching a similar conclusion) rather than on this study's own statistics.

> [!contradiction] The paper's own conclusion outruns its data: with p-values of 0.637 and 0.355, this study did not find a statistically significant effect of trenbolone on bone length, yet the abstract and conclusion assert trenbolone causes early epiphyseal closure. This should be read as a small, likely underpowered study whose numerical trend is consistent with (but does not itself establish) the anabolic-steroid/early-closure hypothesis found more robustly elsewhere in the literature it cites (e.g., McCoy et al. 2012, cited within, found trenbolone actually *preserves* bone mineral density in orchiectomized rats — a different, not clearly reconciled, finding).

> [!gap] Very small sample size (n=7/group), single anatomical measurement method (caliper), short duration (4 weeks), and a conclusion not statistically supported by the study's own results.
