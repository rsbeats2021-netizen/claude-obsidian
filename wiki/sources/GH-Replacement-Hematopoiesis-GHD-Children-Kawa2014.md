# GH Replacement Therapy Directly Stimulates Hematopoiesis in GHD Children (Kawa et al. 2014)

Extends the GH-IGF-1 axis to a tissue context this domain hadn't yet covered in depth: bone marrow hematopoiesis. GH-deficient children showed reduced red-blood-cell parameters and diminished erythroid progenitor clonogenicity; GH replacement therapy reversed this, directly up-regulating cell-cycle genes and IGF-1 expression in isolated CD34+ hematopoietic progenitor cells.

> [!key-insight] This adds a third distinct GH/IGF-1-responsive stem/progenitor cell population to this domain, alongside growth-plate resting-zone epSSCs ([[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]]) and circulating VSELs ([[GH-Therapy-Circulating-Stem-Cells-VSELs-Wedrychowicz|Wędrychowicz et al. 2025]]) — GH/IGF-1 signaling appears to broadly support stem/progenitor cell proliferation across multiple, tissue-distinct compartments (cartilage, circulating blood, bone marrow), not just the growth plate specifically.

> [!gap] Human pediatric cohort (60 controls, 40 GHD children); the mechanistic link between GH/IGF-1 signaling and erythroid progenitor proliferation specifically (versus other hematopoietic lineages) isn't fully resolved in the extracted abstract.

## Relevance to This Domain

A third tissue-distinct stem/progenitor-cell target of GH/IGF-1 signaling for [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]], alongside growth-plate epSSCs and circulating VSELs — reinforcing GH/IGF-1 as a broadly pro-proliferative systemic signal across multiple stem-cell compartments.
