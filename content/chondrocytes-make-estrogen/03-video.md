# Video script — "Your growth plate makes its own estrogen"

60s · 176 words · ~178 wpm. **Only `SPOKEN:` lines are read aloud.**

### [0:00–0:07] HOOK
SPOKEN: Your growth plate doesn't just receive hormones from your bloodstream.
        It manufactures them on site. And vitamin D is one of the things
        controlling that.
  [VISUAL: cartilage with a small factory icon]

### [0:07–0:16] PROBLEM
SPOKEN: Most people picture hormones as delivered goods — made in a gland,
        shipped through blood, received by tissue waiting for instructions.
        Cartilage doesn't work like that.
  [ON-SCREEN: "NOT A PASSIVE TARGET"]

### [0:16–0:36] EVIDENCE
SPOKEN: Growth plate chondrocytes produce 17-beta-estradiol directly. That was
        measured in isolated cells, and the production is regulated by active
        vitamin D — putting a nutrient upstream of local estrogen signalling in
        cartilage. And it's not just estrogen: cartilage also converts
        testosterone into DHT locally, with DHT the dominant metabolite that
        accumulates there.
  [ON-SCREEN: "LOCAL PRODUCTION + LOCAL CONVERSION"]

### [0:36–0:50] PAYOFF
SPOKEN: Which reframes the whole question. Blood hormone levels often correlate
        poorly with what a tissue is actually experiencing. A serum number is
        not a growth plate measurement, and the interesting chemistry here is
        local.
  [ON-SCREEN: "SERUM ≠ TISSUE"]

### [0:50–0:60] CAVEAT + CTA
SPOKEN: Now the limits, because this one gets misused. It's rat cell culture.
        It shows the machinery exists and responds to vitamin D. It does not
        tell you the direction of any height effect — and remember estradiol is
        also what closes plates. Don't forget, it's never over.
  [ON-SCREEN: "MECHANISM YES. DIRECTION NO."]
