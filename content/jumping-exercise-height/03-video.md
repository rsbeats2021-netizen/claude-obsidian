Jumping. And it was tested in short-stature children, not gym influencers.

[cover card]

Everything gets framed hormonally, as if the only inputs are molecules you swallow or inject. Bone is a mechanically responsive tissue and that gets left out.

[mechanical loading]

A controlled intervention in short-stature children, measuring serum growth hormone, IGF-1, IGFBP-3, and the IGF-1 to IGFBP-3 ratio before and after.

[used jumping exercises]

IGFBP-3 binds IGF-1 in circulation. The ratio between them approximates how much IGF-1 is actually free to act, rather than how much simply exists.

[is the interesting part]

Bone and cartilage cells respond directly to mechanical strain. Impact loading is a recognised anabolic stimulus for bone independent of any hormone you add.

[mechanistically]

Non-randomised, controlled but not blinded, and the endpoints are blood markers — not adult height. Nobody followed these children to final stature.

[are important]

It's free, it has no adverse-effect profile, and the mechanism is real. Very little else in this topic can claim all three at once.

[takeaway card]

The floor for this intervention is 'harmless'.
