# Testosterone Dose-Response for Fat-Free Mass (Bhasin et al. 2001)

The reference dose-response trial. It does not measure energy expenditure, but because the androgen effect on TDEE runs through lean mass, this is the input to the arithmetic.

## Numbers

| Weekly dose | Nadir total T | Fat-free mass change (20 wk) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 mg | 253 ng/dL | — |
| 50 mg | 306 ng/dL | — |
| 125 mg | 542 ng/dL | **+3.4 kg** |
| 300 mg | 1,345 ng/dL | **+5.2 kg** |
| 600 mg | 2,370 ng/dL | **+7.9 kg** |

Dose dependence P = 0.0001; correlation with log testosterone r = 0.73. **Energy and protein intake were standardised**, and subjects were not resistance training — so this is the pharmacological effect in isolation.

## Why the design detail matters

Endogenous production was clamped off with a GnRH agonist, so the dose groups are genuinely separated in exposure and the low-dose arms are mildly hypogonadal rather than eugonadal-plus-drug. That makes the dose-response curve clean but also means the 25 and 50 mg arms are not "placebo."

## Using this for TDEE

Skeletal muscle tissue costs roughly **13 kcal/kg/day** at rest; whole fat-free mass averages nearer **20-22 kcal/kg/day** because it includes organs. FFM gained on testosterone is muscle plus intracellular water and glycogen, which are metabolically near-inert, so the muscle coefficient is the defensible one:

- 125 mg/week → +3.4 kg FFM → **≈ +45 kcal/day**
- 300 mg/week → +5.2 kg FFM → **≈ +68 kcal/day**
- 600 mg/week → +7.9 kg FFM → **≈ +103 kcal/day**

> [!gap] This arithmetic is a derivation, not a measurement. It assumes all FFM gained is muscle at 13 kcal/kg/day and ignores any direct hypermetabolic effect. It lands lower than the measured +112 kcal/d in [[Bauman-2011-Testosterone-SCI-Resting-Energy-Expenditure]] and the +7% in [[Welle-1992-Testosterone-BMR-Lean-Mass]], which is the honest uncertainty band.
