# Research brief — The accidental overgrowth

## Core claim / hook angle
A child given a pan-FGFR inhibitor for an unrelated tumour developed skeletal
overgrowth — an accident that validated the mechanism behind an entire class of
achondroplasia drugs, from the opposite direction.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Achondroplasia is caused by gain-of-function FGFR3 mutations that
  constitutively suppress chondrocyte proliferation. Excess FGFR3 signal =
  suppressed growth.
- **Tier 2** — 2024 case report: a child on a *pan*-FGFR inhibitor (for a
  tumour) developed skeletal overgrowth. The mirror-image phenotype, in a human,
  observed as an adverse effect rather than produced by a trial.
- **Tier 1** — TYRA-300: purpose-built FGFR3-selective TKI, dual indication
  (achondroplasia + FGFR3-mutant urothelial cancer).
- **Tier 1** — Infigratinib (BGJ398): FGFR1-3-selective, 2024 NEJM trial, with
  the Fgfr3^Y367C/+ mouse model preclinical arc behind it.
- **Tier 2** — Meclizine: decades-old OTC antihistamine found to inhibit the
  same pathway; Phase 2 in Japan, co-administered with GH.
- **Tier 2** — Snail1 is the transcription factor executing FGFR3's
  growth-suppressing signal (de Frutos 2007) — candidate pharmacodynamic readout
  for all three drugs.

## Why this angle works
The case report is a natural experiment: the mechanism confirmed by accident, in
a human, in the opposite direction from the therapy. That's a stronger and more
memorable proof than any trial result, and it makes the drug-strategy comparison
land instead of reading as a pipeline review.

## Required framing — slide 8
These are therapies for a specific genetic skeletal dysplasia, tested in people
who have it. The deck must not read as a height-enhancement pathway for people
without the mutation. Correcting a broken receptor ≠ improving a working one.
