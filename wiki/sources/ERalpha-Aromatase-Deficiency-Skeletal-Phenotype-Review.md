# Estrogen Action on the Skeleton: Rodent and Rare Human Disorder Review

A comprehensive review synthesizing the detailed skeletal phenotype of human ERα disruption and aromatase deficiency: delayed epiphyseal maturation, tall stature, trabecular thinning, marked cortical thinning, genu valgum, and reduced cortical volumetric BMD — but, notably, preserved trabecular number and normal-to-increased periosteal expansion, showing the estrogen-deficient skeletal phenotype is not simply "less bone everywhere" but a specific pattern of cortical-vs-trabecular divergence. Also notes that estrogen replacement, even started late in adulthood, still produces meaningful skeletal benefit in these rare individuals — relevant context for [[Aromatase-Deficient-Man-Estrogen-Therapy-Bilezikian1998|Bilezikian et al. 1998]]'s original clinical case already in this domain.
