# Research brief — Mechanical loading and bone

## Core claim
Bone and cartilage sense mechanical force through named receptors and convert it
into gene expression. The response is dose dependent: anabolic in a range,
catabolic above it.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Chondrocyte mechanotransduction runs through a three-tiered
  cascade: ion channels, intracellular signalling, gene expression.
- **Tier 1** — ERα acts as a **ligand-independent** mechanotransducer in
  articular chondrocytes, separate from its estrogen-binding role.
- **Tier 1 — the dose response, and the important part** — Damaging/excessive
  loading inhibits ERα expression, downregulating COL2 and SOX9 and
  upregulating MMP13 and ADAMTS4 (cartilage-degrading). Same tissue, same
  receptor, opposite direction depending on magnitude.
- **Tier 2** — Mandibular condylar cartilage growth responds to functional and
  mechanical loading via cAMP and calcium second messengers (Kantomaa).
- **Tier 2** — P2Y2 inhibition increased periosteal bone formation in response
  to exercise loading in **adult** mice. Adult bone still responds to load.

## Why this deck exists
The `aas-jaw-growth` deck was misread by viewers as denying that interventions
change craniofacial structure. Part of that is a copy problem, now fixed. The
other part is that the channel had no deck explaining the loading mechanism at
all, so there was nothing for the jaw content to sit on top of.

This deck is that foundation. It establishes that force is a real signalling
input with named receptors, and that magnitude decides the direction.

## The distinction that carries the deck
Not "does loading work" (it does, clearly) but "where on the curve". Slide 5 and
slide 6 are the payload. Both ends of the dose response are evidenced, and a
deck that only shows the anabolic half would be as incomplete as one that only
shows the catabolic half.

## What is NOT claimed here
No specific loading protocol, magnitude or frequency. The literature in this
brief establishes the mechanism and the existence of a dose response. It does
not define where the threshold sits in a human jaw, and this deck does not
pretend otherwise.
