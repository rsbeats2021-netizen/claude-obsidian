# ERα and AF-1 for Growth Plate Closure in Female Mice (Börjesson et al. 2012)

This paper resolves a specific mechanistic question left open elsewhere in this domain: growth-plate closure requires functional estrogen receptor alpha (ERα), but which of ERα's two transcription-activation domains (AF-1 or AF-2) actually does the work?

Using two mouse models — complete ERα knockout (ERα-/-) and a model with only the AF-1 domain deleted (ERαAF-1⁰, which still expresses a truncated, AF-1-lacking ERα protein) — the authors found opposite growth-plate phenotypes. Old female ERα-/- mice had prolonged, enhanced longitudinal bone growth and wider growth plates, closely resembling the phenotype seen in aromatase-deficient and estrogen-resistant humans (correcting an earlier, less complete ERα-knockout mouse model that had shown conflicting results). In contrast, old female ERαAF-1⁰ mice showed accelerated growth-plate closure, with growth plates fully fused — driven by a measurably altered balance of chondrocyte proliferation (decreased) and apoptosis (increased) in the growth plate.

The authors' interpretation: growth-plate closure is induced by ERα functions that do not require AF-1, and AF-1 normally has a repressive/opposing effect on the closure (senescence) pathway — when AF-1 is missing, ERα (acting via AF-2) becomes effectively hyperactive at driving fusion.

> [!gap] This is a female mouse study; growth-plate physiology genuinely differs between mice and humans (mouse growth plates don't fuse directly after sexual maturation the way human ones do), a caveat the authors themselves repeatedly emphasize.

## Relevance to This Domain

The clearest direct mechanistic link in this domain between an estrogen receptor's specific molecular domain and the chondrocyte proliferation/apoptosis balance described generally in [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]] — this paper supplies actual experimental support for the "apoptosis" theory of growth-plate closure (in this specific ERαAF-1-deficient genetic model), where [[Pubertal-Growth-and-Epiphyseal-Fusion|Shim 2015]] noted classical apoptosis had been directly contradicted in unmodified human growth plates.
