The evidence base. And only one of them measured whether anyone actually ended up taller.

[cover card]

As the established way to delay fusion and gain height. The confidence in how people talk about them is not matched by the literature behind them.

[get discussed confidently]

207 participants total. 84 actually on the active drug.

[randomised trials]

Predicted adult height went up in every trial. Which sounds like the answer until you notice what predicted adult height actually is.

[improved the prediction]

One study followed participants to actual near-final adult height — and it did so under non-randomised extension conditions, not as a controlled comparison.

[reported real height]

No meaningful difference in final adult height. The one time anyone measured the outcome that matters, the effect didn't appear.

[showed no difference]

Mechanistically well grounded, clinically used, and the randomised evidence for adult height gain is thin and does not yet demonstrate the benefit.

[takeaway card]

Plausible and unproven are allowed to coexist.
