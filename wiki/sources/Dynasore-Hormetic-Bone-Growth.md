# Dynasore Modulates Bone Growth in a Hormetic Manner (Marchan-Alvarez et al. 2026)

Dynamin is a GTPase required for membrane scission processes across nearly all mammalian cells — clathrin/caveolin-mediated endocytosis, Golgi vesicle trafficking, and mitochondrial fission — but its specific role in growth-plate chondrocytes had not previously been examined. This paper (from Phillip Newton's Karolinska Institutet group, a co-author on the epSSC niche discovery cited elsewhere in this domain) tests dynasore, a reversible small-molecule dynamin inhibitor, on ex vivo cultured mouse metatarsal bones.

The central and somewhat counterintuitive finding: dynasore's effect on bone growth is hormetic (biphasic by dose) — low concentration (40 μM) *increased* longitudinal bone elongation, while high concentration (220 μM) impaired growth and eliminated chondrocyte proliferation entirely. More surprisingly, the low-dose growth-promoting effect occurred *despite* — not because of — reduced chondrocyte proliferation; instead, growth was driven by increased epiphyseal cartilage matrix accumulation in the resting and proliferative zones, preserved collagen type X (hypertrophy marker) synthesis, activated mTORC1 signaling, and blocked autophagy (evidenced by SQSTM1/p62 protein accumulation, a standard autophagy-flux marker).

> [!key-insight] This result complicates the field's usual assumption that "more chondrocyte proliferation = more bone growth" (the implicit model underlying most of this domain's other growth-plate papers, e.g., [[Skeletal-Stem-Cells-Growth-Plate-Hedgehog-Signaling|Trompet et al. 2024]] and [[GPER1-Chondrocyte-Proliferation-PTHrP-Ihh|Chou et al. 2025]], both of which link growth directly to proliferation rate). Here, low-dose dynasore achieved growth via matrix accumulation and altered remodeling dynamics *instead of* proliferation — demonstrating these are separable levers, not a single unified mechanism.

> [!gap] A bioRxiv preprint; ex vivo organ-culture system only (mouse metatarsals), and the specific dynamin-dependent vs. dynamin-independent contributions to the hormetic effect (dynasore has known off-target effects on cholesterol homeostasis and actin remodeling) are not fully disentangled.

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a genuinely novel mechanistic wrinkle to [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]] and the epSSC/Hedgehog research thread — a case where bone elongation and chondrocyte proliferation rate move in *opposite* directions, mediated by matrix accumulation, mTORC1 activation, and autophagy suppression rather than cell-cycle effects.
