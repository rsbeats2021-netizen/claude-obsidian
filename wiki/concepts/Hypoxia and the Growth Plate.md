# Hypoxia and the growth plate

> The growth plate has no blood supply. Its cells sit further from a vessel than almost any others in the body, and it runs a dedicated programme to survive that.

## Why it is hypoxic
The plate is **avascular** by construction. Vessels reach the metaphysis and stop; everything beyond travels by diffusion. Cells at the centre are the furthest from supply and the first to run short. This is a geometric fact about the tissue, **not** a function of arterial oxygen content — the distinction the niche consistently loses. See [[plate-hypoxia-implies-intervention]].

## How the tissue copes

**1. The HIF response is mandatory.**
Conditional HIF-1α deletion causes massive cell death in the plate interior. The death is central rather than uniform, which is the fingerprint of a diffusion limit. *(tier C — [[schipani-2001-genes-dev--hif1a-chondrocyte-survival]])*

**2. Chondrocytes make their own haemoglobin.**
They express haemoglobin at high levels and condense it by phase separation into membraneless cytoplasmic bodies (**Hedy**) that act as a local oxygen reservoir. Driven by **KLF1** via KDM5A inactivation — independent of the HIF pathway, which makes it a second, parallel system. *(tier C — [[10.1038-s41586-023-06611-6--haemoglobin-body-chondrocyte]])*

**3. The storage profile is measurable.**
P50 of **27.85 mmHg** for chondrocyte haemoglobin against **58.2 mmHg** for red blood cells. A left-shifted dissociation curve means it grabs oxygen and holds it: a depot, not a delivery vehicle. *(tier C)*

**4. Removing it is lethal.**
Chondrocyte-specific haemoglobin deletion abolishes Hedy, kills the inner zones, delays hypertrophy; homozygotes die at E16.5–E18.5. *(tier C)*

## The delivery consequence
Because the tissue is diffusion-limited, a serum concentration is a poor proxy for what arrives at the plate. This is why an IGF-1 reading describes hepatic output rather than local exposure, and why adding compounds does not fix a transport problem. Links to [[GH-IGF-1 Axis]].
