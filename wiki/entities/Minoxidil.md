# Minoxidil

> A prodrug for hair growth whose accepted mechanism has never been cleanly demonstrated in the tissue it acts on.

## Identity and class
- **Class:** K_ATP channel opener; originally an oral antihypertensive
- **Active species:** minoxidil **sulfate**, not minoxidil itself
- **Route:** topical solution/foam, or oral at low dose
- **Status:** topical OTC in most markets; oral is off-label for hair

## Mechanism
1. **Activation.** SULT1A1 sulfotransferase converts minoxidil → minoxidil sulfate. Follicular SULT1A1 activity varies between people, which is the leading candidate explanation for non-responders. *(tier C)*
2. **Proposed action.** Minoxidil sulfate opens K_ATP channels, hyperpolarising the cell and reducing intracellular free calcium. *(tier D — see caveat)*
3. **Caveat that matters.** Messenger & Rundegren: no clear demonstration that K_ATP channels are expressed in the hair follicle at all. The mechanism is inferred from vascular tissue. *(tier D)*
4. **Other in-vitro effects.** Proliferation ↑, collagen synthesis ↓, VEGF ↑, prostaglandin synthesis ↑. *(tier C)*

## What it does in humans
| Outcome | Direction | Tier | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hair count, vertex | increase | A | consistent across trials |
| Shaft diameter | increase | B | part of the visible effect |
| Maintenance vs placebo | large | A | most of the benefit is loss avoided |
| Regrowth of long-miniaturised follicles | limited | B | consistent with [[bald-scalp-retains-stem-cells]] |

## Kinetics
Topical: effect requires continued use; discontinuation returns to the untreated trajectory within months. This is the single most under-stated fact about it.

## Adverse effects
**Reversible:** scalp irritation, initial shedding, unwanted facial hair growth.
**Needs care:** oral use is a systemic vasodilator — tachycardia, fluid retention, and a cardiovascular profile that is not a cosmetic-grade risk.
