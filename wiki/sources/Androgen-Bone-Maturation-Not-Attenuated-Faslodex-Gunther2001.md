# Androgen-Accelerated Bone Maturation Doesn't Require Estrogen Receptor Signaling (Gunther et al. 2001)

A direct pharmacological test of this domain's central androgen-vs-estrogen controversy: testosterone-accelerated bone maturation in mice was unaffected by co-treatment with Faslodex (fulvestrant), a pure ER blocker/degrader that eliminates all estrogen receptor signaling regardless of aromatization. If androgen's bone effect worked purely by conversion to estrogen, blocking the estrogen receptor should have blocked the effect — it didn't.

> [!contradiction] This is a genuinely direct counter-data-point to the "it's really estrogen" camp already documented in [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]] (Shim 2015, Bouliari et al. 2026, Swerdloff et al. 2017) — unlike those clinical/pharmacological sources which infer aromatization-dependence indirectly (via aromatase inhibitor or 5α-reductase inhibitor effects), this paper directly blocks the estrogen receptor itself and still sees the androgen effect, about as clean a test as this domain has for whether ER signaling is *necessary* for androgen-driven bone maturation.

> [!gap] Mouse model measuring bone maturation (likely radiographic/histological) specifically; whether this generalizes to the growth-plate-closure endpoint specifically studied by this domain's other AI/SERM sources isn't established, since "accelerated bone maturation" and "growth-plate closure" aren't necessarily identical endpoints.

## Relevance to This Domain

A significant direct-evidence addition to [[Estrogen vs Androgen in Male Bone Health]]'s central controversy — one of the cleanest tests in this domain's corpus of whether androgen's bone effect genuinely requires estrogen receptor signaling, with a result favoring the direct-androgen-action side of the debate.
