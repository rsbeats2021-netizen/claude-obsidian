# Sex Steroid Regulation of Cartilage and Bone Cell Proliferation (Somjen et al. 1991)

Somjen et al. (1991) compared how cultured rat bone cells versus cultured rat cartilage (growth-plate) cells respond proliferatively to estradiol and DHT, and whether that response depends on the sex of the donor animal.

**Bone**: sex-specific — estradiol stimulated proliferation only in cells from female rats; DHT stimulated proliferation only in cells from male rats.

**Cartilage**: not sex-specific — both estradiol and DHT stimulated proliferation in cartilage cells regardless of donor sex.

> [!key-insight] This is a clean tissue-level distinction: bone cells appear to have sex-differentiated steroid sensitivity, while growth-plate cartilage cells respond to both major sex steroids in either sex. This complicates any single unified model of "estrogen dominates in females, androgen dominates in males" — that framing may hold for bone specifically but not for the cartilage that becomes bone.

> [!gap] A 1991 in vitro rat study; the mechanism behind the bone/cartilage divergence (e.g., differential receptor expression) is not established here, and translation to human tissue is not tested.
