Your bone age. NOT SURFACE LEVEL

[cover card]

So when someone tells you your bone age, that number came from an X-ray of your hand and wrist. One picture, one number, and then that number gets used to guess how much growing your legs have left.

[comes from your hand]

Now you might think that is a mouse quirk. So they did it in rats too: 12 weeks in the phalanges, 16 weeks in the metacarpals, and the tibias and femurs still open at 16.

[same order]

The same study measured how fast each bone was actually growing. At one week old, the metacarpal was growing at 49% of the tibial rate, and the number of cell divisions per column in its growth plate was 46% of the tibia's.

[they grow at different speeds]

Kvist 2021 did MRI on 958 people aged 14 to 21, imaging the wrist, the knee and the ankle in the same person. They found that fusion timing varied by site inside one body, and that complete fusion happened about 2 years earlier in girls than boys.

[kvist 2021, acta paediatrica]

Wrist 0.596, upper shin 0.597, ankle 0.598, heel 0.514 in males. Females lower but present, 0.287 to 0.513.

[what predicts fusion]

BMI correlated with growth plate maturity, 0.186 to 0.384, higher in males. In females there was one very low correlation at the wrist, 0.095, and nothing else.

[were tested directly]

Treat it as an estimate from your fastest-ageing bones, not a readout of your legs. Track your pubertal stage, because that tracked fusion at every site the study imaged.

[takeaway card]

The hand finishes first. Always.
