# Soy Isoflavones Are Not Endocrine Disruptors: Technical Review (Messina et al. 2021)

A large, multi-author technical review directly relevant to this domain's phytoestrogen thread ([[Genistein-Estradiol-ATDC5-Chondrogenesis-TGFbeta|Takeda et al. 2025]]'s genistein/chondrogenesis work, [[S-Equol-Estrogen-Receptor-Anti-Androgen|Setchell et al. 2005]]'s S-equol mechanism): weighing the full observational and clinical evidence base (not just the animal/in vitro studies that originally raised concern), the review concludes neither soyfoods nor isoflavones warrant classification as endocrine disruptors. On the specific question of testosterone-feminization concern in men, a few small human trials did find modest testosterone decreases with high soy intake, but the review notes these often lacked adequate controls or transparent isoflavone-content reporting, limiting confidence relative to the broader (reassuring) evidence base.

> [!gap] A 426,000-character technical review, read strategically via its conclusions section given the length; the review's detailed tissue-by-tissue (thyroid, reproductive, breast) risk assessments were not extracted in full — only the overall conclusion and the testosterone-specific findings most relevant to this domain.

## Relevance to This Domain

An important risk-assessment counterweight for [[Estrogen Receptor (ER)]] and this domain's phytoestrogen sources: while [[Genistein-Estradiol-ATDC5-Chondrogenesis-TGFbeta|Takeda et al. 2025]] found genistein does have real, measurable cell-culture effects on chondrogenesis, this review's broader clinical evidence synthesis suggests those effects don't translate into a population-level endocrine-disruption concern at typical dietary exposure levels — an important distinction between "has a measurable in vitro effect" and "is clinically an endocrine disruptor."
