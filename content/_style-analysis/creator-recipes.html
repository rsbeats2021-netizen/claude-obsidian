01

Bone reads the speed of a load, not the size

Three papers on why the same force does different things depending on how fast it arrives.

EPIPHYX

02 · first study

Journal of Bone and Mineral Research

Osteocytes convert mechanical strain into chemical signals. Strain rate, not peak strain, predicted the osteogenic response across loading conditions in the tested model.

So it is the rate of change that the cell detects. A slow heavy load and a fast light load are not the same input, and this is where most training advice goes wrong.

STRAIN RATE

03 · second study

Cells desensitise

The response saturates early. Continuing past the plateau adds load without adding signal, which is why the papers keep recommending rest between bouts rather than more repetitions.

SWIPE

02

The mechanism

Bone has osteocytes, which are cells that convert mechanical stress into chemical signals. When they sense strain they release nitric oxide and PGE2, and those signals recruit the cells that actually build bone. Everyone stops at "load builds bone" and misses that the cell is measuring rate, not weight.

OSTEOCYTES

03

How many reps should you do?

Everyone gets this COMPLETELY WRONG. The classic loading studies compared 40, 120 and 400 cycles. 120 produced almost the same response as 400, so the last 280 did close to nothing. After roughly 12 weeks the tissue adapts, which is why the authors suggested raising the load every 6 weeks instead of adding volume.

120 ≈ 400
