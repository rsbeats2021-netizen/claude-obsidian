# Research brief — The GH literature disagrees with itself

## ⚠️ Two corrections, in order — read this before editing

**Correction 1 (to the original script).** `.raw/style-refs/scripts/Video
examples.txt` video 2 concludes "if you aren't growth hormone deficient you can
not grow taller with growth hormone." Stated that absolutely, it's too strong:
meta-analyses of idiopathic short stature find +0.84 SD vs controls, ~4–6 cm.

**Correction 2 (to correction 1).** That rebuttal was *also* too clean. There is
a real literature showing neutral-to-harmful outcomes in non-GH-deficient boys —
most pointedly *"Unfavorable effects of growth hormone therapy on the final
height of boys with short stature not caused by growth hormone deficiency"*
(J Pediatr 1997): untreated mean final height **162.0 ± 5.4 cm**, GH-treated
**154.2 ± 4.2 cm**. Roughly 8 cm in the wrong direction.

The original script's "5–6 cm decrease" figures were **not fabricated**. They
track a genuine strand of the literature. The deck now presents the split
rather than picking a side.

## Core claim / hook angle
The GH height literature genuinely contradicts itself, and everyone quoting it
shows you only one half.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — ISS meta-analyses: +0.84 SD vs controls; 4–6 cm average adult
  height gain (range 2.3–8.7).
- **Tier 1** — J Pediatr 1997, non-GH-deficient boys: treated 154.2 cm vs
  untreated 162.0 cm. GH did not improve height SDS for bone age prepubertally
  and *reduced* it through puberty.
- **Tier 2 — reconciliation** — Different populations and designs. Pooled
  estimates lean on ISS cohorts, many uncontrolled; unfavourable results come
  from controlled comparisons in non-deficient boys followed through puberty.
- **Tier 1** — Cost ~$35,000 per inch where gains occur.
- **Tier 3** — No trial has tested GH for height in normal-statured people.
  Absence of evidence, stated as such.

## Still unverified
Individual citations from the original script — Bierich 1992 (traced to *Acta
Endocrinologica*, "constitutional delay of growth and adolescence"), Koplowitz
1995, Lach 1994, Kawaii 1997, Bernasconi 1997, Lanes 1998, Witt. Bierich exists;
its specific final-height numbers were not confirmed. **Do not put per-study
doses or outcomes on a slide until each is checked at source.** The current CSV
deliberately cites only the two claims that were verified.

## Why this version is strongest
It's more contrarian than either previous framing — it contradicts both camps —
and it's unfalsifiable in the good sense: presenting a genuine split can't be
"debunked" by someone citing the other half. It also models the behaviour the
channel is built on.
