# Hanson et al. 2020 — DHT-Enanthate Replacement in Castrate Rats

*Biol Sex Differ* 11(1):48. PMID 32843085. PMC7448502. Open access — which is why the formulation is recoverable.

## Formulation — the second of only two published DHT ester concentrations

| | DHT-enanthate | TES-enanthate |
|---|---|---|
| Dose | **1.00 mg/kg** | 1.75 mg/kg |
| Frequency | **2×/week** | 2×/week |
| Route | subcutaneous | subcutaneous |
| Vehicle | **90% olive oil / 10% DMSO** | same |
| Concentration | **5.0 mg/mL** | 5.0 mg/mL |
| Source | Steraloids (Newport, RI) | Steraloids |
| Duration | weeks 10–15 (~5 weeks) | weeks 10–15 |

5.0 mg/mL ester = **3.6 mg/mL DHT-equivalent**. Castrated Sprague-Dawley males, in-house bred, ~14 weeks old at start; body weights 519 ± 13 g (wk 0) → 670 ± 26 g (wk 15) in the DHT arm.

> Internal discrepancy: the Discussion describes the dose as "~1.0 mg/week × 5 weeks," which conflicts with the Methods' 1.00 mg/kg twice weekly. Treat the Methods figure as authoritative.

## Result

Castration drove a progressive rise in systolic BP over 10 weeks (109 ± 3.3 → 143 ± 3.5 mmHg) while intact controls stayed flat. Testosterone replacement returned BP to normal (113 ± 1.3); **DHT-enanthate replacement reduced BP in castrates to a similar extent**. Losartan (250 mg/L drinking water) prevented the hypertension entirely (100 ± 1.5 at wk 10).

Castration raised renal mRNA for renin (+92%), ACE (+58%) and AT1R (+80%); testosterone replacement normalised all three. Since DHT-enanthate reproduced the BP effect and DHT is non-aromatisable, the antihypertensive action is androgen-receptor-mediated rather than estrogen-mediated.
