# Video script — Erdafitinib

PAN-FGFR INHIBITOR · ORAL · ONCOLOGY DRUG, ONE HUMAN CASE

238 words, ~80s at 178 wpm. Target band 45-90s.

Spoken lines only. Visual cue in brackets.

---

Erdafitinib. It grew a child's long bones. It also bent his spine.

[cover card]

A pre-pubescent child with an FGFR1-mutated glioma was treated with erdafitinib, and the tumour responded. While on treatment the patient experienced rapid skeletal and long bone overgrowth.

[the case report]

Read their wording in full: rapid skeletal and long bone overgrowth resulting in kyphoscoliosis. The paper's own framing is that this is the first detailed report of a serious adverse effect.

[names the problem]

Erdafitinib's toxicity profile is largely attributed to FGFR1 inhibition, and FGFR1 is engaged before FGFR3 is fully hit, because the drug is more potent at FGFR1. So you cannot dose it up to properly release the FGFR3 brake without first saturating a receptor that is doing something else.

[the selectivity problem]

Whether an FGFR-pathway drug is actually active on a given person's growth plates can only be confirmed by serial bone-age X-ray. No serum marker reports it.

[the blind spot]

If the problem is FGFR1, the fix is a drug that does not hit FGFR1. TYRA-300 is FGFR3-selective and was tested in three genetic contexts: wild-type mice, an achondroplasia model, and a hypochondroplasia model.

[tyra-300]

TYRA-300's data is mouse. Erdafitinib's bone data is one human child on oncology treatment.

[with its limit]

It demonstrably grows human long bone, which almost nothing else on this list can claim. It demonstrated it by deforming a child's spine, in a paper written to report that as an adverse event.

[takeaway card]

Proof of concept and cautionary tale, in one patient.
