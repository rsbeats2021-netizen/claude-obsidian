# Research brief — The aromatase inhibitor monitoring gap

## Core claim / hook angle
The main drug used to delay growth plate closure has a documented adverse-effect
profile that paediatric height studies don't screen for. Not because it's safe —
because nobody looked.

## Misconception it corrects
That "no reported side effects in this population" means "no side effects." It
means no outcome measures were collected.

## Key points (tier-tagged)
- **Tier 1** — Aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole) reliably delay bone
  maturation and increase adult height in boys (Bouliari 2026).
- **Tier 1** — The same drugs are standard adjuvant therapy in postmenopausal
  breast cancer, often for years, with intensive adverse-effect surveillance.
- **Tier 1** — Documented in that population: arthralgia, myalgia, altered
  retinal function, subtle verbal/visual memory effects. The brain expresses
  aromatase and makes local estrogen; these drugs block that too (Azcoitia 2021).
- **Tier 1 — the gap** — Bouliari's cohort had *no* cognitive or neurological
  outcome measures at all. Median ~25 months of treatment, no endpoints.
- **Tier 2** — One citation states letrozole doesn't impair cognitive
  development in boys. A single line, not a monitoring programme.
- **Tier 2 — inference** — A developing brain may compensate differently than an
  ageing one. Plausible, and untested in this population. Neither source
  directly studies the age-dependent difference; it's cross-literature inference.

## Why this deck matters
It's the most useful thing this channel could say about aromatase inhibitors.
The topic usually gets covered as "the height drug that works" — this covers
what nobody measured, which is more valuable and more honest.

## Deliberately not included
No dosing, no regimen, no sourcing. AIs are prescription drugs with real bone
mineral density consequences in adolescents; the deck describes a literature
gap, not a protocol.
