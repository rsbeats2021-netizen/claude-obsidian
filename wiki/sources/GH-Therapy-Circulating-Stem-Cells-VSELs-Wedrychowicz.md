# GH Therapy and Circulating Stem Cells (VSELs) in Pediatric GH Deficiency (Wędrychowicz et al. 2025)

A distinct stem-cell angle on the GH axis from this domain's usual resting-zone-chondrocyte focus: this paper tracks circulating (blood-borne) very small embryonic/epiblast-like stem cells (VSELs) — not growth-plate-resident cells like the epSSCs covered in [[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]] — in 20 GH-deficient children over up to 8 years of GH therapy. Long-term GH treatment increased CD34+ VSELs alongside parallel rises in hematopoietic stem cells, mesenchymal stromal cells, and endothelial progenitor cells, with no adverse organ-function effects observed despite ongoing controversy (noted by the authors) about GH's relationship to aging and lifespan in animal models.

> [!gap] Small cohort (20 patients, 8 re-examined at 8 years); correlational findings (VSEL-MSC, VSEL-glucose correlations) don't establish whether VSEL mobilization is a cause or downstream marker of GH's other tissue effects. The specific contribution of these circulating VSELs to actual bone/growth-plate growth (as opposed to general tissue regeneration capacity) is not directly tested.

## Relevance to This Domain

A complementary but mechanistically distinct addition to [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]] and [[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]] — this is the domain's first source on GH's effect on a *circulating*, body-wide stem-cell population rather than the growth-plate-resident epSSC population that dominates the rest of the stem-cell cluster. Whether circulating VSELs and resting-zone epSSCs are related populations, or entirely independent GH targets, is not addressed by any source currently in this domain.
