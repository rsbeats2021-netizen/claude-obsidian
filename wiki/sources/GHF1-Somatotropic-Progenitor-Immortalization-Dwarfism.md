# GHF-1-Targeted Somatotropic Progenitor Immortalization Causes Dwarfism (Lew et al. 1993)

A foundational (1993) developmental-biology paper establishing that dwarfism can result not just from insufficient GH signaling at the growth plate (this domain's usual focus) but from a failure further upstream — arrested development of the pituitary cells that are supposed to produce GH in the first place. GHF-1, a POU-homeodomain transcription factor, marks a somatotropic progenitor cell population that exists transiently before differentiating into mature GH- and prolactin-secreting pituitary cells. By targeting SV40 T-antigen expression to this GHF-1-expressing progenitor stage specifically, the authors trapped these cells in perpetual progenitor state — they kept expressing GHF-1 but never turned on GH or PRL transcription — producing dwarf transgenic mice.

> [!gap] A 1993 developmental-genetics paper using an artificial oncogene-targeting approach (SV40 T-antigen) rather than a naturally occurring mutation or disease model; direct clinical relevance to human GH-deficiency etiology is indirect (illustrative of a developmental principle rather than a natural human pathology mechanism).

## Relevance to This Domain

Adds a pituitary-developmental (upstream-of-GH-secretion) mechanism of dwarfism to [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]], distinct from every other growth-plate-level or hormone-signaling-level mechanism already covered in this domain — a reminder that "GH axis" dysfunction can originate anywhere from pituitary cell-fate specification through to growth-plate chondrocyte response.
