Trenbolone got tested. The bones came out shorter. Not longer.

[cover card]

Usually justified by mechanism. Aromatase inhibition, IGF-1 sensitivity, delayed closure.

[in height stacks]

Femur and humerus in young rats after four weeks, across four groups: trenbolone, exercise, trenbolone plus exercise, and control.

[was long bone length]

Trenbolone shortened femur and humerus length, though the difference did not reach statistical significance in this study.

[was numerically shorter]

No significant differences in bone corpus thickness, cortex thickness or medullary diameter between any of the four groups.

[nothing changed]

A study looked for effects on growing bone and found no benefit, with the length trend pointing the wrong way. That's different from saying we don't know.

[precisely]

Receptor level reasoning predicts what could happen. Measuring the bone tells you what did.

[doesn't survive this]

Rat study, four weeks, underpowered for the length difference. It doesn't prove shortening in humans, and nothing in it supports the height claim either.

[takeaway card]

Weak evidence against. No evidence for.
