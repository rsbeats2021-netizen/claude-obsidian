# GH/IGF-1 Signaling and Longevity: An Inverse Relationship Across Species

Introduces a genuinely novel dimension to this domain's GH/IGF-1 axis literature — until now focused entirely on growth/height outcomes — by reviewing evidence that GH/IGF-1 signaling and lifespan are inversely related across species: GH-excess transgenic mice have shortened lifespans, while GH-receptor-null mice (no GH signaling at all) have extended longevity, mirroring similar longevity-signaling pathways identified in C. elegans. This raises an implicit tension worth flagging: therapies that boost GH/IGF-1 signaling for height (GH therapy, GH secretagogues, cartilage-targeted IGF-1) may carry this longevity tradeoff as an unstated cost, though this domain's growth-focused sources don't discuss it.

> [!gap] No source currently in this domain connects this longevity tradeoff back to any of the domain's specific GH-therapy-for-height interventions (rhGH, GHRP-2, ipamorelin, cartilage-targeted IGF-1) — worth flagging as an open question for future sources to address.
