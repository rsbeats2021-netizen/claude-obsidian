# Differential Timing of Growth Plate Senescence Explains Bone-Size Proportions (Dolan et al., PLOS Biology)

A significant modern (PLOS Biology) contribution to this domain's growth-plate senescence literature: rather than treating senescence timing as uniform across the skeleton, this paper shows the same senescent program ([[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms|declining proliferation, hypertrophy, and progressive cell depletion across all growth-plate zones]]) occurs at systematically different times in different bones — earlier in small bones (metacarpals, phalanges), later in large bones (femurs, tibias) — and that this differential timing, not differences in growth rate alone, is what produces the dramatic size disproportions across the skeleton (e.g., 20-fold longer femurs than finger phalanges).

> [!key-insight] This reframes growth-plate senescence from a single "developmental clock" into a bone-specific, differentially-timed program — directly relevant to [[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]]'s finite-proliferative-capacity model, since it implies the resting-zone stem-cell pool's "capacity" (or the rate at which it's depleted) must itself be set differently in different bones, not governed by one body-wide signal.

## Relevance to This Domain

A significant modern extension of [[Epiphyseal Fusion Mechanisms]] and [[Epiphyseal Skeletal Stem Cells and Hedgehog Signaling]] — growth-plate senescence timing is bone-specific, not uniform, and this differential timing is the proposed mechanism behind skeletal size proportions.
