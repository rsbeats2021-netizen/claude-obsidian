# Honey Microbiome

The bacterial and fungal communities naturally present in raw honey, and what drives their diversity across honey types.

---

## Key Findings (Xiong et al. 2023)

- **Bacteria are relatively uniform across honey types** — dominated by *Lactococcus lactis* (a lactic-acid bacterium), plus *Citrobacter*, *Pseudomonas*, *Serratia*, *Cedecea*. 66 bacterial ASVs were shared across all four honey types tested (monofloral, wildflower, manuka, feral), suggesting a conserved "core" honey bacterial microbiome.
- **Fungi are far more diverse and type-specific** — zero fungal ASVs were shared across all four honey types. Dominant genera (*Bettsia*, *Yarrowia*, *Zygosaccharomyces*, *Skoua*, *Metschnikowia*) varied substantially by honey type; feral honey (from wild-swarmed, unmanaged colonies) had a distinctly different fungal profile.
- **Physicochemical drivers**: titratable acidity best predicted bacterial community richness/diversity; water activity and color best predicted fungal community diversity.
- **Pathogen DNA ≠ active disease**: potential bee pathogens (*Aspergillus flavus*, *Ascosphaera* sp.) were detected as DNA in some samples without any corresponding disease outbreak in the source hives — these fungi often persist as dormant spores.

## Relevance to Other Honey Concepts

This is largely an independent research thread from [[Honey Hypoglycemic Mechanism]] and [[Honey and Bone Metabolism]] — it's about what *lives in* honey, not what honey *does* to human metabolism. It connects to [[Honey Composition and Bioactivity]] insofar as microbial activity (e.g., lactic acid bacteria fermenting sugars) could plausibly influence honey's chemical profile over time/storage, though this specific paper didn't test that link directly.

## Evidence Strength

> [!key-insight] This is the first NGS/metabarcoding characterization of manuka and feral honey specifically — prior microbiome studies of honey relied on culture-based methods, which miss unculturable organisms and introduce selection bias.

> [!gap] Single study, 36 samples from one research group (Cornell) — geographic/seasonal generalizability beyond central New York and imported manuka honey is untested here.
