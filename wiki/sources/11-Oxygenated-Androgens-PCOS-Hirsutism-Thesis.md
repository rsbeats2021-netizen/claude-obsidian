# 11-Oxygenated Adrenal Androgens in PCOS and Hirsutism (Varausová 2023)

A Czech bachelor's thesis (Charles University, Prague) literature review on 11-oxygenated androgens — a less-classical class of adrenal-derived androgens (11β-hydroxy-androstenedione and its downstream 11-oxygenated testosterone/DHT derivatives) — and their potential relevance to Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) and idiopathic hirsutism.

The thesis's central point (per its English abstract): DHEA-sulfate, the most abundant adrenal androgen, has low intrinsic androgenic potency but serves as a circulating precursor reservoir; separately, 11β-hydroxy-androstenedione is a precursor specifically to 11-oxygenated derivatives of testosterone and DHT, which have *high* androgenic potency and relatively high circulating levels. The thesis argues these 11-oxygenated androgens deserve routine attention alongside classical testosterone/DHT measurement in PCOS and hirsutism, since they may account for cases where clinical symptom severity doesn't match classical androgen levels.

As an undergraduate thesis (not peer-reviewed original research), it should be treated as a literature-review-level source rather than primary evidence.
