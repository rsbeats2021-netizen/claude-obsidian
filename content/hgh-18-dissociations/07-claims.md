# Claims — hgh-18-dissociations

A synthesis. Every claim is already verified in an earlier slideshow's ledger;
this file points at them rather than restating the quotes.

| # | Finding | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Isaksson 1982: GH into one tibia grew that leg; same bloodstream, same circulating IGF-1 | hgh-04 |
| 2 | Loveridge 1995: GH antiserum narrowed the plate while circulating IGF-1 was unchanged (10.3 vs 10.7 nmol/l) | hgh-04 |
| 3 | Hunziker 1994: GH beat IGF-1 at every stage "even though it induced a lower level of circulating IGF-I" | hgh-04 |
| 4 | Bick 1992: continuous GH gave higher serum IGF-1 and less growth than the same dose pulsed | hgh-03 |
| 5 | Lui 2010: cartilage-targeted IGF-1 grew bone; identical non-targeted IGF-1 did not | hgh-04 |
| 6 | Wang 2025: 24 weeks of jumping raised IGF-1 with serum GH unchanged (p > 0.05) | hgh-10 |
| 7 | Fasting: GH rises and IGF-1 falls (acquired hepatic GH resistance) | hgh-17 |
| 8 | Ly 2020: the same GH dose produced IGF-1 across the whole range, 0.01-0.10 mg/kg/d in both groups | hgh-09 |
| 9 | Olney 2006: an NPR2 (CNP receptor) defect produced low IGF-1 and GH resistance | hgh-15 |

## What the slideshow concludes
That serum IGF-1 is a downstream marker with many inputs, not a readout of growth.
It does NOT claim IGF-1 is unimportant — local IGF-1 is the mediator in most of
these experiments. The distinction is between the local pool and the serum number.

## Scope of the evidence
No protocol, no target, no dose.
