# Membrane Effects of Sex Hormones on Growth Plate Chondrocytes (ElBaradie 2012)

A PhD dissertation from Barbara Boyan and Zvi Schwartz's lab at Georgia Tech, adding yet another distinct estrogen-receptor mechanism to this domain's growing catalog: rapid, non-genomic, membrane-localized signaling. Using isolated rat costochondral resting-zone chondrocytes from both sexes, the thesis found that E2 activates protein kinase C (PKC) within minutes specifically in *female* cells — male cells show no such rapid response — even though both sexes' cells express the same receptors and respond identically to E2's slower genomic apoptosis effects. This rapid response requires both ERα AND ERβ acting together (not either alone), and depends on receptor palmitoylation to reach the plasma membrane at all.

A later chapter extends the same rapid-membrane-response framework to DHT, finding it too produces sex-dependent rapid signaling in growth-plate chondrocytes, separate from its established slower genomic effects (matrix/alkaline-phosphatase stimulation, already covered via [[Testosterone-Metabolism-Rabbit-Cartilage|Takahashi et al. 1984]] and others in this domain).

> [!key-insight] This thesis demonstrates that growth-plate sexual dimorphism in hormone response isn't just a whole-organism-level pattern (different pubertal timing, different final height by sex) — it's built into the intrinsic, cell-autonomous signaling machinery of individual chondrocytes, at the level of which receptor combination reaches the membrane and how fast. This adds real mechanistic depth to [[Chromosomal-Hormonal-Factors-Human-Sexual-Dimorphism|Fukami et al. 2026]]'s broader claim that sexual dimorphism operates through multiple parallel mechanisms beyond simple circulating-hormone-level differences.

> [!gap] Read strategically (front matter, summary, and table of contents) given the thesis's length; the detailed chapter-by-chapter results (PKC kinetics, palmitoylation assay specifics, DHT chapter data) were not extracted in full. Rat costochondral (not appendicular growth-plate) chondrocytes specifically; translation to the long-bone growth plates this domain otherwise focuses on is not directly established.
