# Hedgehog-Activated Resting Zone Chondrocytes Transform into Osteoblasts (Orikasa et al. 2023)

This paper — cited directly by [[Skeletal-Stem-Cells-Growth-Plate-Hedgehog-Signaling|Trompet et al. 2024]] as reference 18 — comes from the same Noriaki Ono lab that first identified the PTHrP+ resting-zone skeletal stem cell population, and asks what happens to these cells' fate specifically (rather than just their proliferation rate) when Hedgehog signaling is genetically activated within them.

Using a PTHrP-CreER; Patched1-floxed; tdTomato lineage-tracing mouse model (genetic rather than pharmacological activation, avoiding the systemic confounds noted in Trompet et al.'s SAG-based experiments), the authors found that Hedgehog-activated PTHrP+ cells form unusually large, clonally-expanded circular populations they term "patched roses," producing abnormally wide chondrocyte columns and growth-plate hyperplasia. Critically, tracing these cells over the long term revealed that many descendants leave the growth plate entirely and migrate into the diaphyseal marrow space, where they transform directly into trabecular bone osteoblasts — a novel osteogenic fate route for resting-zone stem cells distinct from the classical pathway (where hypertrophic chondrocytes at the *bottom* of the growth plate transdifferentiate into osteoblasts).

> [!key-insight] Together with [[Skeletal-Stem-Cells-Growth-Plate-Hedgehog-Signaling|Trompet et al. 2024]] (same broader stem-cell-niche research area, though different specific labs/mechanism of Hedgehog activation — genetic here vs. pharmacological/genetic there), this paper establishes Hedgehog signaling as a lever that can be pulled on two related but distinct axes: increasing epSSC proliferation to drive linear bone growth (Trompet), or redirecting resting-zone cell fate toward direct osteoblast formation (Orikasa) — suggesting Hedgehog's downstream effect on these stem cells may depend on dose, duration, or genetic vs. pharmacological delivery method.

> [!gap] A bioRxiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed at time of ingestion); mouse genetic model only, using irreversible genetic activation rather than a titratable pharmacological approach, limiting translational applicability compared to Trompet et al.'s bead-based local drug delivery system.
