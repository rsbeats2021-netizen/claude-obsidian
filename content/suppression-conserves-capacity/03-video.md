Slowing growth. And researchers deliberately tested whether it generalises.

[cover card]

Hypothyroidism slows chondrocyte proliferation in rats — and it also slows growth plate senescence. The plate ages more slowly while it grows more slowly.

[was about thyroid]

The follow-up asked whether delayed senescence is a general consequence of growth inhibition from any cause, rather than something particular to thyroid hormone.

[whether it generalises]

Growth inhibition from unrelated mechanisms delays senescence the same way. What matters is that proliferation slowed — not what slowed it.

[across causes]

Senescence tracks cumulative proliferation history, not calendar age. That's the assumption underneath treating growth plate exhaustion as the mechanism of fusion.

[the load-bearing assumption]

Suppress growth temporarily and the plate spends its reserve slower. Release the suppression and growth rate exceeds normal for age, because more capacity remains.

[catch-up growth]

Anything accelerating growth velocity also accelerates the spending of a finite reserve. The interventions that preserve height are frequently the ones that slow things down.

[takeaway card]

The counter-intuitive answer is the correct one here.
