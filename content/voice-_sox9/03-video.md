Does bone age tell you. Short answer: no.

[cover card]

In mice, epiphyseal fusion happened at about 3 weeks in the phalanges and 4 weeks in the metacarpals but had not yet occurred at 12 weeks in tibias or femurs. Many of you will think this is a mouse thing, which is wrong because they repeated it in rats: 12 weeks phalanges, 16 weeks metacarpals, tibia and femur still open.

[dolan et al, plos biology]

At one week, metacarpal growth rate was 49% of the tibial rate and proliferation per column was 46%. Every marker of growth plate senescence, which is the plate running out of divisions, was more advanced in the metacarpals and phalanges than in the femur and tibia.

[the part that actually matters]

MRI of the wrist, knee and ankle in 958 people aged 14 to 21. Pubertal stage correlated with fusion at every site, 0.514 to 0.598 in males.

[kvist 2021, 958 people, mri]

In females one very low correlation at the wrist, 0.095, and nothing else. BMI on the other hand did correlate, 0.186 to 0.384.

[the training advice is wrong]

Track pubertal stage, which is what actually correlated. And stop treating training as the thing that closes your plates, because in 958 people it was not.

[takeaway card]

The hand finishes first. Every time.
