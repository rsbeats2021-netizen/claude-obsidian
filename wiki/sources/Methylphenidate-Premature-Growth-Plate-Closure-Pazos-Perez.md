# Methylphenidate Promotes Premature Growth Plate Closure (Pazos-Pérez et al. 2023)

A striking and clinically significant finding entirely outside this domain's usual steroid-hormone framework: methylphenidate — a stimulant medication taken daily by millions of children for ADHD — promotes premature growth-plate closure in vitro. This is the first source in this domain showing a non-hormonal, non-steroidal drug class directly affecting the same growth-plate closure process otherwise driven by estrogen, GH-signaling mutations (SOCS2), or anabolic-androgen-accelerated maturation.

> [!key-insight] Given methylphenidate's status as one of the most widely prescribed pediatric medications globally, an in vitro finding of premature growth-plate closure — if it translates to in vivo/clinical relevance — would represent a genuinely significant, under-recognized side effect entirely outside the hormone-focused frameworks (aromatase inhibitors, anabolic steroids, GH therapy) this domain otherwise considers when thinking about growth-plate closure risk.

> [!gap] In vitro evidence only; whether this effect is clinically significant at typical pediatric ADHD dosing, and the specific molecular mechanism connecting a stimulant medication to growth-plate chondrocyte biology, are not established by the abstract-level detail extracted here.
