# A Transcriptional Atlas of the Pubertal Human Growth Plate (Chu et al. 2025, preprint)

A direct human-tissue follow-up to [[Skeletal-Stem-Cells-Growth-Plate-Hedgehog-Signaling|Trompet et al. 2024]] from the same Chagin research group (four shared co-authors, including Dana Trompet and Andrei Chagin as corresponding author), addressing a question that mouse studies alone cannot answer: do human growth plates contain the same kind of resting-zone stem cells discovered in mice, and does growth hormone act on them directly?

Using rare human pubertal growth-plate tissue (obtained from epiphysiodesis surgery performed to correct idiopathic tall stature — a clinical procedure that deliberately fuses part of the growth plate early, incidentally yielding tissue for research), the authors performed single-cell RNA sequencing on ~23,600 cells from four adolescent donors. Within the chondrocyte population, they identified two distinct resting-zone stem cell subpopulations differing in proliferative activity and molecular signature, with quiescent cells occupying a niche defined by low Wnt and TGFβ signaling — directly paralleling (and confirming in humans for the first time) the mouse stem-cell-niche biology established in prior Chagin-lab work.

The paper's central mechanistic finding: using a newly developed human growth-plate explant culture system, GH directly stimulated tissue growth and stem-cell proliferation — meaning GH's growth-promoting effect is not purely mediated indirectly via liver-derived IGF-I (consistent with prior mouse data showing liver-specific IGF-I knockout only marginally reduces bone length). GH activated JAK/STAT (the canonical GH receptor pathway), ERK, and — notably — TGFβ signaling in an autocrine loop, while simultaneously *inhibiting* AKT signaling, a specific combination of pathway effects not previously mapped in human growth-plate tissue.

> [!gap] A bioRxiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed at time of ingestion. Tissue source (idiopathic-tall-stature surgical patients) is not representative of the general pediatric population, and the explant culture system, while novel, is an ex vivo model that may not fully recapitulate in vivo GH signaling dynamics.

## Relevance to This Domain

Directly extends [[Skeletal-Stem-Cells-Growth-Plate-Hedgehog-Signaling|Trompet et al. 2024]]'s mouse epSSC findings to human tissue for the first time, and adds substantial new mechanistic detail to the [[Growth Hormone-IGF-1 Axis]] vocabulary page — establishing that GH's local, direct-to-growth-plate mechanism (as opposed to purely systemic IGF-I-mediated action) operates through JAK/STAT, autocrine TGFβ, and ERK activation with AKT inhibition, specifically within the stem-cell compartment.
