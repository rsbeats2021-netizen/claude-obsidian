# Testosterone Replacement Raises REE ~112 kcal/day in Hypogonadal SCI Men (Bauman et al. 2011)

Prospective, open-label, controlled trial: 11 hypogonadal and 11 eugonadal men with chronic spinal cord injury; the hypogonadal group received transdermal testosterone 5 or 10 mg/day for 12 months.

## Numbers

| Outcome | Baseline | 12 months |
|---|---|---|
| REE | 1,328 ± 262 kcal/d | **1,440 ± 262 kcal/d** (P < 0.01) |
| % predicted BEE | 73 ± 9% | 79 ± 10% (P < 0.05) |
| Total lean mass | 49.6 ± 7.6 kg | 53.1 ± 6.9 kg (P < 0.0005) |
| Trunk lean | 24.1 kg | 25.8 kg |
| Leg lean | 14.5 kg | 15.8 kg |

## Why this is a weaker reference point than it looks

This is **replacement dosing in a hypogonadal, immobilised population**, open-label, with no placebo arm for the treated group. The REE rise is partly recovery from an abnormally suppressed baseline (73% of predicted). It establishes the direction and rough scale but cannot be extrapolated to supraphysiological dosing in a healthy trained man.

> [!gap] The paper reports REE and lean mass but not REE adjusted for lean mass, the analysis that made [[Welle-1992-Testosterone-BMR-Lean-Mass]] interpretable. The implied ~32 kcal per kg lean added is well above skeletal muscle's accepted ~13 kcal/kg/day, which is unexplained and may be noise in n=11.

## Relevance to This Domain

The only trial that puts an **absolute kcal/day figure** on androgen-driven REE change in men. +112 kcal/day is the number to quote, with the caveat that it comes from replacement in a deficient population.
