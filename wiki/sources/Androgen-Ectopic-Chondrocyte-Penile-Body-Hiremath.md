# Supraphysiological Androgens Cause Ectopic Chondrocyte Formation in Penile Tissue (Hiremath et al.)

A striking demonstration that androgen's chondrogenic (cartilage-inducing) potential isn't limited to skeletal tissue: supraphysiological androgen levels caused chondrocytes to accumulate ectopically in mouse penile body tissue, displacing smooth muscle and impairing both penile structure and fertility. This is androgen-driven chondrogenesis occurring in a tissue where cartilage doesn't normally belong at all.

> [!key-insight] This is a genuinely novel data point for this domain: it shows androgen signaling's capacity to drive chondrocyte differentiation (the same fundamental process [[Sox9]] and [[Molecular Regulators of Growth Plate Chondrocyte Maturation]] cover in the growth plate) is not tissue-restricted — given a strong enough androgen signal, chondrogenic differentiation can be induced in tissue that should remain smooth muscle. A cautionary supraphysiological-dose finding relevant to anabolic-androgen misuse risk.
